# Learning Festivals & Curriculum
_Last built: 2026-11-24. Sources: ~50 docs, 77 blog-post summaries (via the learning-festivals tag page), 2 emails + 1 2027 meeting note, 4 year READMEs. Recent years first; every fact has its year._

**Scope:** finding hosts, scheduling, activities/curriculum, supplies, registration & photo releases, surveys, what kids liked. (Money for supplies is in `03`/`06`; housing at hosts is in `09`; route dates are in `08`.)

## TL;DR for 2027
- **Target ~10 LFs; "a solid number is around 10."** 2026 ran 10 (9 dates + the DC double-site day); 2025 ran 10 (~400 students); 2024 planned 13 venues and ran ~8–9. Over-asking is fine, because several hosts always fall through. (2026, 2025, 2024)
- **Start host outreach at the end of January, right after the rough route exists (IAP).** Re-contact past hosts first; then fill gaps. Both the 2025 and 2026 crash courses say this. (2026, 2025)
- **Must-do repeat hosts:** DC Dept. of Corrections + Youth Services Center / Maya Angelou Academy (Judge Zia Faruqui; kickoff day, ~June 1), Challenger Learning Center in Hazard KY (Tom Cravens/Charles Bush; every year since at least 2016), Fallon Youth Club NV (all meals, lodging), and AccelerateKY in Bowling Green KY (largest crowds: 96 registered in 2026). The 2026 team also says "do more LFs with correctional facilities" and try to add a San Francisco facility. (2026, 2025)
- **Format that works:** 7–8 MIT students split into 4 groups, 4 workshops running at once, 4 sessions of ~1 h, 9:45–3:15, host-provided lunch, K–12. Groups rotate through the stations. In 2025 there were 5 groups with one set of "floaters"; in 2026 there were 4 groups and no floaters, and it still worked. (2026, 2025)
- **Registration:** one Google Form per venue, opened 2–4 weeks ahead and re-shared. Give the host edit access. Include a photo-release acknowledgment and allergy/diet fields. Sign-ups burst right after each share and in the last 2–3 days. Confirm the real headcount with the host a week out (aSTEAM 2026: 35 planned, 15 came). (2026)
- **Check the school calendar and the day of week.** Fallon 2026: school-supply pickup day meant 5 kids came (1 + 4). Fallon 2022: Sunday LF, schools started the next day, low turnout. Avoid Sundays and holidays. (2026, 2022, 2019)
- **Kids' favorites are hands-on things they make or keep:** 3D printing and bottle rockets (2025 survey); Monster Genome and electric motors (2026 blog). Least liked are talk-heavy sessions (machine learning, Caesar cipher). The top complaint is "more time / more activities / didn't get to print." (2025, 2026)
- **Design and dry-run workshops before the trip.** The 2019 team prepped "way too late"; the 2025 team did a real dry run at Carbone Hall, Framingham 18 May; 2026 first ran its workshops at DC on day 1. Finalize LFs by April so supplies can be bought. (2019, 2025, 2026)
- **Correctional facilities have hard rules:** IDs and names early, a materials list approved weeks ahead, no scissors/glass/sharp metal/electronics, nothing can be left behind, precut everything. (2026, 2025, 2024)
- **Budget reality:** 2026 "Workshop Supplies" was budgeted at $1,500 and spent $1,141.70; 2025 planned $562.80 for 11 LFs / 660 kids and said to buy more of the consumables. (2026, 2025)

## Playbook — how to do it

### 1. Pick the number, the people and the roles (Nov–Dec)
1. Assign LF roles at the first meeting. 2027: Gloria and Kevin = "Learning festivals scheduling / general workshops"; recommended 2 people. The role: make the LF development timeline for the semester, check on workshop leads, coordinate with Route/Housing to finalize dates, and talk to hosts. (2027 first meeting, 2026 role descriptions)
2. Everyone teaches. Design workshops in pairs, with a topic that fits each person's major or skills. Plan for workshops that one person can run alone if a teammate is out. (2026, 2022, 2019)
3. Read the master guide (`docs/master-guide/sub-guides/guides/2026_learning-festival-and-workshops-master-guide.md`) and the hosts table below before emailing anyone. (2026)

### 2. Find and book hosts (end of Jan → March)
1. **Wait for the rough route (end Jan/IAP).** Hosts anchor the route; the route fixes the dates. Per the 2018 team: confirm the host's availability for the date before arranging housing around it, and do it in that order. (2026, 2019, 2018)
2. **Re-contact past hosts first** with the "past location" template (see Templates). Past hosts are warm and often host the team overnight. Best-rated across years: Challenger Learning Center KY, Lab: Revolution MO (2016–2019 ratings), Newton Public Library, Fallon Youth Club, AccelerateKY. (2026, 2019, 2017)
3. **Then new hosts:** schools, libraries, churches, Boys & Girls Clubs, camps, youth clubs, correctional facilities. 2026 crash course: "schools, libraries, churches, educational camps". How 2026 fared with new sites: Fluvanna County Library VA (72 registered, Friends of Fluvanna supplied lunch), Lamar Community Building CO, Main Street UMC Tazewell VA (church). (2026)
4. **Correctional facilities:** go through the Judge Faruqui / TEJI network. TEJI (Educational Justice Initiative, contact Lee/Lperlman, "Carole") helped with DC and the 2022 Massachusetts DYS LF. They asked in 2024 for photos and quotes from the 2023 DC jail LF and a Spokes page on their site. Ask TEJI for corrections facilities along the route (a San Francisco one was tried in 2024, 2025 and 2026 and fell through each time). (2024, 2022, 2026)
5. **Use alumni and past-team leads:** Sam Ford (MIT alum) organized Bowling Green in 2022 and lodging for many Kentucky nights; 2024 leads Hermann MO (Keely Pettijohn) and St. Louis Excel Center (Alexander Stoll) asked for STEM programs; 2025 leads Jennings County IN / Purdue (Jeff Walker). None had been set up as of the last notes. (2025, 2024, 2022)
6. **Track everything in one sheet.** The 2026 route workbook's "2026 Learning Festivals" tab is the model: contact, Contacted?, Save the Date, Workshop Topics, Registration Link, Photo Release, Staff Feedback, Space, Student details, Meals. The 2019 coordinator: "some coordinators were bad at email — just call them; the status spreadsheet was crucial." (2026, 2019)
7. **Pitch copy:** one-pager from 2024 (4 one-hour activities, ~15 students each, adaptable elementary→high school, especially impactful for youth correctional facilities) and the 2025 "Summary of Workshops" blurbs are paste-ready. (2024, 2025)
8. **Ask for a short pre-trip call (15–30 min Zoom/phone), ~2 months ahead** — "how can LFs fit your community?" The 2024 J-WEL stakeholder outreach did this with Gunnison and Newton, and learned: Gunnison has many Spanish-speaking families (provide Spanish materials), attendance trickles in (~60 people over 2 h is good), Newton wants ages 10–18 (~90% are 10–13/14) and caps at ~40. (2024)

### 3. Confirm logistics with each host (March → May)
Cover these in one follow-up email (2026 Hazard example) and save the answers in the tracker:
- **Registration graphic + form link** for the host to share; give host edit access to the form. (2026)
- **Workshop descriptions graphic** to advertise; ask what to adapt for their community. (2026)
- **Schedule:** 4 workshops, 4 sessions; **start/end times** (Ely 2026 ran 11–5 and the library wasn't expecting the team until 11:30; confirm it). (2026)
- **Space:** how many rooms, tables, projector/HDMI, outlets (3D printer needs power), outdoor space for rockets, whiteboard. (2026, 2022)
- **Students:** capacity, age range, when registration closes, who to market to. (2026)
- **Food:** can they provide lunch for students and team? Also the other meals and housing if they offer it. 2026 pattern: Fluvanna (Domino's pizza + apples from the Friends group), Tazewell (church corn dogs/chips/fruit), Hazard (lunch provided, dinner by host), Bowling Green and Lamar (pizza), Glenwood (library arranged restaurant catering), Fallon (all meals). (2026)
- **Photo release:** attach one; every parent must sign. In 2026 the registration form carried an acknowledgment (264/264 ticked). (2026)
- **Safety plan** if hazardous materials are used (2022 follow-up email). (2022)
- **Thank-you card** the morning of / after (2024 driver duty). (2024)

### 4. Design and test the workshops (Feb → May)
- **Pick 4–5 workshops** that appeal across ages and are new to most kids or easy to extend. 2019 advice: topics that "appeal to many ages." (2019)
- **Hands-on beats content.** 2022 team's closing reflection: workshops moved "from content-heavy to hands-on and engagement-driven"; 1.5-hour sessions were too short for parallel circuits/Ohm's law, so skip slides and build. Kids love things they can keep. (2022, 2025)
- **Plan for mixed ages on the fly:** open with a question to gauge level (2026 Hazard: "What is a magnet?"), and prepare an advanced version for grades 6–9. (2026, 2025)
- **Build in extensions for fast finishers** (2025 ML) and a "small wins" mindset: Lego motors only worked for a minority in DC 2026. (2025, 2026)
- **Timeline used by 2019** (they said theirs was late): takeaways Apr 22, materials Apr 29, full workshop May 6, handouts May 13, practice May 20. 2016 calendar: curriculum done 5/15, trial-teach 5/29. (2019, 2016)
- **Do a practice LF** before leaving Boston (2019 advice; 2025 did one at Carbone Hall, Framingham; 2022 did a DYS facility in MA; the master guide says "if desired: have a local LF"). Test every setup the night before: 2025's rocket didn't lift off in the dry run, and the food dye was too strong. (2019, 2025, 2022)
- **Revise after the first two LFs.** Workshops often need rework. Do a second pass of slide changes the night before (2022). (2022, 2019)

### 5. Supplies (April → May; ship/resupply on the road)
- Finalize LFs *early* so there is time to buy supplies. Use the ProCard (not the travel card) for LF supplies; no food on it. Receipts within 2 weeks. (2026 master guide)
- **Per-workshop lists** are in the docs linked in Templates. 2026 kit highlights: 9 Invisible Force kits + 2 power strips; liquid starch 2 gal, distilled water 10 gal, 500 pipettes, 12 beakers, 50 safety glasses, 5 boxes gloves; 20 scissors, 1,500 googly eyes, 9 glues; 300 coin batteries, 600 LEDs, 10 scissors, 6 tape rolls. (2026)
- **2026 purchases:** felt/electronics/design $538.26; chemistry $280.72; tilt sensors $68.80 (+$27.52); coin batteries $60.63; copper wire etc. $99.51; beakers+iodine $14.65; CVS chemistry $24.91; copper tape $5.99; T-shirts $252.61 (CustomInk). (2026)
- **2025:** consumables ran short. The doc flags cornstarch, vinegar, baking soda, plastic spoons, fasteners, jewelry cord/pencils as "need more"; 20 two-liter bottles at ~$5 each, ice $55, cooler $39.99. LF supplies were shipped to a campsite via an Amazon box (Bryce Canyon). (2025)
- **Packing:** 2026 LF supplies fit in 2 bins (kitchen 1, bike gear/first aid 1); the team also precut felt/cardboard/paper the night before DC. The van cannot carry five bikes plus LF boxes, so plan both together. (2026)
- **Donate leftovers** to the last host: 2026 Fallon Youth Club. (2026)
- **Corrections facilities:** measure materials per 40-minute session in advance; send the host a list with product links (floppy rulers etc.); nothing is left behind. (2026)

### 6. Registration and day-of
- **Open forms 2–4 weeks before and re-share;** 2026 sign-up data: Fluvanna 71/72 in the first 12 days (5/21–6/4), Bowling Green 23 on day 1 and filled in 10 days (5/7–5/17), Tazewell 17 of 31 in the last 4 days, Lamar only 3 days (7/6–7/8), Glenwood 14 on one day after a push. Lamar's registrations didn't arrive until 2 days before, so ask hosts to push earlier. (2026)
- **Form fields (2026, same for all venues):** student name, grade in fall, allergens, dietary restriction, emergency contact, photo-release acknowledgment, parent email. ~30 of 264 listed allergens (nut, celiac, lactose, food dye). (2026)
- **Don't copy minors' names** into shared docs. Keep aggregates only. (2026)
- **Typical day (2026):** 9–9:45 setup; 9:45 arrive; 10–11, 11–12 sessions; 12–1 lunch; 1–2, 2–3 sessions; 3–3:15 wrap-up; 3:15–4 cleanup. Build a break between sessions (teaching all day is tiring). (2026)
- **Age banding:** sort kids into age groups on arrival and rotate groups. Glenwood 2025: groups 5–7, 8–10, 11–14 over 4 stations. Bands rotate so each station runs 4×/day. (2025)
- **Safety:** goggles; check bottles for cracks before the vinegar/baking-soda demo (2025 Fallon: a cracked bottle sprayed a student); adults do the super-gluing (2024); bottle rockets: warn before launch; check local fire bans for flame-based workshops (2015 Salt Lake). (2025, 2024, 2015)
- **Explain the rocket mechanism to hosts** ahead of time: Tazewell church members thought it was firecracker-powered, and the church asked for no bottle rockets in 2025. (2025)
- **Run a lunch break where kids can play** (gym or outdoors) so the team can eat; add short breaks between sections. (2026, 2025)
- **After:** team tradition of ice cream/DQ after each LF (2026 receipts: Papa Jim's, DQ, Meltdown). Rest and decompress the day after a LF. (2026, 2025)

### 7. Surveys and feedback
- **Student survey (2024 template):** 3 yes/no-scale items (learned something / liked it / interested in learning more on my own), "tell us two things you learned," "what could we do better?", and age box (5–11, 12–14, 15–18, 18+). 2025 used the same items as an online Google Form plus favorite/least-favorite fields. (2024, 2025)
- **Expert advice (Kathy Vandiver, 2024):** ask "2 things you learned"; the key question is whether it increased interest; no gender question needed but ask age; emoji scales; kids rarely suggest improvements (high schoolers do). COUHES (MIT human-subjects review) approval can't be finished in time, so frame it as a normal classroom feedback survey and don't plan on publishing data. (2024)
- **2026:** two Google Forms existed (Staff/Volunteer, Student/Parent) but **no responses are in Drive**; ask the 2026 team (Tatiana) for the linked response sheets. (2026)
- **Host feedback:** the 2024 J-WEL wrap-up planned a survey of past LF hosts with the 2026 team; check whether it was sent. (2024/2026)

## What worked / what didn't

### 2026
- **Worked:** 5 new, hands-on workshops; 4-group rotation without floaters; per-venue Google Forms + graphics; hosts supplying lunch; first LF at DC corrections as the test run; Monster Genome and electric motors favorites at Fallon; kindergarteners did felt-monster genetics, older kids iodine clock; Fluvanna library (Friends group) and Glenwood library (catered lunch) were strong new hosts; Fallon Youth Club (affordable aftercare, all meals, lodging); Hazard high schoolers helped as in 2019/2022. (2026)
- **Didn't:** aSTEAM Village KC drew 15 (usual 60–80 expected); Fallon 5 kids (school-supply pickup); Lamar registrations arrived 2 days before; Ely start time misread; Lego motor builds worked for only a minority at DC; Monster Genome leaves felt scraps everywhere and iodine clock needs one beaker per student (extra cleanup); registration forms for Hazard, KC, Ely and Fallon exist but have no response sheets. Alternates (Lamar library, South Lake Tahoe FIRST, SF CCSF, Newton, Denver B&G, Ferguson) were rejected on timing/availability. (2026)

### 2025
- **Worked:** survey (~80 responses) shows 3D printing and bottle rockets are the favorites; "happiness detector" ML activity worked; Glenwood age-band rotation; aSTEAM as the finale of a multi-week camp let the team go deeper; Denver rocket launch the highlight; Tazewell test-run day described by a girl who was thankful in church next week. (2025)
- **Didn't:** 3D printing for 40+ kids is the hardest to run, because not everyone got a print and prints queued; SD card corruption delayed prints in Ely; bottle rocket for young kids bored grades 6–9; mixed-age rooms are hard; bottle rocket cracked at Fallon; sample comments: ML "a lot of talk and not much activity," Caesar cipher "second half harder," "make it easier" at Fallon, "need a bilingual partner" in Denver. (2025)

### 2024
- **Worked:** pre-trip stakeholder calls (Gunnison, Newton); touring AccelerateKY the day before; Bowling Green got local TV; Strawberry DNA (reagent explanation: dish soap breaks membranes, salt neutralizes DNA charge, alcohol makes it visible), concrete sharks/speakers, physics demos at the start of the day while kids arrive. (2024)
- **Didn't:** super glue got kids' fingers stuck (speaker build); Tinkercad bubble-wand designs floated or had disconnected parts, and prints ran late (set a print-time limit; start prints early); Physics of Sound was not approved for the YSC and the first DC jail LF had no electronics; many tracked venues (Ferguson, Denver B&G, Cedar City, Lamar) never replied. (2024)
- **DC format learned:** 40-minute classes, 80 minutes too long ("students lose interest around minute 40"), "get to the point fast," slideshows are boring; 2023 feedback: yogurt experiment too slow, pressure and mixing liquids good, circuits bad, chromatography good. (2024, 2023)

### 2022
- Hazard high schoolers helped; Bowling Green got TV coverage; Ferguson middle school taught only a fraction of attendees (declined an offer to double); Lamar only 4 students; Denver never replied; Ely cancelled after a covid case; Fallon Sunday LF low turnout. Solar cells need chemistry background (middle schoolers hadn't had it), fingerprints ruin them, raspberries/blackberries/black grape juice worked (~0.45 V in sun). Marker chromatography was the DC favorite. (2022)

### 2019 and earlier
- Middle/early high school is where impact was biggest; continuity matters (Hazard); LFs clustered at the start/end, leaving dry spells in the middle; Kansas/Colorado/Utah small towns fit better than bigger cities; aSTEAM Village (80 kids in a paid camp) was the hardest for classroom control; Boys & Girls Clubs and libraries were easy; "libraries and Boys and Girls Clubs were easiest." (2019)
- 2017: informal venues (maker-spaces, homeschool) = smaller, regular groups; circuit capstone (binary half-adder → 5-bit adder) was a hit. 2014: team-based tinkering and bike-hub teardown with 8-year-olds worked. 2013: let kids move freely between stations; stay several days to build word of mouth. (2017, 2014, 2013)
