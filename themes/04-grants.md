# Grants — J-WEL, LEAP, departments, foundations: deadlines, applications, amounts won
_Last built: 2026-11-24. Sources: ~75 docs, 2 blog posts, 2 emails. Recent years first; every fact has its year._

Scope: competitive/application-based money (MIT grants and funds, foundations, corporate giving programs). MIT department gifts, crowdfunding, raffles and sponsorship are in [03-fundraising](03-fundraising.md) and [05-sponsorship](05-sponsorship.md); budgets in [06-budget-and-finances](06-budget-and-finances.md).

## TL;DR for 2027
- **J-WEL Education Innovation Grant is the single biggest win in Spokes history: $45,330 in 2024** (period Jul 1 2024–Jun 30 2025; about 2/3 of the 2024 money). The master guide says "Apply to J-WEL!!!!!!!!!!!" in March, with the decision at the end of April. But 2025 asked $20,000 and was **denied**, and 2026 never applied. 2027 should apply again (2024, 2025, 2026).
- **Application windows are in winter and early spring, so start now (Nov–Jan).** J-WEL: due 3/15 in 2024, and 2/10 in 2025 after an extension. MindHandHeart: 2/14–2/24 (2025). SHASS Undergraduate Fund: 3/1. PKG: ~3/17–3/19. CSF: opens ~3/31. Kelly-Douglas: ~4/13. MathWorks: closes ~3/25. Baker: rolling 9/1–5/1. ELO: early Oct (2025, 2026).
- **Realistic small MIT grants** (what teams actually won):
  - Kelly-Douglas (SHASS) travel grant: $1,500 (2025, 2023). Needs a sophomore or junior applicant, ideally with a SHASS major or minor.
  - **Social Impact ELO via Edgerton: $5,000** (applied Sept 2025, offered for 2026).
  - Baker Foundation: $1,000–$2,000 (2022, 2023). It needs a campus event, such as the 2022 bike-repair and safety workshop.
  - MIT Coop: $1,500 (2022); the Coop was "not doing anymore" by 2024–25.
  - Texas Instruments: $4,000 (2019).
  - MathWorks: $1,000–$1,500, fairly reliable (2019, 2022, 2024).
  - OME Innovation Fund: $500 (2022, 2023).
- **Most grants say no.** In 2025 Spokes applied to J-WEL ($20k), MindHandHeart ($5k), CSF ($2k), MathWorks ($2k) and Baker, and all said no. Only Kelly-Douglas ($1,500) and ELO ($5,000, for 2026) said yes (2025). Apply to many, reuse one master application, and expect a 20–30% hit rate.
- **A late faculty letter of recommendation lost the SHASS/Kelly-Douglas money in 2023** (about $1,500 at stake). Ask for letters at least 3 weeks before the deadline: J-WEL needs a department-chair letter, and SHASS needs a SHASS faculty letter (2023, 2024, 2025).
- **J-WEL has strings.** You owe a one-page public grant narrative before Jun 5, a final report by Aug 31 of the following year, a mid-year update meeting, a spotlight interview, and photo essays and toolkits. You must also tell J-WEL about any other funding for the same activities (2024).
- **Missed 2026 deadlines to avoid repeating:** Course 7 (Biology) departmental funding (2026), IDEAS, and J-WEL (not contacted in 2026). The 2026 sheet shows most of the grants tab as "Not contacted". Only ELO ($5,000) landed, and a $500 Innovations Fund request and a PKG draft were prepared (2026).
- **New leads for 2027:** MITFCU People Helping People award (suggested by Dooyon Cho, Course 7 development director, Apr 2026); Course 7 early (Nov 2026 handoff); the Edgerton Building Clubs & Teams Showcase on Apr 8 2027 (2026–27).
- **Carry grant money forward deliberately.** Edgerton (Sandi) says teams should leave about $10k in the bank for next year, and Kim Vandiver asked that next year's bikes be written into the J-WEL budget (2024).
- **Reuse boilerplate.** Every year the team rewrote the same mission, need statistics, tax ID and budget tables. Start from the 2025 J-WEL application and ELO application, which hold the most current budget and impact framing (2025).

## Playbook — how to do it
Steps are drawn mainly from the 2026 master guide, the 2025 preemptive grant list and tracker, and the 2024 J-WEL experience.

1. **Pick a grants owner and a backup** (the 2026 master guide says "keep track of grant deadlines and delegate work"). A single applicant can write for one grant at a time. 2025 split them: Ruth had J-WEL, CSF, SHASS and TBP; Charles had MindHandHeart and Baker; Greta (a sophomore/junior) had Kelly-Douglas (2025). 2024's J-WEL write-up was split by section across 8 people, with a context/evidence/need/budget owner for each (2024).
2. **Meet Edgerton first** (Marvelin Higginbottom, Sandi Lipnoski, Chris Mayer, Kim Vandiver). Ask how external money gets deposited (cost object #2530032), whether there are restrictions, and how J-WEL or ELO money must be tracked. Spokes has a separate J-WEL account/cost object, so ask Sandi about leftover balances (2024, 2025, 2026).
3. **Build one master application bank** (mission, 200-word statement, 75-word abstract, need statistics, past work, team, safety, budget table, MIT tax ID/W-9/IRS letter). Copy from the [2025 J-WEL application](../docs/2025/fundraising/2025_j-wel-application.md), the [ELO application](../docs/2025/fundraising/2025_social-impact-elo-application-fall-2025.md) and the 2022 master application (2022, 2025). Standard stats used: rural vs urban bachelor's gap (22% vs 40%, USDA), STEM pay premium (NSF), $2.25 saved per $1 on prison education (Mackinac), correctional education cuts recidivism 43% (US DOJ) (2024, 2025).
4. **Choose the fit angle for each funder:**
   - J-WEL: research and deployable toolkits, surveys, outcomes.
   - Baker: a *new* campus event or workshop. It "prioritizes new ideas" (2023 email); 2022 ran a bike repair and safety workshop.
   - SHASS/Kelly-Douglas: humanities/arts and education framing, individual travel.
   - CSF: local community service (it does not fund travel, and funds a project for at most 3 cycles).
   - MindHandHeart: wellbeing or "play" themes (2025's theme was play; the team admitted a weak fit).
   - TBP, MathWorks, TI: STEM education and sponsor-recognition offers.
5. **Line up letters early.** J-WEL needs a department-chair letter (Prof. Kim Vandiver served as PI/chair support in 2024 and 2025). SHASS needs a SHASS faculty letter (2025 asked Cherie Abbanat). PKG needs a community-partner letter (Edgerton/Marvelin) and an MIT staff recommendation (2026 draft). TBP 2022 used a TEJI co-director letter (Lee Perlman) (2022–2026).
6. **Calendar every deadline** using the table below, then add each application to the grants tab of the tracker (name, link, point person, deadline, asked, outcome, amount, notes). The 2025 tab has 24 rows to start from.
7. **After you win:**
   - J-WEL: send the one-page narrative by the date in the letter (Jun 5 in 2024), join a J-WEL event, hold the mid-year check-in, write the final report (Aug 31 of the following year), and complete the spotlight interview. Report any other funding received for the same activities (2024).
   - Smaller grants: send thank-yous and receipts, and keep a reimbursement paper trail (Baker 2022–23 and Kelly-Douglas 2023 were paid on reimbursement).
   - Write a close-out summary for funders (SAEF gave an extra $400 after a trip summary in 2022).
8. **Don't rely on a single big grant.** Decisions arrive late April while bikes and car must be ordered earlier. 2024 pursued departments, pK-12, TI/MathWorks and crowdfunding in parallel, and 2025 got only $1,500 in grants despite 7 applications (2024, 2025).

## What worked / what didn't

### 2026
- **Worked:** ELO $5,000 (applied Sept 2025). The 2026 sheet records "Yes 5000".
- **Didn't work:** the other grants on the sheet were never contacted, including J-WEL, SHASS, CSF, Baker and Kelly-Douglas. PKG is an individual fellowship (not team); only the abstract was drafted. The Course 7 funding deadline was missed. Dooyon Cho pointed to the MITFCU People Helping People award instead (Apr 2026). Innovations Fund ask was only $500.
- **Lesson:** a team that waits until spring misses fall and winter windows. The people who owned grants had other duties. Assign owners in the first meeting.

### 2025
- **Worked:** Kelly-Douglas $1,500 (Greta applied; one funding-source line per budget item, assigned to lodging). ELO for 2026: $5,000. The preemptive grant list (Jan 30 2025) gave a ready calendar.
- **Didn't work:** J-WEL $20,000 (deadline extended to 2/10, still denied; no reason for the denial is recorded in the sources). MindHandHeart $5,000 ("does not align perfectly"). CSF $2,000. MathWorks $2,000 (gave $1,000–1,500 earlier). Baker (required an event). TBP: "Don't think this fellowship exists anymore". PKG, ICEO and the grad grants did not apply to a team. Sandbox, Legatum and MIT Innovate said no.
- **Lesson:** applying to J-WEL requires a real research angle (toolkits, surveys) and ideally a new pitch; 2025 listed J-WEL themes of sustainability, AI for education and campus pipelines.

### 2024
- **Worked:** J-WEL $45,330 (deadline 3/15, award 4/23), written by splitting sections across 8 members and a PI (Kim Vandiver). Rebecca met J-WEL contacts to build on prior connections; Kathy Vandiver advised on surveys (COUHES can't be done in time; ask "2 things you learned"). Open Learning pK-12 learning grants paid for module development: Sustainable Materials ($4,428; Sophia Wang, JD Hagood) and the Transportation Game ($1,842.99; Jess Xu, Hank Stennes, Cleo), together about $6,293 reported. MathWorks $1,000, Kars4Kids $750, MechE $200, MIT Alumni Club of Colorado $500 (reported to J-WEL).
- **Didn't work:** companies ("lowkey not successful", Apr 10); Garmin, Tern; TEJI "might have funds next year"; PKG no. Edgerton warned the pK-12 money "doesn't smell right": don't spend on things you didn't say you would (Apr 30). Baker was applied 4/10; no outcome recorded.
- **Lesson:** J-WEL "does not need COUHES" but wants deliverables. J-WEL grants carry no overhead.

### 2023
- **Worked:** SHASS $500, Baker $1,000, Coop $1,500 (sheet lists "this year"), OME $500, Kelly-Douglas $1,500 (reimbursements).
- **Didn't work:** Baker staff said they needed an event: "Don't want to make an event, not worth it" (Mar 13 2023 tracker note), though the Sept 2023 Baker email says the foundation funded Spokes in AY2022-23. Late LOR hurt Kelly-Douglas. PKG rejected. Coop ask was $5,000 (got no more than $1,500).

### 2022
- **Worked:** the most diversified grants year: Baker $1,758.52, Coop $1,500, OME $500, TBP $2,000, St. Anthony $4,000, MathWorks $1,000. The team applied to about 20 funders from January.
- **Didn't work:** PKG, Alpkit, Dell, Entergy and New Earth rejected; several outside foundations never replied.

### 2019
- **Worked:** Mad Money $5,000 (a "very easy application, just a statement of purpose"); TBP $3,500; TI $4,000 (short application); MathWorks $1,500; SWE $400.
- **Didn't work:** North Face denied; Boeing/Microsoft invite-only; SpaceX, Shell, Sony, GoPro, Nikon went nowhere. The family-van plan died and the team applied for a STEM Action Grant ($4,000 of $6,000) to cover a rental.

### 2018, 2016, 2014–2013
- **2018:** TBP $5,000 after a full application with resumes and "why I joined" blurbs. Other asks: CRW $4,000, Ford $6,000 (van), Koch $5,000, Dell. TI said no.
- **2016:** LEAP asked $3,500 for teaching materials (20 laptops, rockets, electronics); the 2016 team had $17,820 secured and needed $12,711 more. The handbook says "grants didn't work in 2015; each member personally emailing 10 people worked".
- **2014:** about 20 grants, about $26k total. PSC fellowship and Davis rejected; Harvard SEAS $5,000 and MITFCU were the big wins.
