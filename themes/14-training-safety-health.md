# Training, safety & health
_Last built: 2026-11-24. Sources: ~45 docs, ~110 blog posts (via the by-tag planning notes), 3 emails. Recent years first; every fact has its year._

> Scope note: built mainly from the training docs, the 2017 Hub safety docs, the 2018 Bike & Build notes, the 2026 meeting minutes and crash course, the Travel FAQ, and the per-post planning notes in `blogs/by-tag/health-injury.md` and `safety.md`. There is no formal Spokes safety manual from a recent year; the 2017 Emergency Procedures are still the only written protocol (see Playbook).

## TL;DR for 2027
- **Pick a team safety officer now.** Justin Schmidt (Edgerton Clubs & Teams Liaison) asked on Nov 24 2026 for one person as his safety point of contact. This is the first Edgerton task, due before or at your first meeting (week of Nov 30). The 2026 team wanted this role created (2027, 2026).
- **Write a dog protocol before Kentucky, not during it.** In 2026 a loose dog bit Caroline on Day 13 and she needed six rabies shots plus follow-up doses, and the team spent hours waiting for rescue. Every team since 2017 has been chased in eastern Kentucky (2026, 2025, 2024, 2022, 2017).
- **Write a storm/lightning protocol before departure.** In 2024 the team lost about 30 minutes arguing in a Kansas thunderstorm. In 2026 the rule was that if the van deems a situation dangerous and comes back for you, you HAVE to get in (2026, 2024).
- **Every team has had injuries.** 2024 had a broken elbow that ended a rider's trip and a concussion. 2025 had gravel-descent falls, a collision and an allergic reaction. 2026 had a dog bite, a sprain, an elbow nerve injury and urgent care visits (2026, 2025, 2024). Bring a medkit in the van and on each group, and know your rural hospitals.
- **Training is optional but pays off.** The first 1-2 weeks hurt regardless, and several teams SAG'd heavily early (2026 Day 2: most of the team SAG'd after 88 miles on little sleep). Do spring rides, a few 50+ miles, hills, clip-in practice, and the Midnight Marathon (Apr 19 in 2026) (2026, 2025, 2024).
- **Get a bike fit.** In 2026 six fits with a PT cost $1,150 total, and that PT's rule "sharp, repetitive pain means stop" was used on the road. Saddle sores, knee pain and numb fingers recur every year (2026, 2025).
- **Heat is the main medical risk.** Heat exhaustion appeared in 2026, 2025 and 2024. Carry ice, start at dawn, and put electrolytes in one bottle (2026, 2025, 2024, 2022).
- **Everyone who drives MIT rentals needs an MVR check.** Marvelin requested it for 7 of the 8 members on Nov 24 2026; Liv Girand has no license and is excluded (2027).
- **Never bike onto an interstate, tunnel or no-shoulder highway without checking.** The worst roads were Moab to I-70/Crescent Junction, US-285 out of Denver, KS-68 and US-50 out of Tahoe (2026, 2025, 2024).
- **Fix the chain of command before you leave.** Edgerton pays insurance; do not buy any (2025). For accidents call Edgerton first. See Playbook step 8.

## Playbook — how to do it
### 1. Set up the roles and the safety officer (Nov–Dec)
1. **Name the safety officer** and email Justin Schmidt (justinms@mit.edu) with the name; book the Bookings meeting with him and Marvelin for the week of Nov 30 or later (2027).
2. **Assign a training lead** (2027 draft: Max and Liv). 2026 said the training role is "optional, we didn't do much as a group, but you can make this a thing": the lead sets the plan and holds events (2026).
3. **Collect a medical/emergency sheet** for every member: two emergency contacts each, health card copy, health info, background-check status, first aid certificate (2017 Emergency Procedures). 2026 had a parent contact on file for every rider (2026).
4. **Decide the primary and backup emergency contact** inside the team, plus the MIT contact (Justin/Marvelin) and a fallback (2017).

### 2. Training (Dec → May) — all optional, all recommended
5. **Use the 16-week plan** (base wks 1-4, build 5-9, peak 10-13, taper 14-16): 2-3 indoor rides a week at first, 1 outdoor ride a week from wk 5 (10+ mi), then 3-4 rides with consecutive-day rides in the peak phase. Glossary: endurance 60-80% max HR, tempo 76-90%, recovery under 60% (2025, reused 2026). Doc: `../docs/2025/training/2025_training-plan.md`.
6. **Or use the 2026 Zone 2 planner**: Mon strength, Tue ride, Thu strength, Sat ride, week 5 deload; Zone 2 rides 60-90 min at 145-155 bpm; lower-body focus with RPE 8-10 (2026). Doc: `../docs/2026/training/2026_spokes_workout_planner.md`.
7. **Do at least a few long rides** (50+ miles), ride hills (Boston is flat), practise braking and turning downhill, one-handed riding, and eating and drinking on the bike (2025 team notes from 2024, 2024 advice from 2023). Practise clip-in shoes: "you will fall over if you don't" (2026, 2025).
8. **Group rides**: MIT Cycling Club, Saturday group rides (2025), the Minuteman Bikeway (~30 mi, flat, car-free) (2026, 2025), and the **Midnight Marathon** (team bonding and training; Apr 19 in 2026; fundraiser in 2024) (2026, 2025, 2024).
9. **Do a long day before leaving.** 2018 recommended building to the century point; one 2026 advisor trained to 100 miles in under 10 hours (2026 minutes, via a 2023-era alum). Ishaq's 2025 plan did not aim for full 50-70 mile days, only the first weeks (2025).
10. **Buy bikes around Spring Break** so riders can get used to them and get fitted (2026, 2025). Book a PT/bike fitter who comes to campus (Cycle Life PT; two one-hour sessions were partly covered by insurance in 2026).
11. **Learn bike repair**: MIT Bike Lab (free, weekly), Bikes Not Bombs bike school, Cambridge shops. 2026 had a bike-lab session RSVP. "Learn how your bikes work" is the one slide with warnings on it in both 2025 and 2026 crash courses (2026, 2025).
12. **Heat acclimation**: 2025 found that riders who spent rest days indoors in air conditioning suffered more heat exhaustion; build outdoor exposure in the first weeks (2025).

### 3. First aid and health prep (Feb–May)
13. **Get first aid trained.** Options named by the teams: MIT EMS (suggested 2026), Red Cross Basic First Aid or First Aid + CPR (2016: $70-90 per person, Cambridge), a Wilderness First Aid course (2013, Boston College). 2017's list says every member should hold a first aid certificate and CPR (2026, 2017, 2016, 2013). Track who is certified on a sheet.
14. **Dog training / dog protocol** was proposed by Drew in 2026; no course was found, so write your own plan (see Dogs below) (2026).
15. **Vaccines**: 2026 team members got Tdap before leaving (2026). After the bite the team learned the rabies and tetanus protocol the hard way (see What worked).
16. **Buy first aid kits.** 2026 sheet: 7 kits, 4 walkie-talkies; 2024: 8 bike kits at $13.41, 2 car kits, antigen tests (600), large bottles of ibuprofen, acetaminophen, naproxen, Tums, Benadryl, anti-itch, aloe, water purification tablets (2026, 2024). The 2017 Hub list: wound wipes, butterfly closures, gauze, tape, nitrile gloves, moleskin, elastic bandage, cold packs, blunt scissors, tick forceps, triple antibiotic, sting wipes, ibuprofen, acetaminophen, antihistamine, aspirin (2017). Also carry **waterproof bandages** (a dog bite dressing peeled off on a water slide in 2026) and an **EpiPen** for anyone with a known allergy (2025).
17. **Medication and allergies**: ask hosts about ingredients; the 2025 almond-flour reaction happened at a small-town cafe. Know the nearest urgent care before each remote day (2025).

### 4. Safety rules to write down and sign (Apr–May)
18. **Riding rules to adopt** (Spokes 2017 + Bike & Build): helmet always; never bike alone, groups of 2-4 ("sweet spot"); obey traffic laws; hand signals and calling out hazards; ride predictably, 18-24 inches from the edge; lights front and rear (flashing red tail light); daylight riding; no earbuds blocking traffic noise (bone-conducting earbuds are the 2026 compromise); no drafting without asking; stop 10 feet off the road; never leave a bike unattended (2017, 2018, 2022, 2026).
19. **Rules the 2026 team actually used**: van can order you in if conditions are dangerous; meeting points and late-arrival plan set in advance; download routes (even multi-day) before cell gaps; check weather at every rest stop; the car shadows riders in dog country (2026).
20. **2025 norms (June 6)**: 4 walkie-talkies with a buddy system, driver checks tomorrow's route the night before, the driver may deploy the car for rescue in storms or darkness, pump tires daily (about 45 psi) (2025).
21. **Never ride on interstates unless there is a legal shoulder and a plan.** 2022 used the I-70 shoulder between Glenwood Springs and Rifle (drivers moved over for hi-vis vests) but shuttled when rain made the shoulder unsafe; 2026 said the stretch out of Moab to I-70 at Crescent Junction was their most dangerous road (70 mph traffic about a foot away) (2026, 2022).
22. **Visibility**: 1000+ lumen front lights for rural roads (2026 advice); the 2026 gear order was Blackburn Dayblazer 550/Grid sets (7 sets, $329). Night riding happened in 2025 and 2026; riders wore white jerseys as capes on the overnight Highway 50 ride and used a "CAR" call (2026). Avoid shoulderless roads at dusk: call for a pickup (2024).

### 5. Emergency protocol (adopt the 2017 plan and update it)
23. **Critical injury**: someone stays with the rider; check if critical; call 911; first aid/CPR; call team lead; team lead informs MIT and sends the driver. **Non-critical**: stay with them, call the lead, lead decides on hospital and sends the vehicle. (2017 Emergency Procedures V1.0)
24. **Lost riders**: call the lead; if not back on route after 15 minutes, stop and wait; driver sent 30 minutes past the scheduled stop. (2017)
25. **Vehicle collision**: driver calls responders, then the lead; collect other driver's insurance info; call the rental company; do not give personal insurance, say it is a company car insured through MIT; fill the rental company's incident form ASAP (2017, 2025 gear guide).
26. **MIT reporting**: for any accident call Edgerton (Chris Mayer in 2025; in 2027 start with Marvelin and Justin) and the MIT Insurance Office (Sarah Brown, auto claims); get a police report; submit rental agreement both sides, accident report, travel expense report, Origami Risk incident form (2025 Travel FAQ).
27. **Aggressive animals**: no eye contact, ride away, spray with pepper spray or mace, or dismount and hold the bike between you and the dog; call the van if they persist (2017). See Dogs below.
28. **Weather**: if lightning is seen, stop and take cover; do not stray from the route; coordinate pickup; if severe, the team gathers, decides to wait, stop or drive (2017). 2026 advice: get low, away from the road, with someone; check radar at each stop; keep a radio in case you lose network (2026).
29. **Stranger confrontation / theft**: walk away, call authorities if extreme; for a stolen bike, buy a replacement (2017).
30. **If a rider has to leave**: the 2017 "Injury Contingency Plans" list the open questions (hospital fees, flight home, how long to wait for a hospitalized rider, minimum team size) and were never answered in the Drive. Decide them before leaving. Bike & Build stopped for 3 days after a fatality and then continued (2017). In 2024 Cleo stayed as a passenger for a few days before flying home (2024).

### 6. On the road
31. **Dogs (Kentucky, southern Virginia, Kansas, Illinois)**: see the table below.
32. **Heat**: start before dawn on 100 F days (2024: 4:30-5:45 am starts), ice in the cooler (2026: no ice at 5 gas stations between Grand Junction and Moab), electrolytes plus plain water, shade/AC stops at gas stations, rest stops every 10 miles on 105+ F days, know the signs of heat stroke and cool the patient without causing shivering (2024, 2026, 2013). Driver checks on riders and offers a ride early (2025).
33. **Altitude and Colorado storms**: riders from sea level got winded and nauseated climbing from Golden and Loveland Pass; eat and drink early; start early to beat afternoon lightning (2026, 2022, 2013).
34. **Descents**: long gravel or dirt descents caused the worst crashes (2025 Day 62, 2024 Day 54, 2025 Day 39) and Drew's brakes overheated in 2026 (Day 56). Brake early, stop to cool rotors, and wait at the bottom for slower riders (2026, 2025, 2024).
35. **Air quality**: Nevada wildfire smoke reached "very unhealthy" and the 2026 team called off the Baker to Ely ride on Aug 3 (2026).
36. **Food safety**: a bad cooler made someone sick in 2024; stomach bugs went around in 2024 and 2026; keep sealed items at the bottom of the cooler, check food temperatures (2024, 2026).
37. **After any head impact**: go to the ER for a quick check, take days off the bike if there is a concussion (2026 alum advice), recommended CT scan at the Kansas hospital in 2022; Amulya's 2024 concussion was cleared to keep riding. Replace helmets after crashes (1-year warranty covered Aarushi's in 2026; a WaveCel replacement was about $100 in 2022).
38. **Do not push through** sharp repetitive pain, nerve numbness or knee pain: take a van day. Numbness in the pinky running to the elbow forced a SAG in 2026; knee pain responded to an easier gear in 2024; persistent finger numbness from handlebars hit several riders in 2025 (2026, 2025, 2024, 2022).

## What worked / what didn't
### 2026
- **Worked**: PT bike fits ($1,150 for 6) and the PT's stop-if-sharp-pain rule; walkie-talkies worked when cell service dropped during the dog bite; photographing the dog owner's plate led the sheriff to the owner; the follow car with horn and mace worked as a dog deterrent; Hokie Spokes (Blacksburg) repaired a crash bike the same day; the helmet warranty replaced Aarushi's; family-arranged lodging for rest days.
- **Didn't**: no dog protocol until after the bite (rescue squad and sheriff took about an hour; two counties argued over jurisdiction; the ambulance transfer took about 55 minutes); Waynesboro urgent care wait over an hour and a prescription not sent to the pharmacy until a signature was obtained (Day 7); first KY hospital had no X-ray machine (Tatiana's sprain took three hospitals, Day 16); a pickup truck reserved online was not held (Day 15); most of the team SAG'd on Day 2 after 2-4 hours of sleep and 88 miles; overnight century to Benicia ended at 3:20 AM with riders strung out 20-60 minutes apart; second-degree burns from frying burgers in hot oil; pepper spray leaked in a bike bag.
- The team wanted a safety officer role and "applicable safety trainings" such as dog training and MIT EMS first aid, and proposed 2027 add it (2026 minutes).

### 2025
- **Worked**: first aid kit in the van used on the spot for a rider collision; EpiPen carried by a teammate saved Sarah on Day 64; chewable electrolytes helped a dizzy rider; the van swapped in for riders caught in a storm; locals' advice at a junction avoided a flooded road (Day 14).
- **Didn't**: riders who spent rest days indoors suffered heat exhaustion in late June; several riders did not know their bike had a second set of gears and rode the hardest gear all day; stop sign near-miss on a hill; Katy Trail metal post caused a head hit; Tian's serious gravel descent fall; routes not downloaded before a no-service campground; a rider mounted an iPad on the handlebars (do not copy).

### 2024
- **Worked**: starting at 4:30 am on 100+ F days, rotating the lead every 5 miles, hosts supplying water and electrolytes (Hank's parents).
- **Didn't**: no storm plan (30 minutes of indecision on Day 29; hosts asked weekly what the plan was); Cleo broke her elbow on a steep descent after skipping brakes and left the team; a rental van with bald tires stranded riders (see `../docs/2024/car-rental/2024_spokes-2024-car-troubles.md`); a bad cooler made a rider sick; one rider got no front light and nearly rode in the dark.

### 2022
- **Worked**: Nuun electrolyte tablets ended Day 1 leg cramps; hi-vis vests made drivers move over on I-70; a pre-trip bike safety workshop (funded by the Baker Foundation) produced the roadside toolbox.
- **Didn't**: a deer strike with sun glare in Kansas; a rider tested positive for COVID and spent nine days in hotel isolation in Reno; early-June 104 F heat in Kentucky.

### 2017–2019
- 2017's Emergency Procedures and Safety Rules were drafted for the Hub (the summary calls them a reusable plan for MIT approval); 2018 added the Bike & Build safety program and group A/B navigator system (essentials kit: multitool, tire levers, patch kit, tube, frame pump, first aid, water, snacks); 2018 Day 2 lesson: eat and drink more often than you think (2018). 2019: groups of 2-4 are safest; double shorts and chamois cream; medkit within reach in the car (2019).
