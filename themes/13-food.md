# Food — budget, groceries, cooking, meal plans, nutrition, donations/sponsors, dietary needs
_Last built: 2026-11-24. Sources: ~50 docs, ~160 blog posts (via `blogs/by-tag/food-nutrition.md` plus spot checks of 2026 posts), 0 emails (the email digests contain nothing on food). Recent years first; every fact has its year._

## TL;DR for 2027
- **Decide early whether you're a "cook" team or a "ask-for-free-food" team.** 2026 cooked **zero** meals all summer. One person called restaurants every night asking for free food, and the master guide calls this "extremely successful". Earlier teams (2022–2025) cooked most dinners and bought lots of groceries. (2026; 2025)
- **Real food cost is about $2.4k–$11.7k depending on strategy.** 2026 budgeted $7,200 ($80/day × 30 days × 3 months) and had logged only **$2,378** by the Oct 2026 budget sheet, because the team mostly ate hosted or donated dinners. 2025 budgeted **$11,663**, with $6,078 used on the sheet. Both are far below the $10k line items of 2023–2024. (2026, 2025, 2024, 2023)
- **Budget for the first week and for remote stretches separately.** 2025's first-week Safeway run was **$486.71**. 2026's Costco run in Gaithersburg MD was **$223.28**, plus Wegmans $102.74. Nevada (US-50), Utah desert, I-70 east of Grand Junction and eastern Colorado have no food for 40–75 miles, so stock up in Ely or the last big town. (2026, 2025, 2019)
- **Build a dietary roster before hosts and LFs ask.** 2025 had 1 vegan, 2 no-pork and 1 nut allergy. **A 2025 rider had an anaphylactic reaction** to an almond-flour muffin in Baker NV and needed an EpiPen and a 45-minute drive to urgent care. 2026 LF registrations flagged about 30 allergens out of 264 registrants. (2025, 2026, 2024)
- **Eat and drink more than you think.** Targets: about 60 g carbs per riding hour, snack hourly, electrolytes daily, and about 4,000+ kcal per ride day. 2026 Kansas days used about 30 Capri Suns and 90 pepperoni slices per day. Several bonks and a vomiting incident came from skipped lunch or rationed water. (2026, 2025, 2024, 2022)
- **Ask for free meals early.** 2026 got donated or free dinners from La Cabana, Mi Rancho, Thai Thai, Lucy's Tacos, a pizza place near Luray, Arches Thai, Tamarisk and Makisu Sushi, plus a Lamar steakhouse at **$20/person credit**. LF hosts (libraries, community buildings) often supply lunch. Ask them for it. (2026)
- **Costco and Trader Joe's paperwork.** The Costco donation form is paper only, handed in at a warehouse **4–6 weeks before the event**. Costco gave $500 in 2022 and may issue shop cards. Apply in March–April. (2025, 2023, 2022)
- **Cooler and kitchen failures are real risks.** A bad cooler made a 2024 rider sick, the 2025 propane stove failed for 3+ days and cost 2+ hours in Helena MT, and a church kitchen had no pot lid. Test the whole cooking setup before leaving and carry a spare canister. (2024, 2025)
- **Keep receipts for every grocery run.** Each food receipt needs a name, date and amount in the filename, and MIT auditors checked in 2026. The 2024 food category showed only $781 because most groceries were logged elsewhere. (2026, 2024)

## Playbook — how to do it

### 1. Pick the food model (decide in the semester before)
- **Model A, "call for free food" (2026):** a dinner lead (the master guide lists "Dinner Lead" as an in-trip role) calls restaurants nightly. Pitch: you're riding across the country teaching STEM to kids. Ask about the next 2–3 nights at once. Local restaurants gave meals; a steakhouse offered **$20 per person**; hosts and churches gave many dinners. Pair this with a grocery stock of breakfast, lunch and snacks. (2026)
- **Model B, "cook with a rotation" (2022–2025):** cooks rotate. 2023 used a "Dinner Cook" column in the meal-plan grid. 2025 had a pre-trip grocery sheet by meal and by person. You need a stove, two 10" pots, a pan, knives, cutting boards, baby wipes, a can opener, and a lid. (2025, 2024, 2023, 2022)
- **In practice teams used a hybrid.** Hosts and LF partners cover many dinners and breakfasts (the 2025 itinerary marks each breakfast and dinner as "WS host", "church", "LF providing", "campground" or "kitchen access"). Campgrounds and rest-day Airbnbs are where you cook. (2025, 2026)

### 2. Collect diets and preferences early
- Send everyone a form: allergies, never-eat list, typical diet, foods wanted regularly, and "what else should we know". 2022 did this in Feb 2022 (Google Form; 7 responses including egg, milk, vegetarian, and "never eat" lists). 2024 sent a dietary form to hosts. 2018 and 2017 kept a roster with a diet column. (2022, 2024, 2018, 2017)
- Give hosts, church kitchens and LF partners the summary (1 vegan, 2 no pork, 1 nut allergy in 2025). Ask hosts about dietary needs on the first outreach. The 2026 church follow-up email asks "how do dinner on the [dates] and breakfast work (provided, or kitchen to cook in)? Are there showers and laundry?" (2026, 2025, 2024)
- Allergen safety: carry **EpiPens**, know each rider's reaction plan, and **always ask about ingredients at cafes and bakeries**. (2025)
- For LFs, note registration allergy fields (2026: 264 registrations; ~24 dietary needs; nut, celiac, lactose, food dye) and plan lunch accordingly. (2026)

### 3. Fuel plan on the bike
- **Carbs in the morning and while riding; protein at dinner and later for recovery; electrolytes while biking** (Master Guide 2026). (2026)
- Target about **60 g carbs per hour**, snack roughly hourly, mostly fast carbs and dried fruit. (2022)
- Electrolytes everywhere: Nuun, Liquid IV, Propel, Gatorade. **A tub of Gatorade powder is the cheapest**; also carry packets. 2025 ran out of packets and made gallon batches of fruit-punch electrolyte drink. 2022 Nuun tablets from Target ended leg cramps on day 1. (2026, 2025, 2022)
- Daily: eat breakfast and bring lunch; **don't skip lunch**. 2026: skipping lunch on a 4am start led to a bonk; Day 73 bonked after not eating enough. 2024: a rider ran out of food by mile 35 on the Katy Trail. (2026, 2024)
- On big climbs carry carbs. In 2026 a protein-heavy breakfast left riders short of carbs on an 18-mile opening climb. (2026)
- Food to plan for the remote stretches: US-50 Eureka to Austin (**75 miles with no services**, 2026); Baker to Ely (no viable food stops, 2025); I-70 Grand Junction to Moab (**40–50 miles with no services**, 2025); Baker NV has no grocery (2024); "stock up on food and propane in Ely at the latest" (2019). (2026, 2025, 2024, 2019)

### 4. Shopping
- **First week:** do a big Costco run before the DC start. 2026's list: bars, rolls, cheese, turkey, ham, beef sticks, mango, nuts, avocado mash, Cheez-It, mayo, fig bars ($223.28). A **grocery team** (2 people, 2 hours) bought lunch and snacks for at least two days, "carb and fat dense" (2026 minutes, June 1). Note: **Costco in DC required the card owner at checkout; a borrowed-card workaround failed.** (2026)
- **Daily:** the **driver** does the grocery run; give them a written list so they don't need four supermarkets (2025 Sedalia day: four stops). Keep a list of what's low. (2025, 2024)
- **Where:** Costco and Walmart are "your friend", and you'll miss Walmart in remote areas. Aldi, Kroger, Safeway, Save A Lot, City Market, and small town grocery stores all show up on receipts. Park and national-park groceries are expensive (Capitol Reef 2025). Ask a store about free bread after 5 pm (a South Lake Tahoe Safeway, 2024). (2026, 2025, 2024)
- **Cheap staples:** Minute Rice, bagels, French bread (very cheap from Walmart and tastes better, found late in 2024), tortillas, sardines, canned chicken or tuna, hummus, peanut butter, instant potatoes, Spam, shelf-stable tofu, textured vegetable protein, powdered milk and instant coffee. (2026 Master Guide, 2024, 2022)
- **Ice daily:** nearly every day in July/Aug 2026 has an ice receipt. Cold soft drinks or iced tea from the driver "can be life saving". (2026)
- **Don't over-buy:** 2018 "tossed a lot of food, buy less than you think". 2025 "big grocery runs overfill the cooler". Keep at most one bag on top of the cooler so lunches are reachable, unload the cooler at night, check that every drink is in the cooler (cans burst in heat). (2018, 2025)
- Use a **food inventory with use-by dates** (2023 sheet: name, date bought, days it'll last, date it'll go bad, amount 1–10). (2023)

### 5. Cooking and kitchen gear
- 2-pot meals that work: jambalaya, feijoada, chicken salad (hotel nights), tacos, chicken and rice, spring rolls (no stove), one-pot pasta. Camp grilled cheese feeds a group. (2026 Master Guide, 2025)
- Gear (2024 cooking tab): 12 qt stockpot (the stove fits two 10" pots), 10" non-stick and stainless frying pans, cutting boards, knives, 3 qt kettle; a pocket stove boiled up to 9 eggs at a time in 2022. Don't fry big batches in hot oil: 2026 got second-degree burns frying burgers; use the grill or oven. (2024, 2022, 2026)
- **Test the stove and propane setup before leaving.** 2025's refillable tank, adapter and new stove all failed; a small camping canister worked. (2025)
- Bring a pot lid, a can opener (kept in an easy-reach box), baby wipes (pre-wipe dishes), paper towels as plates. (2025, 2022, 2026 Master Guide)
- **Storing food:** keep food in the car overnight. Raccoons stole bagels at Capitol Reef (2025), squirrels opened bike bags at Mammoth Cave (2024), and a black bear walked past an open dumpster near Lake Tahoe (2026). Roof bag storage made sleeping gear smell and mildew (2026). (2026, 2025, 2024)
- **Cooler:** replace a cooler that doesn't hold temperature. A rider got sick from the team food and cooler in 2024; Yeti was considered too big. (2024)

### 6. Free and donated food
- **Restaurants:** call ahead; "just sell your cause" (advice from a 75-person cross-country team the 2026 team met). 2026 results below under Key contacts. Local restaurants will donate meals; ask a steakhouse early; $20 per person credit is realistic. (2026)
- **Ask when you walk in:** a Denver ice-cream shop gave free toppings and a size upgrade after hearing about the ride (2024); a Fallon diner gave free biscuits and gravy to tired riders on the overnight ride (2026); Hoodoo's General Store gave free all-day parking for $25 of snacks (2025).
- **Stores:** Costco warehouse donation form (2022 gave $500; 2023 and 2025 applications filed); Trader Joe's letters (2013 asks, 2025 in-person asks; no recorded outcome). Whole Foods and Trader Joe's were on the 2013 "potential partners" list. (2025, 2023, 2022, 2013)
- **Restaurant fundraiser days (percent of sales):** use the 2024 template; it was used for the May 1, 2024 DIG fundraiser. (2024)
- **Food-sale fundraisers:** 2024 Lobby 10 boba sale: $1,045 sales, $640 boba cost, **$405 profit**. Judge effort against that. (2024)
- **Hosts:** Warmshowers hosts "usually also give you food" (2026, 2019); churches provided dinners, breakfast and kitchens throughout; church kitchens may have no lid, no showers, or unclear access, so confirm. (2026, 2025)
- **LF partners often feed the team**: pizza lunch (Lamar CO, WKU Innovation Campus, DC DYRS, Fluvanna Library), restaurant lunch (Glenwood Springs library), breakfast and dinner delivered (Fallon Youth Club), breakfast burritos (Ely library, 2025). Ask them in the first logistics email. (2026, 2025)

### 7. Thank-you's
- Each host should get a thank-you card (a role in the master guide). 2024 cooked a hosted thank-you dinner (Mediterranean menu: falafel, couscous, eggplant). (2026, 2024)

## What worked / what didn't

### 2026
- **Worked:** one person calling restaurants nightly gave many free dinners and cut real food spend to about a third of budget. Hosts also fed the team heavily (Alison's taco and burrito bar in Breckenridge, Dirk and Chrissie's pasta bar, Sherie's blueberry french toast, Aarushi's family's Indian meals, Nate's family at Bryce). The "Venn diagram" snack approach (buy what most of the team likes and repeat it) fed the team all summer. (2026)
- **Worked:** libraries and community buildings supplying lunch (Glenwood, Lamar, WKU). A pizza call near Luray got pizzas donated. A stranger (Jimmy Baker) gave $1,000 at a gas-pump rest stop and offered to buy food. (2026)
- **Didn't:** skipped lunch after a 4am start caused a bonk (Day 31). Kansas days use about twice the snacks (30 Capri Suns, 90 pepperoni slices, 3.5 lb grapefruit, 2.25 lb gummy worms, 2 trays watermelon in one day, Day 35). Gas-station and food-hall prices were high ($20–$25 burger at a Colorado food hall; $8 lemonade). Lunch stops ran 2 hours. (2026)
- **Didn't:** gear stored in the roof bag smelled and lost items (a team-gear loss on I-65). Frying burgers in hot oil caused second-degree burns. Costco in DC refused a borrowed card. Registrations for LFs arrived late (Lamar, two days before), so lunch counts were uncertain. (2026)
- **Didn't:** most receipts were not uploaded: the ledger shows only 10 food receipts ($533.43) while the budget sheet shows $2,378 spent. (2026)

### 2025
- **Worked:** a by-meal grocery sheet with each rider's likes (breakfast, lunch, dinner, snack, beverage tabs). The standard lunch kit (turkey, cheese, hummus, avocado sandwiches; hummus + pesto + avocado for the vegan). Big first-week Safeway shop ($486.71). Hosts and churches fed many dinners (Fowler church: burgers and fresh produce; Macksville church breakfast; Natasha and Dan's grilled dinner). A driver float station (ice cream and soda) and groceries (watermelon, smoothies) boosted morale. Leftover-first lunches on hot days. (2025)
- **Didn't:** anaphylaxis from an almond-flour muffin in Baker NV (EpiPen, 45 min to urgent care). Cans burst in the unloaded cooler. Raccoons ate bagels. Propane stove failure cost 2+ hours in Helena MT and days of sandwich dinners. A rider rationed water, then chugged drinks and vomited (Jul 4). Heat exhaustion and dizziness in the high 90s, relieved by chewable electrolytes and gel. A tuna can with no opener. Rice cooked to porridge for lack of a lid. (2025)
- **Didn't:** a cafe near Capitol Reef closed at 9pm; the park campground restaurant dinner was $25 per person; the Bryce camp restaurant was expensive and the pizzeria had a long wait, so cook at camp. (2025)

### 2024
- **Worked:** bagel sandwiches for about 74 days ("French bread, super cheap from Walmart, found too late"), powdered milk + instant coffee + cereal + powdered PB ("Spokes 24 classic"), Nature's Bakery brownie bars, hummus ("very important"). Cooking in a bar's industrial kitchen in Lamar for the whole team. Hosts such as Jen and Tim offered vegan meal options. (2024, from the Master Guide)
- **Didn't:** a rider got ill from the team's food and cooler (Day 16), so a better cooler was recommended. Food was mis-categorised in finances. "Shenandoah emergency food" cost $91.01 on Day 7 from a personal card. (2024)
- **Worked:** the dietary form for hosts. Jess and Amulya (vegan/vegetarian) were ready to find their own food if hosts had none. (2024)

### 2023
- **Worked:** shelf-stable and precooked proteins (rotisserie chicken, meatballs, jerky, canned fish; tofu and plant-based substitutes); premade sauces and curry pastes; Minute Rice; multivitamins; cooler for eggs, yogurt, cheese, plant milk, tofu. 3 vegetarian or vegan members were accommodated by the likes/dislikes list. (2023)
- **Didn't:** the empty daily meal-plan grid (Jun 5–Aug 20) was never filled in the shared file, so we don't know how much of it was followed. (2023)

### 2022
- **Worked:** tortillas and sardines as the lunch staple. Dates from Costco ("carried the 2022 team"). Post-LF ice cream tradition (the library coordinator recommended it). Pocket stove for eggs. Foam sleeping pads as lunch seats. Paper towels as plates. (2022)
- **Didn't:** sardines every day got old; the team's dietary survey asked for lots of fresh produce, but camping and car space limited it. Nuun tablets were needed day 1 for cramping. (2022)

### 2013–2019 (short)
- 2019: Nevada resupply warning, some Utah grocery stores don't accept Visa travel cards, driver gets groceries. (2019)
- 2018: eat and drink more than you think. "Don't do PB and tuna sandwiches" and "don't buy potatoes". Tell Warmshowers hosts about allergies. (2018)
- 2016: tuna/PB/Nutella sandwiches, protein bars, trail mix, pasta and soup dinners; "should have eaten more fruit and fresh food"; Gatorade powder in water. (2016)
- 2013: 5,000 kcal/day, 7 L water capacity, oatmeal/PB sandwiches/pasta as budget staples. (2013)
