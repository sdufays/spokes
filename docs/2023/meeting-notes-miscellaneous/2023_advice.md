From Audrey’s former teacher

That sounds like an amazing trip! I would definitely get comfortable changing tubes, and also patching tubes. You can carry a ton of patch kits for the weight and size of a few spare tubes, so I'd carry both patch kids and tubes, and maybe swap out tubes once they're ready to retire. Maybe get comfortable changing spokes, too, or at least make sure someone in the group is okay with that. And basic chain maintenance, keeping it clean and running smoothly will make your daily riding more pleasant. Pumping up the tires every morning to the right pressure as well. Even in a group, I'd probably want to make sure I personally had what I need to do minor work myself, especially change a tube: a small pump, patch kit, spare tubes, tire levers, whatever wrench you need to get the wheel off, lube and rag for the chain, a multitool that has hex keys.

I think one of the biggest things is no backpacks! You want to be as comfortable as possible while riding, and keeping all the weight on the bike, not you, so panniers (you can do front and back, hopefully just back), those little saddle bags under the seat, maybe a handlebar bag/phone holders for snacks/phones, but those can be cumbersome or distracting. And comfortable clothing, keeps the rain/sun off you, finding a bike that fits you (you'll be on it a long time), has two or three water bottle holders. I think about a gallon of water storage would be good. I found the less stopping the better, so having stuff where you can grab it while riding was nice. Camelbaks weren't as popular back then, so I didn't use one, but I bet people will opt for those, but they might make your back sweaty or chafe after a long, hot ride. Fingerless bike gloves were nice, too! Variety of layers again for all kinds of weather. And I didn't love weary dorky bike shorts, I'd usually wear some light athletic shorts over them, but I think the padded chamois ones were way better than normal clothes, and you'll be very thankful you wore them at the end of the day.

Plotting a safe route, also vital, but maybe that's already been done, but avoiding sketchy roads and staying on established routes (or even better those rail-to-trail paths) will help your sanity and let you enjoy the ride!
