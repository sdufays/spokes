Spokes Gear and Car Master Guide

Choosing And Buying Bikes
Trek Cambridge will give you 20% on all gear you purchase through them. Use this for big purchases. Cambridge Bicycle will sell you things at wholesale price but have smaller selection, use them for small purchases.

Here are some recommendations
Go tubeless! We found that we got fewer flats and repairs were much more simple than if we had to deal with inner tubes
Have everyone get the same type of bike. Can swap out parts between bikes for temporary fixes
You must get gravel bikes.
Disc brakes
Drop handlebars
Tire size: 35 mm tires or wider
Shop around at different bike shops to see if they offer better prices.
Bike shops
Recommend Cambridge Trek in Alewife. Negotiate 20% discount.
Recommend Landry’s. They gave us discount on gear (but not bikes)
Do not go to Cambridge Bicycle. Overpriced

Note from Spokes 26:

We got Trek Chekpoint Alr3 gen3 bikes for everyone, and then got bike fits with Brendan Connor at CycleLifePT

 We chose the Trek Checkpoints because:
The 1x10 gear setup removes the troublesome front derailleur an has a massive back back gear for steep climbs- helps a ton in appalachia.
Wide tire clearance is super important. We spend a significant minority of our time offroads.
Working with trek is pretty nice to work with, We were able to squeeze out several hundred dollars worth of various warranties throughout the trip.
Bike Gear
We left you some bike gear, camping supplies, LF supplies. It’s stored in Milkdrop N52. Previous team can show you where, and you coordinate with Edgerton about storing stuff there.
Here is a list of things to consider buying along with your bike. Good to keep these things on you while biking
Water bottles and water bottle holders
This is a must
Get metal water bottles.  2026 discovered via super el nino that plastic water bottles are not sufficient for keeping your water cool. The money you save on ice will offset the extra cost of getting at least one metal water bottle per person. We got the camelback podium metal (the tall one).
I recommend going ahead and buying metal ones because the plastic ones will break!
See how many you can fit on your bike. During spokes JD had 3 water bottle holders when he was biking through the Utah/Nevada deserts
Some people with smaller bike frames bought camelbacks which hold 60oz of water or something crazy
Frame Bags & Saddle Bags
You need this to hold your snacks (a constant source of carbs while biking is important for biking to not bonk)
This will also be important for holding other stuff on this list
Small first aid kit
You never know when you’ll need it!
Small swiss army knife (optional)
Very handy
Spare tubes
Make sure they match the tire size on your bike
Bike multitool
Make sure it has all the allen keys to repair a flat tire
Small frame mounted pump
Used when you’re fixing your flat
Portable charger (strongly recommended)
Could help you out in a pinch
Also good to have when you’re camping w/o electricity

Car Rental
Type of Car + Rental Company
MIT has a discount on rentals with Budget and Avis and would recommend renting through them. Make sure to talk to Sandi (slipnosk@mit.edu) and Chris (mayerc@mit.edu) about car rentals. For 2024, Chris was the main point of contact. Make the reservation as soon as possible (as soon as you know when you are leaving Boston). We recommend getting a minivan. Preferred Budget location to rent from is the one in Central Square, on 350 Mass Ave. Spokes 2022 could not get a minivan and had to use an SUV (Ford Explorer. We only had 6 so we were able to make it work. We do like the Ford Explorer.) instead. Make sure that the car and the bike rack are compatible. The bike rack used in 2022 is the Saris Bones EX.
2023 and 2024 used a 2022 Chrysler Pacifica. Spokes 24 does not recommend the bike rack that we got, it bent and fell towards the end of the trip.

Avis/Budget is not nice and in our opinion a bad car rental company. Sadly they are the only company that MIT has an agreement with that will cover drivers under 21. You should check in to see if this has changed though. If you have issues talk to Edgerton and also Jessica Tam from the office of the VP for Finance. She helped us get a discount after we had a lot of rental car troubles. For an account of the nonsense we went through with Avis/Budget, please refer to this doc.

Spokes 23 & 24& 26 also rented a truck for the first few days to drive the bikes + extra supplies + people down. You also book this through Edgerton. Spokes 24 got a 2024 Toyota Tacoma and it was awesome, we were able to fit 3 bikes in the truck bed (seen in this blog). It was a fairly large truck though. Keep in mind that you can’t guarantee a specific make and model when you book and the rental co will just give you what they have. We turned this in at Washington Dulles airport which is ~50 min from DC but close to Oakton (where we started the trip). Spokes 2026 got a cargo van.
Car Size
With all of our luggage, LF supplies, bike gear, food, and miscellaneous bullshit in the car, we were only able to have the driver and passenger seats available for use. A minivan also only has 7 seats, and there were 8 of us. When we were driving somewhere we usually would just move some stuff out of the car and cram a bunch of people in the back seat.

An SUV would have 8 seats, and also more space for packing so you probably would be able to get more than 2 seats up. However, this would be more expensive. Also, driver comfort is very important and should not be overlooked. For some of our drivers, it was their first time driving regularly and an SUV initially can be hard to maneuver (and park). I think you can get used to it, but people might still be scared, and your risk of incident will definitely go up. (With the minivan, we got into one small accident with no injuries, and also once we hit a pole while parking in an underground lot. Not saying this to scare you, it’s just a reality of cars sadly.) Sandi recommended against getting a SUV due to this problem, but there are pros and cons to both options. Don’t get anything smaller than a minivan though.

Stanford Spokes 24 had a 11 seater van (like this), and they all said it wasn’t that bad to drive. You could definitely reach out to them to get their thoughts too.
One-way vs Two-way Rental
Spokes 23 & 24 both got one way rentals, and dropped them off at SFO. A 1 way rental is going to be more expensive by a few thousand dollars. Past Spokes teams have gotten a 2 way rental and a few East Coast members drove it back to Boston, along with some of the bikes and gear.

Because of the one way rental, we had to ship a lot of our gear and bikes back to Boston. This was a huge hassle (took us 2 days) and really expensive. Shipping across the country is really costly. To be honest after our experience with shipping, it might not have necessarily been the best choice. Also we covered travel costs for people going back to the East Coast anyways. I know that right now it feels like a lot to ask people to drive after the bike trip, but it could be another form of adventure for people who are already heading back to the East Coast. And it will allow some level of rest once you get to SF.

Getting a two way rental however does not solve the issue that people not going back to the East Coast still have to get their bikes to their destination. One idea is to check your bike on your plane, which some airlines allow.
Background Checks and Acquiring Car
Get in touch with Edgerton to also start the background checks that are needed for students to drive the car. They will be sent to people’s emails and are really quick to fill out. Be sure to bump people to fill them out since they might take a while to be processed. You cannot drive the MIT rental car without one.

Edgerton will book the car for you and take the money directly from the account, so it won’t involve the travel card. However, you can use the T card to book a car on the trip if there’s an emergency. To get the MIT discount and insurance, go to this website and navigate to the page for the specific company.

Before the trip/towards the beginning, you should also have 4 drivers go to a service desk for Avis/Budget to add them to the rental so that they will be able to request roadside assistance. They only allow 5 drivers (person who the rental is under + 4 more) to be added to the car so they can call for help. You can still drive the car if you are not added, as long as you have the background check. Yes, this is an incredibly stupid policy. But once they refused to get us roadside assistance because we didn’t know about this, and the person named on the rental was biking. You should also check in with Edgerton to see if this has changed at all, but this is a very necessary step. I would recommend doing it in Boston or at Dulles airport in DC before the trip starts.

When you get a rental car, ask for the last time it was serviced and also do a whole walk around of the car. Take photos of anything that you see like existing scratches. Check the engine and the tires. Also, look up if that make and model has had a recall on it. This is literally from our experience :|
Car Troubles
Spokes 19, 23, & 24 all had some sort of car trouble. For 23 & 24, it was concentrated in the first week. Here’s what to do.
Rental companies don’t want you doing larger repairs to the car really. That means that your car won’t come with any repair tools like a tire change kit.
Note from 2026: don’t speed in Vail Colorado.
Another note from 2026: We had to rip the oil pan cover off in Kansas because it was dragging under the car. I don’t think they ever found out.
Call the 24 hour roadside assistance number that’s on the keys to the car.
If one of the 5 people who is eligible to call roadside assistance isn’t with the car, call Edgerton to help you out. When Spokes 24 had this happen, we eventually were able to get a tow authorized with Edgerton’s help.
Unfortunately there doesn’t seem to be a way around this rule, since they ask to see your ID when they come. I would recommend talking to Edgerton before the trip leaves to see if there’s any way to make this situation easier.
When we needed repairs, Avis just switched out the whole car. This is super annoying because obviously the car is loaded with gear, and once you get to the middle of the country Avis/Budget locations can be far away.

Accidents or Damage
In the case of an accident/damage to the car:
Call Edgerton (probably Chris for y’all).
MIT doesn’t have a physical insurance card for the coverage that we have. Don’t give your personal insurance, but show the rental car agreement stuff and explain the insurance situation. State that this is a company car through your school, and you are insured through MIT. We called Sandi who explained it to the other driver.
If you can get some kind of written proof of insurance from MIT to keep in the glove box that would be really good.
You’ll have to fill out a Avis/Budget accident incident form and send to MIT. Try to get all this done ASAP
It’ll all be okay! You are insured :)
Day2Day Car
The car will get gross. This is inevitable sadly. Step 1 is to accept this fact, and step 2 is to mitigate it. We recommend buying a small hand vacuum to clean dust/crumbs from the car. And some air fresheners :/

Spokes 2026 Note: It actually won’t get that bad if you are proactive about not getting the car dirty.

Also, would 100000% recommend investing in some hard sided plastic boxes to put stuff in rather than playing Tetris on impossible mode every day. We got these at Walmart for pretty cheap. We had one or two crack but they were mostly reliable.

One of the tires often deflated, so it would be good if everyone knew how to fill up the air in a car tire, and other basic car maintenance stuff. Or at least, they’ve read a random website article on the topic and would feel ok doing it on their own.

The car will be so overloaded with stuff that it will actually be lower to the ground than normal! This is important to know in case the car gets stuck on a really bumpy unmaintained road (like ours did). This was solved by taking enough stuff out of the car until it lifted up and could be driven off the bump.
