Spokes Fundraising and Budget Master Guide

Fundraising
Resource:

2026 Fundraising: Spokes 2026 Fundraising
2024 Fundraising: Spokes 2024 Fundraising
2022 Fundraising:2022 Spokes Fundraising Tracking Spreadsheet

Start early! After getting into Spokes, you should hit the ground running and start fundraising.
You need more than one person handling fundraising. Additionally, it’s best to fundraise as early as possible--by the time spring rolls around, a lot of the excess money that companies might donate to Spokes (Texas Instruments, Microsoft, Dell, etc.) is already gone.

A good place to start is MIT departments. Send a cold-call email to department heads, administrators, etc. asking for money. A good place to look for template emails is the “Fundraising” folder in the “Spokes 2026” Google Drive.

Another place to look are grant applications, inside and outside MIT. Inside MIT, we’ve historically received TBP funding ($2000) after an application process. We’ve also received money ($2000) from the Baker Foundation, for which we planned and hosted a “bike repair and safety workshop”. Also, the “Mad Money Grant” gave 2019 a few thousand for submitting a pamphlet with trip information.

We also reached out to alumni groups! They wouldn’t donate much usually, but it was a great connection and it also helped to get housing in some states :)

We also held a bake sale and a raffle for bike tune ups, this made around $2,000 total!

In 2024, most of our money ($45330) came from the JWEL Education Grant. Applications open in February and we would highly recommend you to apply again. However, since decisions don’t come out until April, make sure you are also pursuing other funding sources.

Outside MIT, there are a lot of corporate / nonprofit grants that have open applications! Check out 2022 Spokes Fundraising Tracking Spreadsheet to see the grants we’ve received, as well as more grants to apply to. We were funded by the St. Anthony Educational Foundation ($4000).

Also reach out to companies and see if they can help! We got help from Lightforce and Desktop Metal (3D-printing companies) with the 3D-printing workshop – we got real, professionally printed parts to showcase in our workshop! Lightforce also sponsored us ($5000) in 2022.

With companies, just beware that companies have to register with MIT for the privilege of donating to MIT (!). Basically, MIT wants to verify that donors don’t have any conflicts of interest before accepting the money. We had to go through this process with Lightforce. Talk to Sandi if you get a corporate donation.

Also, you should crowdsource! Ask family, friends, coworkers, Facebook friends, etc. if they can chip in a few bucks toward your trip and your mission of teaching STEM. Everyone in the group should participate in crowdsourcing.

A few tips:
Emphasize the education portion of the trip. The attraction to the sponsors is the teaching portion of the trip.
Offer sponsor benefits, like recognition on the website / the T-shirt (if applicable). Or if they’re local, pay them a visit and say “hi” in-person!
Cast a wide net and apply to a lot of places. Worst case, they say no.
Use the mail merge feature in Word to send out a lot of emails at once!!

Budget
The budget also dictates how much you need to fundraise, so you should have a nice budget.

Consult Expenses + Budget for our 2026 trip budget, in Google Drive.

A few tips:
Itemize as much of the budget as possible.
When buying bike gear, list out each item you’ll need and its cost at a local bike shop. Sum that up, and you have the bike gear cost.
When in doubt, consult the Spokes 2026 trip finances
We guessed transportation and food costs when we budgeted. Consult our budget maybe to get a rough sense of cost?

Finances
There are two types of credit cards you will need for the trip, the travel card and the purchasing card (procard). The travel card is for expenses that you have to make during the trip such as food and gas. The procard is for other expenses such as gear and learning festival supplies. If it would have made sense to make the purchase while at MIT before the trip, it should go on the procard. Note that “health and beauty” products (e.g. shampoo, body wash, sunscreen. Yes, even sunscreen) cannot be purchased on either card.

To get a pro or travel card, you need to complete some online training materials and fill out an application. Spokes 2026 had two people get pro cards and two different people get travel cards.
Travel Card
Travel card holders will have to make a Concur report at the end of the trip. Making this report is stupid tedious and complicated. Try to upload receipts during the trip as opposed to the end/after
You will need to upload receipts for purchases >$75 and any purchase of food
Even though you don’t technically need other receipts, it’s still good to catalogue purchases so you are sure of the types of purchases being made on your card. Sometimes gas purchases look like grocery purchases on the Concur report. And it just creates a good habit for the team
What happens if you lose a receipt for the travel card? Quotes from our best friend, Sandi Lipnoski:
“Any missing receipts for charges greater than $75.00, I will ask that you attach a missing receipt affidavit and mark the expenses as Non-Recoverable when assigning a description of the charge.”
“Non-recoverable is a category that indicates a problem in posting the charge, which alerts the auditors. In this case, it is due to a missing receipt over amount of $75.00. The worst thing that will happen is an inquiry about the charge if it seems questionable to the auditors. Otherwise, the missing receipt affidavit will satisfy their requirements of having a receipt for each charge over $75.00.”
“The JWEL account would be a sponsored account, since another department is sponsoring the trip. This basically means possibly another approval to go through when outside funds are used. The Spokes account is a department account.  Since I will be charging your travel expenses to the SPOKES account, the trip would be considered non-sponsored activity.”

Pro/Purchasing Card:
Procard holders will need to send receipts to Marvelin within two weeks of making the purchase. You’ll get an email from procard@mit.edu listing transactions that are still pending.
You may also occasionally get audited. When this happens, respond to the email explaining what was purchased and how it will be used. Minimize the number of times you get audited by not buying food, gas, or health & beauty products on the procard
If you respond to emails promptly, Marvelin will give you compliments 😊
What happens if you lose a receipt for the pro card? This is a worse situation. If the purchase gets audited and you can’t send the receipt, they might deactivate the pro card
Misc:
Purchases are tax exempt in MA but nowhere else
