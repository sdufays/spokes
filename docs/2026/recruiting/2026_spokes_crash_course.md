## Slide 4
STEP 1: FUNDRAISING
By far one of the most IMPORTANT parts of getting Spokes to go 🙏🏻
You need to fundraise ~$40K, we are leaving you with about ~$19K 
Email ALL the departments at MIT: it might be a bit rough this year with budgets cuts, but make sure to start ASAP since some funding deadlines close in the fall
Apply to all the grants (MIT and otherwise), Edgerton showcase
Crowdfund and ask your family and friends, post on social media (how we got most of our donations), email Maria Walsh for this
We had a raffle for bike tune ups and also had a bake sale
Y’all should try more outreach to companies and stuff: it’s hard to get monetary donations, but they might be willing to donate gear, equipment, etc. (get deals w/ bike shops!!)
## Slide 6
STEP 2: ROUTE PLANNING
Look @ previous years routes: they have definitely narrowed down and made less changes over the years as safer routes have been discovered and dangerous parts have been avoided
Some years go SF -> DC (no headwinds) but that’s lame :( 
Try to have a general route done by end of January/IAP so everything else can be planned (housing, LFs, etc.)
Try to follow Bike Route 76, as well as the Katy Trail in Missouri 
We’ll tell you which days routes to change
Ex. in kentucky, zion, alien gas station day
Plan in National Park stops! We hit the Big 5 in Utah (Zion, Bryce Canyon, Capitol Reef, Canyonlands, and Arches)
Things to take into account: SAFETY, types of terrain (for example gravel vs. busy highways), elevation, rest stops (we usually had them every 1-2 hours, or every 10-20 miles)
## Slide 7
STEP 3: LEARNING FESTIVALS 
As you are route planning, reach out to schools, libraries, churches, educational amps, etc. ESPECIALLY ones we have previous connections with (Kentucky, Fallon Youth Club, DC Jail)
Start reaching out end of January after the general route is made
A solid number of learning festivals is around 10
Do the learning festival at the correctional facility in D.C. as well as at the Fallon Youth Center in Nevada
See if you can coordinate with the facility in SF to do one there too
Do more learning festivals with correctional facilities!
Some kids are gonna be crazy you just gotta go with the flow sometimes 😭😭
We split into 4 groups, ran 4 lesson plans each festival, we didn’t have floaters but it still worked well
## Slide 8
STEP 4: HOUSING
Housing Options…
Warmshowers: the BEST ppl ever, they are bike-tourists who open up their homes to people on tour when they are home, usually also give you food and have the best stories, maximize these
Churches: nice people, def a good option especially in more deserted/less-populated areas
Learning Festivals: they will sometimes give you a place to stay!
Camping: chill, fun, especially in forests and other nice areas
We had 99% of our housing planned before we started and I think it made our lives 1000x easier
## Slide 9
STEP 5: BIKES!!! 
IDK if this was ever clarified but Spokes will buy your bike, and you get to KEEP it after 😍😍
2025 & 26 all got the same and it made repairs a lot easier plus we all looked dope AF
We got our bikes from Trek, who gave a group discount and free repairs/tune ups at other trek shops along the route 
GET GRAVEL BIKES bc there WILL be terrains other than road. TRUST US ON THIS PLZ. also it makes biking way more fun, you can go on far more beautiful roads
We also got tubeless tires which is great, BUT make sure to maintain them correctly (max 35 psi), we were overinflating ours which led to flats after flats in Kansas
Try and buy your bikes around Spring Break so you can get used to riding them, get them fitted (we have a deal with a PT who will come to campus to get you fit!!! It’s amazing trust).
Practice w/ clip-on shoes! You will fall over if you don’t practice
Also if you can learn bike repair it saves time AND money

## Slide 14
STEP 9: DO YOUR FIRST RIDE!
“You are gonna be a skill issue on wheels” - JD ‘24
You might have some aches and pains for like 1-2 weeks, that is normal 💔
Anything beyond that is uh. not. get that checked out!
Anti-chafing cream (chamois butter) and topical pain relievers will be your #besties
SUNSCREEN!!! WATER!!!
Sun sleeves save lives
Take things slow if you need :) this is a 4,000 mile marathon not a sprint! 😗
## Slide 15
OTHER GENERAL SPOKES TIPS:
We naturally split into groups of 2-4 people, sometimes we rode all together, sometimes we rode alone, depends on the mood, weather conditions, etc.
Ways to stay entertained: music, podcasts, audiobooks, shows, games, calling people if it’s quiet enough and you have service, the lovely scenery 😍
Highly recommend getting “bone-conducting” earbuds: allow you to listen to stuff but still be aware of what’s going on around you
There will be areas lacking cell service: download your routes beforehand (sometimes for multiple days)(bike computers are your friend!), set your meeting points with other bikers/the driver in advance, make a plan of what to do if someone doesn’t show up by a certain time
Make a plan of what to do in dangerous weather (CHECK THIS AHEAD OF TIME AND AT EACH REST STOP): some general good tips are to get down (and get cover if you can), get away from the road if you can, and stick together with someone
We had a rule that if the van deemed a situation dangerous and came back to get you, you HAD to get in
There will be nice cars 😄 and there will be not nice cars 😔
