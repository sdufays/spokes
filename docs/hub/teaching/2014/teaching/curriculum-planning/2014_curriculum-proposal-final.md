Spokes 2014 Curriculum Summary

FORMAT:

Spokes will be teaching to students in a formal classroom setting, as well as to summer programs in libraries, community centers and camps.  The descriptions below list the classes that Spokes team members will be co-teaching with each other; we will combine and reformat the classes as appropriate for each environment we are in.

WORKSHOPS:

Game Theory
During the mathematics workshops, students will be exposed to different concepts of game theory and asked to analyze the games that they play. Different topics will be introduced as games and students will have the opportunity to play against each other or even challenge the lecturer if time allows. Through hands-on exploration, students will be able to devise winning strategies for games such as nim, chomp, and tic-tac-toe.Students will also be exposed to more complicated logic games, including the classic prisoner’s dilemma, and craft simulations to show the outcomes.

This workshop will serve to encourage students to think critically and logically about mathematical concepts without experiencing the negative connotations that the word “math” often carries for them in school.

Laser Music Transmitter
How does a music player work? By using a laser pointer to transmit from a radio or mp3 player to a speaker students can learn about sound, electromagnetic waves, solar power and circuits.  Students will work through the project, learning the theory behind each part as they build it, and will end the workshop with their own wireless speaker. Possible extensions to the project include building a volume control, treble and bass controls, or even building an AM radio from basic circuit components.

Estimation Physics
How much energy do you output when you bicycle for an hour? How much power does a human output through heat? When watching a crazy stunt in a movie, do you wonder is that really possible? How could you check it?

Students will work with basic force balance and dimensional analysis to learn how to estimate things relevant to their daily lives. Emphasis will be placed on the process – asking the relevant questions, breaking the problem down into parts, making comparisons and order of magnitude estimates.  The goal of this workshop is to inspire kids to question the world around them and come up with reasonable answers for their inquiries, noting that it's important students realize there is often no correct answer to problems, only best estimates based on whatever resources are available.  Exploration requires no special skills or materials, just the ability to think divergently and use what is available.

Computer Thinking
This workshop will show students how they use algorithms and efficiency thinking in their everyday lives. Students will script dances to learn how their computers follow directions and race to count jellybeans as an example of how using the right algorithm can make even the simplest job faster and easier.  Using BeagleBone computers provided by our sponsor Texas Instruments, we will also expose students to the hardware driving the technologies that are hidden away inside the things they use every day.

We hope that students will come away with an understanding of the power of machine processing and efficient design, and how computer science can help them apply these things to their daily lives even without formal training. We also hopes students will come away aware that humans, not magic or anything else, are behind the creation of powerful and amazing technologies and that they can become the ones changing the world by studying STEM.

Bicycle History and Tinkering
Bicycles are recognizable around the world, and are relatively simple at first glance.  But bicycles have a surprisingly interesting history, and have undergone many evolutions of engineering and design since they were first invented.  From pneumatic tires to ball bearings, and paved roads to women’s suffrage, bicycles have had a huge impact on engineering as a whole, and on the freedom of movement of a large number of people.

While exploring the history and development of the bicycle, we’ll take apart the parts of a bike to explore how they work, learn the different systems of a bike, and talk about bicycle repair.  We’ll also brainstorm how we could use those same parts in other engineering projects, look at some fun examples of bicycles and pedal-powered machines throughout history, and talk about how bicycles might help modern social movements and freedom of movement.  Time permitting, we may even build our own bicycle blender, or brainstorm our own pedal-powered machines or bicycle modifications!

Manufacturing
Manufacturing processes are critical to our economy, but most students never learn about how factories are run. Teams of students will compete in several races to build the most copies of an item within a time limit. With each race, students can change the amount of buffer they have between machines, the size of their inventories, and the location of their quality controllers to try to improve their productivity and product quality. By introducing students to different lean manufacturing strategies and encouraging them to apply them to speeding up their teams' productivity, the students will become familiar with the basic concepts of lean manufacturing that are used in most industries today.

Design Thinking
'Design thinking' has generated a lot of press of late; the process of empathizing, defining a problem, asking the right questions, prototyping and reiterating, is one that exists in arguably any field. Through some friendly (and perhaps not-so-friendly) competition, we will challenge students to apply the design thinking process to problems and processes around them. The workshops will change based on a school's context and where the students want to do with the exercise, but at the end of the day, we hope that students will begin to understand how to explore the heart of a problem and craft solutions!

INFORMAL ACTIVITIES:

Informal Activities (Outside the Workshops)
In addition to these official activities for students, we look to engage the broader communities we stay with through informal activities outside of the classroom. To help develop a community of tinkerers, we plan to use public spaces to demo creative and sustainable engineering projects, such as a bicycle-powered smoothie blender, explorations of energy generation through shake-powered flashlights and DIY wind turbines, and group brainstorming.  These projects are easy to engage with briefly or more in depth, and will hopefully generate interest in tinkering and exploring, showing that anyone can engage in their own explorations.  We hope to connect new or experienced tinkerers of any age with ideas, resources, and each other!
