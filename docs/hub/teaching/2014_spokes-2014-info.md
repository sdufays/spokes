What was the format at different locations?
Had a mix of super informal open table events at festivals and libraries. People would drop by the table. 5+ minute activities for people who wanted to stay for longer. EG have a bunch of bike parts and get to have informal conversations

Had some times when they would say every hour or every other hour they would hold a full workshop and invite people to come back to the table for that.

Also had full workshops. For example the homeschool group. That was much more formal. A lot of the locations ended up being unique/different.

EG - turn a blender into a bicycle-powered blender. Callie did an estimation workshop. Would could sit around and talk about estimation. Someone talked about computer science. Would walk around a space and see if they could find something to improve on.

Think that they ended up having people choose their own workshops. Choose 2 workshops. A lot of the places we sent the workshop proposals to first. So some places did a “pre-grouping” to assign people before we showed up.

For girl scouts, were much younger than expected so they split up and did a single topic for the whole day.

What important changes/adjustments did you make during the trip?
Didn’t have all the teaching locations down before leaving, so being flexible about format was critical because things got nailed down as they went.

They put together objectives at the beginning for many of the workshops. This was very hard if they were at libraries or festivals.

Big difference if the parents were also around and engaged. Students would stick around more if the parents did.

Very different engagement in different locations.

Being flexible with whether or not people stick around and having brief things in case people didn’t want to stick around.

Variable length activities.

Student Reaction
What was the optimal style of workshop?
Maddie liked set amounts of students for set amounts of time. When people decide which of the topics they want to go to. Let them do more and had more engagement.

LIbraries were challenging because of lack of advertising & difficulty with attention. With exception of Loveland.

Other ones got a larger spectrum of people.

What was the optimal classroom size that you taught?
For classroom workshops, 30 people felt fine & manageable. 7 teachers, maybe 4 workshops at a time. ~5-8 people per workshops. Mostly single teachers. Sometimes other teachers would stop in to help.

What age group of students (K-5, 6-8, 9-12) worked best with your workshop? (Were the most attentive, learned the most, had the most fun, whatever you felt like “clicked”)
Had planned on middle to high school. Younger was a little challenging.
