What were the formats?
Started with a 7 member team. Only 4 of them reached DC at the end - 2 people had to leave early. Angela got a concusion and she decided to elave early. The 7 of them would host 6 workshops.

10am to 12pm - three workshops
20 minute break
2 hours in afternoon, another 3 workshops

People chose 1 workshop from each session.

A lot of the places did not have that many students - Maybe 15 total.

Vince’s workshop
Teaching some basic ideas in game theory
Wanted to convince them that monte hall was true in three ways
Did experimentations. Upped the number of doors. Tried to prove it.

What important changes/adjustments did you make during the trip?
Initially they built things for highschool, but ended up with a lot of people in 6th to 8th grade. Much more content. Rebuilt a lot of their workshops.

Reached out to a lot of libraries and high schools. Mostly heard back from libraries, not so many high schools. Most of their outreach was to middle schools.

What were the most valuable lessons that the students got from your workshop?
Did they learn something? Did they have fun? Are the going to continue working on this going forward?

Gave feedback forms. Vince will share.

Feedback largely depended on the locations. For the same workshop, the number of students really influenced things. Larger classes did much worse.

What changes were made to the workshops?
Most everyone had to change thigns a bit.
Disecting a sheep brain was the most popular one!
Peopel had to tone down the vocabulary to help students understand
For the monte hall workshop, they didn’t really even understand probablity. So they had to get through some of those basic ideas first. Were having some trouble with this.
About half way through, switched to graph theory. Math without calculations. Draw a figure without lifting up the pen. Students were much more excited abotu this.
There were a lot of students who were fearful about math. It was more visual. More gamey.
