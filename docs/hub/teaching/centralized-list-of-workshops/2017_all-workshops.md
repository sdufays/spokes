| Physics Workshop | 2017.0 | MIT/Harvard | Caity Looby, Cody Winkleblack | I specifically taught the students about Simple Machines and the 3 classes of levers. After identifying different types of simple machines and levers that they might see in every day life, I touched on the idea of mechanical advantage. For the older students, I would talk about force and distance and how work = force*distance in order to get conservation of work within the system. After this, I would give the students about 20 minutes to use any of the resources provided (straws, craft sticks, D batteries for weights, string, rulers, tape, hot glue, ketchup cups, pencils, pompoms, rubber bands) to draw out a design and actually build it.  I would explain to them that engineers always draw out a good design with labels before building it and then going back to their sketches afterwards to compare. As the students were building, I would go around and ask them what type of lever they were building and where the fulcrum, load, and effort were. After time was up, they would have a competition to see whose catapult could shoot the mini pompoms the farthest. After the distance challenge, we had a range challenge in which I would give them some range (varied depending on how far the original catapults could go, but was usually in between the farthest and closest distances), in which they had to try to make it between. Prizes of candy were awarded to the farthest distance from round 1 and the ones who made it in the range for round 2. After this, Cody would teach about E&M and how to build a motor (he can elaborate more on this). Sometimes, at the end if we had extra time, we would have a catapult war where the students would line up on each side with a barrier and have to try to shoot the pom poms across the barrier with different challenges (sometimes red ones were worth more points or the larger ones were worth more points). | My original goals for the students were geared towards high school students who had already seen multiplication and algebra. Because many of the students were elementary and middle school age (with a few high schoolers, some of which had also not seen algebra yet), my focus was for them to have fun and enjoy doing things in physics. I was happy if they learned where the load, effort and fulcrum were on their catapult and could identify the three classes of levers. I also wanted to emphasize to them that they could see physics concepts all the time in their every day life and that it wasn't some esoteric idea. | Things needed to build catapults and motors: straws, craft sticks, D batteries for weights, string, rulers, tape, hot glue, ketchup cups, pencils, pompoms, rubber bands, AA batteries, insulated copper wire, neodymium magnets. |   |   |   |   |
