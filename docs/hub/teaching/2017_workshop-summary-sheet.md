How To Structure a Workshop
Most workshops are structured around some fun excercise or project. They generally start with short lectures, games, or projects that introduce the basic ideas that will be discussed. Then they move on to a main project. They often end with a creative period where students can think up their own unique twist to the project, ask their burning questions about science, technology, or the world, or start a new project using the resources available.

In general, when designing a workshop, you might want to:
Define learning goals. What do you want students to gain over the course of your workshop?
Choose a project that fits your interests and learning goals
Outline the most import 3 or 5 ideas or words a student needs to understand in order to start your project
Plan hands-on games, mini-projects, or very short lectures to teach these concepts
Outline how you will guide students through the project
Plan an exciting way to end the project. A competition? A game? A brainstorm?

Computer Science - 2013
Over the course of the Spokes trip, I became skeptical of the effectiveness of the lecture format, and the class became more open-ended.

**Every time we visited students, many of them begged to learn about how to make their own video games.**

Unfortunately, we did not have a nice way to get larger groups kids making video games in under an hour, but in contexts that allowed for it (e.g. when we were working with the smaller homeschooler group outside of Kansas City), we started piloting a more interactive tutorial-style workshop where we created video games from scratch, based on whatever the students wanted to make!

Many of the younger students wanted to start by making clones of their favorite games (e.g. Minecraft, or a platformer game).

There are some great open source online materials for building Minecraft clones, and for more generic 2d games, I found a really pleasant Lua library, which is a great language for first-time programmers:

LÖVE 2D Lua Game engine:
https://love2d.org

Webcraft (Minecraft Clone in the browser using JavaScript):
https://github.com/Overv/WebCraft

Teamed up with Turner Bohlen on these smaller interactive videogame-making workshops. These were pretty successful, just hard to scale to a bigger group of students!
