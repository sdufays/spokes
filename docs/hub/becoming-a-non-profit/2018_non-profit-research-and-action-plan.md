Initial research

Forming a 501c3 in MA:
2-step process
“To create a 501(c)(3) tax-exempt organization, first you need to form a Massachusetts nonprofit corporation. Then you apply for tax-exempt status from the IRS and the state of Massachusetts.”
2-12 month process for IRS
Pay more → can expedite the process
Some good reasons to incorporate:
Limits on Personal Liability: Any agreements Spokes would enter as a corporation limit the liability to the corporation - if Spokes were to go bankrupt, the individual employees (namely, us) would not be held liable for that debt
Tax treatment: non-profits have favorable tax treatments
State to register in:
Legal Shield recommends Nevada or Delaware because they are “corporate havens”
Delaware:
If locate in DE but don’t operate, DE does NOT charge corporate income tax
This probably isn’t a problem for us either way since we’d be a non-profit, but does non-profit mean no federal OR state tax, or just state tax?
Purchases in DE are not taxed and income tax is a flat rate
Delaware Economic Development Office also has a lot of resources for small businesses
Nevada
No corporate income tax, no inventory tax
Also has a lot of resources for small businesses
Non-profits may be eligible for tax breaks/incentives in Nevada as well
Initial documents
Terminology:
Required: mandated by state law
Needed: good to have to prevent future problems/lawsuits
Registered agent: the entity authorized to accept delivery of legal documents on the corporation’s behalf
Need to file annual or biannual reports depending on the state
Required:
Certificate of incorporation (like a birth certificate for the corp)
Requires the corp to file Articles of Incorporation with the state (and fees)
Should be kept with the registered agent
Articles of Incorporation are the basics of the business, i.e.:
Name
Purpose
Name and address of registered agent
Stock information
# of shares the corp can issue
Designation of share classes
Initial value of each share
Identify the people responsible for signing and filing the Articles
State the number and names of the first directors
Most states have a standard A of I form that you fill out
Can elect to have special provisions, but consult a professional when considering them
Needed:
Bylaws/operating agreement
Probably most important doc for an LLC
There are templates available
Describe how the corporate office functions
How shareholders’ and directors’ meetings are called and conducted
Shareholder voting
Director qualifications
Board committee functions
Procedures for/limits on issuing/transferring shares
Provisions to protect the directors, officers, etc
Clause stating that bylaws can be enforced even if one provision is held invalid
Must be consistent with A of I - if not, A of I takes precedence
Can amend as necessary - should state the necessary procedures for amendment
Meeting minutes
Every corporation has to have an annual meeting for shareholders and directors
Can also call special meetings
Minutes are the official record for those meetings
Should remain light - only include key information, not lots of detail
Committee charters
Bylaws for specific committees, if your corp has them
Most corps only need Board of Directors
But some have federally mandated committees like litigation or audit committees
Code of Ethics/Conduct
Ethics code: shareholders’ expectations of the corp and officers
Code of conduct: the corps expectations for employees
Not necessary for smaller corps where shareholders, directors and officers are synonymous
Behaving like an entity
Forming the entity just creates an empty legal shell; the owners need to transfer the activity to the entity
If you don’t, can be ignored by courts or taxing authorities
“Alter ego” could apply where an individual is held accountable for debts incurred by the corporation if they think you are using the corporation as a cover for your personal activities
Basically once we form the 501c3 we need to start using it properly so that its not perceived as a front
Adopting a DBA
DBA: Doing Business As
I.e. it’s a trade name, the name that you use to conduct business for your corporation - in our case: Spokes
Legal name for a business with sole proprietor is the owner’s personal name, or the surnames of the partners for a partnership
DBA allows you to conduct business under this other name
Many states need you to register with local/state gov to use the name
Reasons why you want it:
Opening a business bank account with the business name
(other reasons if you’re a franchise, doesn’t apply to us)
How to get it:
First make sure name isn’t already being used
Fill out a form with Sec of State or County Clerk
Pay nominal application fee
NOTE: DBAs have expiration dates, so you need to renew!
Limitations:
DBA is not a trademark - don’t have protections for it under the DBA filing
DBA does not shield you from infringing on another company’s trademark - especially from unregistered trademarks which are not on file with the government
Make sure we have checked that no one else has used Spokes to avoid legal battles
Can be a good idea to trademark the name if you want…
Registered agent
The person the state goes to when it wants to talk to a corporation
Responsible for fulfilling legal obligations between the state and the business
I.e. if state has to give notice to a business, the registered agent is the person who is given notice for the corporation
Person acting as the intermediary for the corporate entity
More important if you are incorporated in a different state from where you do business (i.e. inc in DE, work in MA)
Paperwork goes to this person, and is point of contact for legal actions
Registered agent has to:
forward docs/notices from the state to the corporation
Report on entity’s standing with the state (i.e. Good Standing)
If you don’t have one, could lose legal status, pay penalties
The registered agent must be a resident of the state with a physical address where someone will be available during regular business hours
Most businesses use a third party to be the registered agent
Can be another business (which has authorization to do business in the state that you’re registered in)
Can be a corporate officer or director, lawyer or CPA
Advantage because then the business can move without doing additional paperwork of an address change for the registered agent
The third part can also keep up with new requirements/legal changes in the state for the business
Maintains good corporate image because a legal action will never be served at the business office, it will be served at the third party registered agent’s location
Corporate governance
Can be simple for a small corp, includes just:
Articles of incorporation
Initial meeting
Elect corporate officers, decide on corporate bylaws
Have annual meetings for directors and an annual meeting for shareholders
Directors meeting:
Plans for the business
Decisions on the future of the business
Shareholders meeting:
Must be announced in advance - state has rules for notification
Update on the business; previous, ongoing and future activities
Elect new directors as necessary
Meetings must have minutes, including:
Time and place
Attendance
Agenda items
Votes
Annual report filed with state that updates or confirms the information from the certificate of incorporation accompanied by a fee
LLC vs nonprofit
LLC is different from a corporation - its more flexible, has less paperwork
Although most nonprofits are NOT LLCs, some states allow nonprofit LLCs
Texas
California
Tennessee
Michigan
Minnesota
BUT if you form a non-profit LLC, the IRS will not give it tax-exempt status unless all the members are themselves tax-exempt organizations
So we probably don’t want to do this
Local registration
Varies widely from state to state so the best thing to do is to contact local government and ask them what licenses/permits we need to obtain for Spokes
Can also review: https://www.sba.gov/
Legal entity vs tax entity
Legal entity is LLC or corporation
Tax entity is sole proprietor/partnership vs C-corp vs S-corp
Tax entity: how the IRS sees the business
Legal entity: how everyone else sees the business
Corporations can choose to be either C-corp or S-Corp
C-Corp: Double taxation
Profits taxed at corporate tax rate
Then dividends to shareholders are taxed at individual tax rate
S-Corp: pass-through taxation
No corporate tax, but has additional requirements to qualify for S-Corp
Owners of a corporation are the shareholders
C-corps can create unique stock class structure so that they can distribute ownership stakes to members regardless of capital contribution to the corporation, i.e. everyone can have an equal share of the profits
S-Corps are not allowed to have a unique structure: must have a single class of stocks with dividends distributed in proportion to the capital investment of the shareholders
Corporations have:
Board of Directors: management responsibilities
Corporate officers: day to day opersations
Shareholders: owners of the corp, separated from daily business decisions except for major corporate decisions - only power is to elect directors
Shareholders may be elected as directors or appointed as officers
Business name vs trademark
Business name is easy to get, just done with secretary of state
Trademark can take 6+ months and is registered with federal government
Business name is only protected in the state where it’s registered
Trademark is exclusive property
Apply with US Patent and Trademark Office
6+ month exam period
Then 30 day waiting period where your mark can be challenged
Then trademark is registered
Need federal tax ID number (or employer identification number, EIN)
Need this to legally conduct business
Easy to get from IRS:
Fill out Form SS-4 and submit to IRS
Fill out online applicaiton (in one session)
Third party may also apply for you
Location to register
If you incorporate in a different state from where you do business, need to make sure you are not considered as a foreign corporation
Things to consider
How are corporations taxed there?
Are additional taxes applied to foreign corps?
Is there an income tax on various entity types, or a minimum tax/franchise tax?
Does the cost of qualifying as a foreign corporation outweight the financial advantages?
What are the total taxes that you expect to pay?
Delaware’s advantages include:
Flexible business law
Court of Chancery where judges (not juries) focus exclusively on business law
No state corporate income tax for companies formed in the state that do not transact business there (Note: there is a franchise tax)
Favorable tax requirements for dealing with complex capitalization structures or high volumes of shares of stock
Non-residents pay no personal income tax
Non-residents can still be shareholders, directors, or officers of a corporation, or members or managers of an LLC
No state taxes for stock shares owned by non-residents
Nevada’s advantages include:
No state corporate income tax, and no fees on corporate shares
No personal income tax or franchise tax for corporations or LLCs
Non-residents can still be shareholders, directors, or officers of a corporation, or members or managers of an LLC
NOTE: IF YOU INCORPORATE IN DE OR NV FROM ANOTHER STATE, YOU WILL STILL NEED TO QUALIFY AS A FOREIGN CORPORATION IN ALL THE STATES WHERE YOU DO BUSINESS!!!
Generally recommend to start by registering in the state where you will do business, because it can be difficult to arrange for the registered agent in another state
If you are using a service provider to incorporate, you can ask the provider to be the registered agent
Can also hire an independent one
If you incorporate in the state you do business, you can:
Have your own corporation be the registered agent - if the state allows it
Some states require a third party
A corporate officer/director/manager/member may be the registered agent - all states allow this
They must reside in the state
