Hi Sandi,

Long time no see! I hope this e-mail finds you well and enjoying the start to a new school year! How are you doing? How is your family?

I just wanted to start off by saying thank you, Sandi, for everything you have done for Spokes, ever since we first reached out to you in 2014. I know that I felt much better about cycling across the country knowing that you were with us every step of the way. And I’ve learned from other Spokes team members that you have continued to go above and beyond in supporting the project for over 3 years now. I know that the continuation of Spokes would probably not have been possible without your help. I also know that you personally have put a lot of time and effort into supporting and guiding Spokes teams, and that you have done far more for Spokes than the Edgerton Center does for most student teams. Thank you so much for all of your time and effort, Sandi, we really appreciate it.

I wanted to reach out to you because I know that Thi and Caity recently stopped by to tell you about the new Spokes Hub. I am also part of the team and I just wanted to go over what Spokes Hub is and what our goals are, because I know how much you are doing for Spokes right now and I think it is critical that we work with you going forwards. Currently, the Spokes Hub is made up of Spokes alumni: Turner Bohlen, Drew Bent, Jamin Liu, Madeline Hickman, Thi Bui, Caity Looby, Francesca Childs and Chloe Yang.

The Spokes Hub is working to become a non-profit corporation in the US that can provide educational, logistical, financial and legal support to future Spokes teams that are formed at different universities across the country. We envision that the Spokes Hub will create continuity from year to year as it provides a backbone of support that will allow individual Spokes teams to focus on developing amazing workshops and reaching out to underrepresented areas in the US. In the short term, our plan is to first establish an official non-profit organization in the next 6-12 months. At first, we will offer support on developing workshops, connecting with communities that Spokes teams will visit, and provide advice on all the logistical tasks related to running a Spokes team. In the next 2-3 years we would hope to be able to add more support capabilities, including financial support (such as procurement cards and a bank account to store funds that the teams had raised) and legal support (such as waivers for students and Spokes instructors, insurance for rental cars).

This is a new journey for the Spokes team, and we want to keep you informed from the beginning. We are aware that making this transition will have a lot of implications for you, the Edgerton Center and for MIT, and we want to discuss that with you so that we can find a solution that meets everyone’s needs. We are also new to the non-profit world and all the work it entails, so we welcome any advice or suggestions you would share with us. We are also working with lawyers for legal advice on establishing the non-profit. I want to emphasize how grateful we are for everything you have done for us over the past 3+ years and for your continued support as we start to ramp up the 2018 teams. We think this is the right move to make because Spokes has started to grow beyond MIT and we think that with a formal non-profit in place, we will be able to provide the same support that the MIT Spokes teams are fortunate to receive from the Edgerton Center to all teams across the country. We think that creating a dedicated non-profit is the best way to develop and expand the Spokes project so that any university can form a team and so that all teams can focus on creating excellent educational content for those who need it the most.

As a next step, we would love to hear your thoughts on all this. Would you be willing to join a conference call with me, Turner Bohlen (the Spokes founder) and other members of the Spokes Hub project? Please let us know when you are available in the next couple of weeks and we will set something up!

Thank you so much Sandi, and have a great week,
Emma
