Exercises for choosing central Spokes ideas (in Vision Brainstorming doc)
Popular: hands on, self-directed, everyone is a learner, project-based, peer-to-peer
STEM focus? Is expanding too broad? There is also value in teaching “STEM” because it is a hot topic/buzz word/will help with fundraising and support
Emma: Could start with STEM at first and expand later, could combine STEM and other topics--STEM and music/art, etc
Turner: By saying Spokes is STEM focused, delivers a narrow set of skills as opposed to teaching students how to acquire any skills they need for any ideas they want to pursue
How do we present “STEM” and “non-STEM” subjects/workshops to the public?
Francesca: Can create a new language of critical thinking and interdisciplinary thinking
Emma: Can separate presentation given to students vs given to funders
Can frame STEM and non-STEM in such a way that breaks down divide between technical and “non-technical” disciplines, more interdisciplinary perspective
Priorities exercise (also in Vision Brainstorming doc). Disagreements discussed below:
Biking vs something broader vs no emphasis on biking?
Arguments for biking alone:
Drew: Easier for others to grasp Spokes goals/impact by having a single focus, easier to understand the mission
Emma: Biking aspect attracts a certain kind of person desirable for Spokes (adventurous, likes community involvement, etc)
Chloe: Never thought about it as one over the other. “Heres the spokes mission. Here it is perfectly exhibited in this bike trip we have”
Turner: Harder to incorporate multiple examples of sustainable transportation without losing focus, but want to also make Spokes something more. Could start with biking as focus at first
How to balance biking and teaching?
Biking is a brand and a clear thing to focus on, but mission statement should be focused on education
Underserved vs all communities
Jamin: Underserved kids do not have access to programs that allow them to succeed, but Spokes aims to build a mindset for learning, and it’s not just underserved communities that do not have the necessary skills for self-directed learning
Francesca: Dangers in letting zip code dictate--should focus on areas that have less exposure/opportunities, but should not exclude other more “developed” communities because they still appreciate/need Spokes learning
Turner: Trying to develop a certain kind of thinking, and communities everywhere need this--how do we phrase this with respect to which communities we target?
Emma: Can try to expand the communities Spokes reaches beyond “low hanging fruit”
Drew: Spokes should focus more on diversity of communities, cross-pollinating perspectives and backgrounds
