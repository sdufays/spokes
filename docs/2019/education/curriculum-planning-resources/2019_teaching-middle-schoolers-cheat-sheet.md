**Short version:** keep things short and interesting; be encouraging, not condescending; lots of activities; enjoy and SMILE.

- Short attention span: "pop song rule" - never lecture more than 3 minutes without a question or participatory action ("raise your hand if...").
- They like to talk: ask discussion questions with no right answer.
- Maturity varies; do not treat them as children; give respect; sarcasm doesn't work.
- Think concretely: use "scale of 1 to 5" questions; give concrete tasks inside abstract questions.
- Encourage and thank them for risks; don't single kids out in front of class (ask a general question or talk during a break).
- Relate but don't pretend to be one of them: be a role model.
- Pre-plan vocabulary; avoid unfamiliar symbols; check "have you heard this term?".
- Assign groups (never let them choose; fear of social isolation).
- College-style lecture is the wrong model; use "royal we", facilitate; get kids out of chairs.
- Park tangents: "I appreciate your enthusiasm, but let's save that for the end."

**Knowledge baseline (Massachusetts public schools):**
- Starting 7th grade: know negative numbers, ratios, fractions, prime factorization, one-variable equations, coordinate plane, area/volume/pi, mean/median; NOT functions, lines, quadratics, logs, trig. Science: atoms (p/n/e), seasons, rock cycle, water cycle, solar system; NOT force/momentum/energy, wavelength, pH, balancing equations. Life science: classification, plant anatomy, body systems, ecosystems; NOT DNA->RNA->protein, Punnett squares.
- Starting 8th grade: percents/interest, unit conversion, probability, random sampling; tectonic plates; cell organelles; NOT functions/lines, mass vs weight, pH.
