Source: Massachusetts Public Schools Curriculum Benchmarks via MIT ESP. Condensed:
- **Starting 7th:** know negative numbers, ratios, algebraic expressions, one-variable equations, graphing points, areas/volumes, statistics; atoms, seasons, rock cycle, water cycle; classification, body systems, ecosystems. Not: functions, lines, quadratics, logs, trig, force/energy, pH, DNA->protein.
- **Starting 8th:** + percents/interest, unit conversion, probability, random sampling; tectonic plates; organelles. Not: functions/lines until grade 8, wavelength, mass vs weight, pH.
- **Starting 9th:** irrational numbers, roots, equations of lines, functions (y=), congruence/similarity, Pythagorean theorem, scatterplots; genes/chromosomes, body systems, evolution. Not: trig, logs, e, kinetic theory, force/momentum.
- **10th:** Algebra I (many Geometry; a few Algebra II/trig). **11th:** Geometry/Algebra II (a few precalculus); know kinetic theory, wavelength, formulas/names, acids/bases, activation energy. **12th:** Algebra II/trig, many precalculus, a few calculus; know intro physics (force, momentum, energy).
- AP students: AP Physics B know potential vs kinetic energy (not derivatives/integrals); AP Calc/Physics C know derivatives, integrals, vector fields, Taylor series (not partial derivatives, flux, del).
- Notes: Newtonian physics takes long to sink in; most students who have seen e and complex numbers still don't know why e^(i*pi) = -1.
