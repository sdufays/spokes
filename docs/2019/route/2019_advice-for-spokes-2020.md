## Packing/Gear
1. Make a packing list; only 2 bike outfits (jersey + shorts); non-bike clothes minimal (2 outfits, one you can teach in). Most brought one large backpack or duffel; did lots of laundry.
2. Order bikes ASAP and make sure they're good: 2019 got bikes ~1 month before leaving; one rider only put clips on the day before. Do more training rides.
3. Jerseys a size bigger (they run small and shrink).
4. Get waterproof bike lights.
5. Pack light: van space is limited.

## Learning Festivals
1. Don't schedule on major holidays or Sundays (religious areas).
2. Be creative with workshop ideas; best topics appeal to many ages, are new to most kids or easy to advance (e.g. repairing a bike to add an LED, Scratch coding).
3. Do a practice LF before leaving Boston.

## Route / places to stay
1. Warmshowers: "a beautiful app, 10/10". Free; usually food and showers; often cyclists with nice houses; often sleep indoors.
2. Camping: fun but costs money and rain makes packing tents miserable; do sometimes.
3. Motels: available anywhere; 8 people in one room is possible (be sneaky, pay the fee if caught).
4. Churches: many don't respond, esp. with short notice; cast a wide net; usually no showers; a church may email its congregation to find house hosts; ask early.
5. Fire stations/police departments: call and ask to stay; they may suggest free places to pitch a tent, such as public parks.
6. Plan well in advance but not perfectly; places can be found on short notice if needed.

## Biking
1. Sore butt: double bike shorts, tilt seats down/forward, use Chamois Butt'r liberally.
2. You're only as fast as the slowest; wait at hilltops, especially early in the trip.
3. Groups of 2-4 are safest (help with flats, cars can pass).
4. Utah and Nevada are hot and dry: carry lots of water, extra jugs in the car.
5. For very long days (e.g. 120-mile day in Nevada) split with breaks: 55, then 35, then 30 miles.

## Budgeting
1. Keep a comprehensive budget and save receipts (photograph them immediately).
2. Start fundraising early: many MIT departments finalize budgets early and missing the window means no money. Apply to as many grants as possible (including ones 2019 didn't); do crowdfunding.

## Random
- Get alone time. Bring quarters for laundry (keep rolls in the car).
- Keep accessible in the car: medkit; bag with sunscreen, Chamois Butt'r, bug spray; bar bag (lots of Clif Bars).
- Driver rotation: one driver morning, one afternoon; driver gets groceries and a break from talking.
- Write thank-you notes for hosts. Split daily tasks (pack van, thank-you notes).
- Some Utah grocery stores don't accept Visa (MIT travel cards).
- Middle of Nevada: towns are tiny with no real groceries; stock up on food and propane in Ely at the latest; Eureka and Austin have next to nothing.
