Describe the need that you and your community partner will address and explain why it's significant. As specifically as possible, describe the impact you intend your project to have on the community. How will the community be different because of your work? What are the sustainable benefits of your project? Who will benefit?
Today the US is struggling to produce enough skilled scientists and engineers who can innovate, design and maintain the technical systems in industry that form the backbone of our economy. The goal of Spokes is to generate more interest in STEM education among students at an early age so that they will pursue degrees in STEM when they graduate from high school. Specifically, we would consider our project a success if we even changed the trajectory of one student per school so that they discovered a new interest in STEM subjects. In the long term, we will build an online community of students who all participated in our learning festivals; we will post challenges on our website that build on the skills we taught in our workshops, and we will post students’ responses in a gallery that they can use to see each other’s work. We will also be exploring the possibility of creating after-school clubs that do creative, student-driven STEM projects throughout the year and culminate in visits from the Spokes team during the summer. Our goal is to benefit both the students we interact with in our learning festivals, and the larger community of students at the school who hear about our work and are excited by it. We also hope to help all the teachers we collaborate with by connecting them together so that they can share teaching strategies and ideas.

Work plan (1-2 pages)
Essentially, this is your plan of attack. Imagine you are describing your work plan to someone who needs to implement it without you. What are your goals? What steps will you need to take and in what sequence in order to accomplish those goals? What is the timeline for this work? 
Our key goal is to reach at least 400 middle school students across the US during the course of the trip, and to visit between 10 to 12 schools (or other education institutions). We want students to perceive STEM subjects as exciting fields of study that require creative problem solving and curiosity. We also want to build lasting relationships with the students, teachers and institutions that we visit so that we can continue to teach and influence the students we meet after we leave. 
Our complete work breakdown structure is included in the appendices, but to summarize, we have four key phases of preparation and delivery. First, we need to aggressively fundraise because our total budget is on the order of $40,000 (our complete budget is also included as an appendix). Next we will need to spend the money on travel costs, equipment, bicycles, First Aid training, bicycle and automobile repair training and background checks. Third we will need to plan each learning festival with our partners in advance, and prepare our detailed lesson plans according to their needs and preferences; we will also lay out the route we will follow and begin contacting hosts and campsites where we will stay. Finally, we will execute the trip, following a set of standard operating procedures (also included in the appendices), biking to each school and stopping for 3-5 days for each learning festival, and then continuing to communicate with our students through monthly challenges. 
For fundraising, we are currently applying to a series of grants at MIT, as well as talking to specific departments and groups within MIT to ask for funding. We are also asking professional contacts and alumni in technical companies to support our work through either donations in kind or monetary support, and we have a designation through our parent organization, MIT Academic Teaching Initiative, on the MIT Giving website so we can accept donations from friends and family. Our goal is to have raised $40,000 by June 6th, and our intermediate goal is to have raised $10,000 every three weeks. Because we do not expect to be awarded as much money as we apply for, we will be applying or asking for more money by each 3-week checkpoint than we need so that we can increase our chances of getting all the funding we need by June 6th. 
For purchasing equipment and supplies, we have prioritized our spending: we will use the money first to pay for background checks for all team members. We will then begin to purchase bicycles (we have an arrangement with a supplier where we only need to place a 25% down payment to place an order). After that we will need to buy flights (although we are asking for flight donations from JetBlue), and rent a car from Budget or Avis (they have rental and insurance agreements with MIT). We will use some money to pay for First Aid training and automobile and bike repair classes during this time as well. The rest of the money can be used to purchase supplies in the two weeks before the start of the trip, and to pay for the cost of food, gas and other incidental expenditures during the trip.
While we are fundraising and purchasing equipment, other members of the team will be working on preparing the curriculum and the route to follow. We have already contacted all of the schools that Spokes visited last year (please see the appendix for the complete list) and our goal is to have planned out dates and outlines for each learning festival before we leave for San Francisco on June 7th. We aim to have finalized our teaching plans by May 1st so that we can refine them during the month of May by teaching them to small audiences either on campus through ESP or in local area schools. We will also have set the route by April 11th and will have a complete list of potential places to stay prepared by June 7th. We will try to contact as many hosts as possible ahead of time - we have a list of hosts who invited the team to return this year, and we also will be reaching out through the MIT alumni network to find people willing to host us overnight. We will also find campgrounds and national parks along the way where we can camp. As part of the preparation for the trip, we will also be writing a complete set of standard operating procedures and emergency/contingency plans that we will show to the MIT Liability and Insurance offices. Please see the safety section for more information, and the appendices for our draft standard operating procedures and emergency/contingency plans. 
During the trip we will be spending approximately 10 weeks on the road, beginning on June 9th and ending on August 15th. We will be biking between 60 and 100 miles per day. We will stop to teach at 10 to 12 institutions, spending 3-5 days in each location to set up, deliver the learning festival, and rest.

We will also keep records of all team members’ health insurance information and relevant health conditions. All team members will get First Aid training and bike repair training. On our trip every team member will bike, and we will rotate as driver for the support vehicle. We will be camping or staying with hosts, and we will be teaching in classrooms and other educational institutions. Therefore, our main safety concerns can be broken down into four categories: cyclist safety on the road, driver and vehicle safety on the road, team safety at stops and rest areas, and safety in the classroom. 
At the beginning of each riding day the team will review the route as a group, and locate the nearest two emergency care facilities so that we know where to take teammates in an emergency. All cyclists will carry small first aid kits and repair kits for their bicycles, and two riders (at the front and rear of the group) will carry larger survival kits. The driver will carry more survival and medical gear and repair equipment as well. Each rider will carry at least 4 liters of water and sunscreen, as well as a charged cellphone for emergencies, with emergency contact numbers programmed into their phone. We will also have a AAA membership so that if we have problems with the car we can call for help; since the car will be rented, we can also ask for support from the rental company. As described in the emergency procedures, the team member closest to an accident and able to make a call will first call for emergency services and then will call me. As the team leader, I will be the point of contact responsible for communicating any problems to the authorities and MIT in a timely manner.
