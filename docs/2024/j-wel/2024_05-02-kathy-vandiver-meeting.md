Attendance: Rebecca, Hank, Kathy Vandiver (works at MIT Center for Environmental Health Sciences / Superfund Research Center funded by NIH; translates research to public; Harvard Ed School grad; taught 6th grade science many years).

- **COUHES** (MIT human-subjects review): not enough time to get full approval; can't use data in a publication. Kathy has COUHES approval (look under COUHES online). Framing: "educational survey not different from what people do in a normal school day". J-WEL wants a final report and doesn't seem to need COUHES.
- **Survey advice**: ask "2 things you learned"; categorize answers; key question = did this increase interest in learning more about the topic? No gender demographics needed, but age is good. Use little emoji scales. Kids rarely have ideas to improve activities; high schoolers will. Qualitative feedback is valued. Kathy got her survey model at a museum.
- Misc: kids are confused by subscripts in chemical formulas.
- Materials Kathy has: ocean acidification; air chemistry and pollution. Teacher training/setup PDF: edgerton.mit.edu Understanding Air Setup Instructions 2024; ocean molecule set: edgerton.mit.edu/molecule-sets/understanding-oceans. Also Edgerton DNA and proteins activity.
- Keep learning objectives in mind as you design activities.
