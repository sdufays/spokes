## Overview
**Title**: Bridging STEM Education Gaps: Fostering Aspirations through Learning Festivals and Deployable Learning Toolkits in Underserved American Communities. Short title: Bridging STEM Education Gaps through Learning Festivals.
**Abstract (75 words)**: MIT Spokes aims to inspire underserved communities to pursue STEM education through interactive, individualized learning festivals during a cross-country cycling tour; provide learning kits and instructional materials; design surveys with expert guidance to assess short and long term benefits of STEM experiential learning.

## Context
- **Need**: rural/low-income communities left behind (school funding via property taxes; schools lack funds for experiential learning); incarcerated youth receive less instructional time.
- **Evidence**: 2021 share of younger adults with bachelor's degree 40% urban vs 22% rural (USDA); STEM disparity (Saw and Agger, 2021); STEM jobs +20% 2011-2021, STEM workers earn 60% more; correctional education reduces recidivism by 43% (US DOJ).
- **Prior research**: post-secondary STEM enrollment is driven by aspiration and preparation (Blau and Duncan 1967); best programs for interest are summer camps, internships, distance learning (Harris and Hodges 2018).
- **Prior work**: guidance from Dr. Kathleen Vandiver on survey design (her chapter in "Environmental Health Literacy").
- **Prior J-WEL funding**: none. Fundraising from MIT departments, alumni clubs, crowdfunding.

## Past work
Spokes has run as a student org since **2013**; students recruit and plan in the fall, fundraise/organize through winter and spring; trip about 10 weeks, 8-10 learning festivals, hundreds of students reached each trip. Funding sources historically: MIT organizations/departments, small grants such as the **MIT Baker Foundation**, crowdfunding. Past activities: AI, electrical engineering, physics of music, chromatography and color, decoding DNA (gel electrophoresis). Findings so far informal/undocumented; new in 2024: learning toolkits, formal surveys.

## Outputs and outcomes
- **Deliverables by June 2025**: digital learning toolkits (setup, materials list, teaching steps, continued-learning resources), customizable by age/education/interest; one-day learning festivals e.g. at Washington D.C. youth detention facility and the Challenger Center in Hazard, Kentucky (town of 5,000). "Gateway topics" (e.g., 3D printing leads to CAD). Include surveys and non-academic programming such as dinners to build comfort.
- **Data**: pre-festival survey (motivations, prior exposure), immediate post-survey, follow-up months later; contact community centers 3-6 months later about toolkit use (informed by Idema and Patrick 2019).
- **Beneficiaries**: K-12 teachers, camps, events in under-resourced settings. **Dissemination**: toolkits published on the Spokes website with survey results; partner with MIT education-outreach labs; ask J-WEL to leverage non-profit/K-12 connections.

## Budget narrative
No-cost festivals for partners; goal is cost-neutral for team members. About 70 days of cycling. **Bikes/gear for 8 people: $16,000. One-way van rental DC to SF: $6,000** (avoid two-way rental so members do not have to drive it back); gas extra; plane tickets back to Boston subsidized if needed. **Lodging (camping, hosts via paid platforms, other): $6,000. LF equipment/materials: $3,500.** LFs at schools, community centers and prisons, up to 60 students each.

Application components required by J-WEL: 4-page Excel workplan/team/budget template, budget narrative, **letter of recommendation from department chair** (second letter in some cases). Due Fri 3/15/2024 11:59pm.
