(All advice is from the Spokes 2023 team unless noted; role owners in brackets.)

**Fundraising [Katherine, Audrey, Joy] (2023):** Start early and get creative. Contact MIT departments toward end of the fall semester because they may have extra funds left. Departments are far more likely to donate if someone on the team is in that department - make emails personal. Track in a spreadsheet; reach out to past donors.
**Crowdfunding [Joy, Andrew] (2023):** MIT crowdfunding (https://crowdfund.mit.edu/story/Spokesamerica) was set up a bit late but is a great tool, especially if you become a featured campaign on the front page; giving.mit.edu is less direct. Social media fundraiser: bike 1 mile for every $5. Andrew raised over $1K by reaching out to everyone he knows directly - do this.
**Budgeting [Audrey]:** You will need to fundraise around $30K (2023).
**Finances [Andrew, Joy]:** Talk to Marvelin and Sandi (Sandi is more in charge, Marvelin more responsive for day to day). Apply for a travel card and procard ASAP so nothing is out of pocket. Need access to the Spokes account (2530032) on Atlas; as of Nov 2023, $5,770.53 in the Spokes account (Edgerton was to transfer $5k; funds can be transferred via giving.mit.edu/search/node/spokes; Kelly Douglas grant money was also being transferred in). Payment routing: Sandi/Edgerton directly for giant orders (e.g., the single $13k bike purchase; Edgerton is invoiced); ProCard for non-travel items not on the Non-Allowed list (https://vpf.mit.edu/non-allowed-items-for-procard-purchases); everything else on the Travel Card. Using the correct card matters to avoid audits. MIT tax-free forms for Massachusetts are in a shared Drive folder.
**Edgerton Center [Joy]:** Sandi, Chris, and Marvelin are main contacts; they help buy bikes, rent the van, procard/travel card.
**Route planning [Duha, Vin]:** DC to SF is tradition but you have freedom (could go the other way for tailwinds). 2023 route spreadsheet exists. "Before choosing which towns to stay in, find housing first" (Warmshowers, alumni, hotels/campgrounds) - otherwise days of rerouting. No need for the week-long national park detour; balance getting to the end, real rest days, and sightseeing. 2023 squeezed into 71 days = long days and few rest days; **aim for 75+ days.** Day to day: Strava premium plus checking road conditions in Google Maps; 2 people had GPS bike computers, others used phones; Lucy recommends Komoot (upload planner's route for turn-by-turn). Vehicle lunches stopped when it got hot.
**Housing [Katherine, Duha, Lucy]:** Warmshowers: make a new account, mention the support car, start early, contact many people per location (most say no), show flexibility ("we can sleep on the floor"). Churches: great (spacious, kind, often cook dinner); call all the churches in the area a few days before. Campsites: book far in advance especially national parks (recreation.gov; Utah has its own state park site); can use travel card or reimburse; RV parks are also fine (hookups, showers). MIT alumni: search the database, contact groups especially in big cities; ask to join their monthly club-lead calls - you'll get donations that way.
**Bikes and gear [Andrew]:** Bikes from Landry's Bicycles; Edgerton pays the big order via invoice. 2023 bikes: Giant Revolt 2 gravel bikes (tubeless) and Liv Devote (women's equivalent); carry plug/patch equipment and a pump (manual and electric options).
**Other gear [Lucy; stored with Joy]:** REI quality but pricey in bulk; Walmart for cheaper. Practice setting up tents/rain covers (wrong = wet night). Everyone needs own sleeping pad and bag; tents are crowded (2023 ended up with everyone in a single). Communal cooking: two-burner camp stove, pots, utensils, plates. Several large plastic bins. Maintenance gear: hex wrenches, chain lube, tubeless sealant, chain tool.
**Car rental [Lucy]:** Chrysler Pacifica ("very trusty"); contact Chris at Edgerton; book as soon as possible (thousands of dollars difference). Recommend one-way rental, pick up in Boston not DC. AAA is good for emergency towing but rental company requires its own tow service for flats. Their rental had no spare tire. Read the contract. Rental company does not allow towing so no hitch for a rack; they used a trunk-mounted 3-bike rack.
**Social media [Joy, Audrey]:** Lots of TikToks and reels; took over @mitstudents Instagram for a week - several applicants first heard of Spokes that way.
**Website/Blog [Katherine]:** Wix; own mitspokes.com domain (pointed at 2023 site; migrate 2023 to mitspokes.com/2023 and use main domain for the new year; copy the previous site). Login spokes-2024@mit.edu (password redacted).
**Training [Andrew]:** Definitely do spring training rides, a few 50+ miles; MIT Cycling has routes; ride hills (Boston is flat); do the Boston marathon midnight ride. Less pain in first weeks.
**Learning Festivals [Audrey, Duha]:** 2023 LF list in the 2023 route spreadsheet (tab). Do more (~10), schedule was tight. Opportunity for an LF at a correctional facility in SF at the end of the trip - impactful. Contacts: laurel_beeler@cand.uscourts.gov and wyatt@acuriae.org. Teach own workshop or in pairs; schedule 1-2 months ahead; PRACTICE your LF during SPLASH or SPARK.
**Biking advice:** Group chat and share locations; lots of sunscreen/long sleeves/pants (breathable); some switched to Brooks leather saddles; everyone learn bike care - Bikes Not Bombs "bike school" (donation based) https://bikesnotbombs.org/bike-school/bike-school-jp/.
