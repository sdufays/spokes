**Plan (1 hour)**
- Intro (10 min): Scratch = site to make interactive stories/games with code; sprites with blocks run top to bottom; "when flag clicked" starts a program; copy example "when flag clicked, move 10 steps".
- Make the taco game (30 min): walk students through code on projector.
- Expand (10 min): obstacles, increasing difficulty, other sprites/backdrops.
- Share (10 min): split in two groups, each views the other's projects; show Scratch community and how to make an account (internet may not be possible at YSC).

Learning objective: programmatic thinking, step-by-step problem solving; goal is excitement to continue on their own.

**Alternate flow:** synchronous instruction with laptop on projector (~20 min: move/turn, looks/sound, events, loops, if touching mouse); free play (~10 min: make a story with broadcasting, or a run-away/chase game); variables demo (~10 min: change color after 5 clicks); free play (~20 min); show-and-tell. Creativity matters more than understanding variables.

**OctoStudio / OctoSparks variant:** 10 min intro (what Scratch is, relation to other languages; make cat travel in a square); coding cards for star-chasing game; 20 min help students through it; 10 min show on projector; 20 min expand.

**Questions raised:** Is OctoStudio more engaging than Scratch? Does it work on very cheap phones / how to acquire devices (plan: Scratch on leftover laptops from previous years)? Adapting to age groups (mostly middle school, first LF at a youth correctional facility with 16-20 year olds)? Keeping students on task?
**Feedback notes:** keep topic small and focused; start personalized with intros; incorporate cycling?; 15-min intro for hour-long session, 5-min intro if students never coded (get cat moving).
