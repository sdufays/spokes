# Spokes Learning Festival & Workshops Master Guide
Combines knowledge from multiple years of Spokes.

## Reaching Out to Learning Festivals

### Email template: reaching out to past locations (2026 example)
> Good afternoon, Judge Faruqui and YCS Office!
>
> I hope your spring is off to a wonderful start! My name is Tatiana Vassiliev, and I am a senior at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) in Cambridge, MA. **I am a member of MIT Spokes 2026, and I am reaching out on behalf of my team to coordinate hosting a Learning Festival with the Department of Corrections and Youth Corrections Service!**
>
> Spokes is a program which sends a team of 7 MIT students on an 80-day biking trip across the United States as part of an effort to rethink STEM education. As we go, we will stop at corrections facilities, schools, libraries, and camps throughout the country to hold free Learning Festivals where we will teach hands-on, project-based workshops inspired by our passions in STEM.
>
> Previous Spokes members have loved hosting Learning festivals at the YCS and Department of Corrections, and our team would be excited to add this stop to our route again! **Washington DC is where our journey begins, so we would be available to host a Learning Festival on June 1st.**
>
> Let us know if you would be interested in having us this upcoming summer. We can provide more information about our curriculum as we approach the summer.
>
> Thank you, deeply, for your continued support of youth initiatives across the country! If you have any questions, please don't hesitate to reach out to me at my email or phone number. You can also check out our website at https://www.mitspokes.com/ for more information on our team!
>
> Warmly, Tatiana Vassiliev, Aarushi Mehrotra, Nathan Mustafa, Drew Dowdy, Carmen McCay, Nunu Zhuang, and Caroline Huang -- MIT Spokes 2026

### Email template: following up with learning festivals (2026 example, Challenger Learning Center, Hazard KY)
Sections to cover in the follow-up email (text from the Charles Bush / Challenger Learning Center email, 6/14/2026):
- **Registration:** create a registration graphic for the host to share; give key info (Challenger Learning Center, 6/14/2026, 1 Community College Dr, Hazard KY 41701, Google Form link, K-12, 9:45am-3:15pm); give the host edit access to the form so they can see responders and fix content.
- **Workshops:** attach a graphic with workshop descriptions to spread the word; ask for feedback and needed adaptations for their community.
- **Schedule:** 4 workshops of the 5 run simultaneously during each session (see Typical Schedule below).
- **Space:** confirm what is on record (2026 Hazard: 3 college classrooms with projector/smart board with HDMI, tile floors, 3-4 rows of tables, outlets; a separate carpeted 20x30 "briefing room" with 5-6 round tables and HDMI projector). Ask if still accurate so you can decide how many workshops run simultaneously.
- **Students:** how many can the space hold? is K-12 appropriate? when to close registration?
- **Food:** ask if they can provide lunch for students and team; list the other meals needed (2026 Hazard: dinner 6/13, breakfast 6/14, lunch 6/14 during the LF, dinner 6/14, breakfast 6/15).
- **Photo release:** attach one; all parents must sign for students to participate.
- **Housing:** ask about staying at the host's lodging (2026: the Harmony House on the 13th and 14th).
- Thank them warmly. Sign: Tatiana Vassiliev & Spokes 2026.

## Typical Schedule
Usually 4 workshops run in parallel across 4 sessions. Build in a break between sessions for transitions and snacks (teaching all day is tiring).

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 9 - 9:45am | Spokes setup |
| 9:45am | Students arrive |
| 10 - 11am | First workshop period |
| 11 - 12pm | Second workshop period |
| 12 - 1pm | Lunch/Break |
| 1 - 2pm | Third workshop period |
| 2 - 3pm | Fourth workshop period |
| 3 - 3:15pm | Wrap up |
| 3:15pm | Students leave |
| 3:15 - 4:00pm | Spokes cleanup |

### 2019 Learning Festivals & Reflections
General reflection (Evan, 2019 LF coordinator): Many reasons to do outreach. Middle school / early high school is where the team felt it made most impact but got many elementary kids (need lots of energy). LFs were clustered at the beginning/end, leaving dry spells in the middle; many small towns in Kansas, Colorado and Utah fit the target demographic better than e.g. Farmington or Lindon. Continuity matters: Hazard, KY shows how a strong connection can mean a lot. Maile/Erin handled LF communication early; later Evan and Edgardo. **Some coordinators were bad at email; just call them. A status-tracking spreadsheet was crucial** (times, ages, spaces differ). **Class prep was done way too late** (funding struggles into April; by May people were too busy to practice/iterate); recommend finishing workshop design before Spark 2019 (ESP). Evan has class-design resources and offered feedback. 2019 LF sheet: https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1jiUDzLexF_eeIJCIWBkugcuJuLIMSP6VojC0Z2_YdtY/edit (saved in linked/).

## Workshops
Teach in pairs; design your own curriculum covering varied topics with lots of hands-on activity. Activities must adapt on the fly to different age groups ("you never know what you're going to get"). Workshops often need revising after teaching once or twice: rework and improve.

All slide decks: https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1LlKDMpANFpUCAMzIRAG_is_0tYG9JtDM?usp=sharing (folder link, not crawled by this task). 2026 expense log shows workshop supply purchases for chemistry, felt/electronics/design, tilt sensors, coin batteries (see linked budget).

### 2022 Workshops (Drive folder links)
- **Electronics** (Franklin Zhang, Mariela Perez) https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/156M8JYSmH9EJt6U_WcbFd625E3hXGTRD . V1 Breadboarding: voltage/current/resistance via analogy; components (LEDs, wires, batteries); worksheet on breadboard closed loops; build an LED circuit with optional buttons, buzzers, potentiometers. V2 Paper Speaker: how speakers/sound work; build a speaker from paper template, wire coils/electromagnet, magnets; plug into phone/laptop (quiet but audible).
- **Physics of Music** (Robert Henning) https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/15BMN7URJ1NxQKfs47yFSXAszkSZ0CB4m
- **Chromatography and Color** (Simone Lassar, Sophia Fang) https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1DKP1QKX4kLFex5Q4QRcjbkSIc9TbNFom . Physical vs structural color; painting and chromatography; go outside to collect leaves.
- **3D Printing** (Maxwell Yun) https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1mUXg4IfRMj7-j6nKEQdS8Ai2LEyBXjFl . Real-world applications and technologies; hands-on with professionally printed parts (from Lightforce/Formlabs); watch a printer print live.
- **Solar Cell** (Sophia Fang, Simone Lassar) https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/15KoMkRydRc-utqBjlTZRY969OwqCtUkp . Build a dye-sensitized solar cell using raspberry/blackberry juice; measure voltage outside with a multimeter. Better for high schoolers (middle schoolers haven't had chemistry).

### 2019 Workshops
- **Design, Test, Repeat!** (Leah Yost, Caralyn Cutlip): rapid prototyping with cardboard, foamboard, construction paper, straws; challenges: keep a ping pong ball moving as long as possible; prototype a carnival game others test; discussion of why prototyping helps.
- **Decoding DNA** (Maile Jim, William Kuhl): extract DNA from strawberries; gel electrophoresis to identify mystery solutions ("science is not always certain").
- **Intelligence: from the Brain to Self-Driving Cars!** (Evan Tey, Edgardo Letona): brain, intelligence, AI, sight/memory/learning, self-driving cars; demo of reading nerve signals to control an arm; students build and test a neural network to tell nice from mean statements.
- **Fun with Chemistry** (Devin Seyler, Erin Leydon): demo-based (Whoosh Bottle, Elephant Toothpaste); younger students write hypotheses (scientific method).
