# Spokes Route Planning and Housing Master Guide

## Planning the Route
For many years MIT Spokes has started in Washington, DC and headed west to San Francisco. Recommended: take a similar route (2026 route sheet: https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1FZTiJBeVcn6Pf-vUAqcwMFPduEfY1yFDthliL9qTybc/edit) because lots of documentation/planning exists, and strong relationships exist with some learning festivals and hosts on it. The route follows **US Bike Route 76 (TransAmerican Bike Route)** from DC to Colorado. Adventure Cycling USBRS info: https://www.adventurecycling.org/routes-and-maps/us-bicycle-route-system/maps-and-route-resources/?route=virginia76

2026 chose to bike through many national parks and visit cities slightly off route, which made mileage higher than previous years. Each team decides which detours to take.

**Important (2026):** when planning, keep the Warm Showers host map, Google Maps and USBRS maps open at the same time. 2026 planned the route before checking Warm Showers hosts in each town and had to make several changes due to lack of hosts. Having at least 2 people on route planning (2026 had 3) worked well: one to contact Warm Showers, one to book campsites.

## Day to Day Navigation
- Route usually includes rest stops, water stops, car meet-ups, lunch stops. 1.5-2 hrs (~15 mi) between stops works well for refilling water. Gas stations have soda fountains for cold water, snacks, bathrooms. Meet the car when there are no gas stations.
- 2026: whole route generally preplanned before summer; route team adjusted the night before. Read previous years' blogs for notes on the next day's route.
- Tools: **Ride With GPS** (free; create GPX files for bike computers; charge computer every few days). Optionally one person gets **Strava premium** to see paved/unpaved road types. Google Maps street view for road type and shoulder width. Google Maps for unforeseen reroutes; download offline maps (areas without service).
- 2024 daily routes: Strava pages of Hank, Varsha (https://www.strava.com/athletes/69682060) and Jess (https://www.strava.com/athletes/77959178).

## Finding Stays
Spokes 2022 stays by frequency: Warm Showers, campsites, learning festivals, friends, MIT connections, churches, motels/hotels. Most WS hosts allow showers + laundry, cooking dinner/breakfast, and a place to sleep. LFs are often happy to help find lodging, so ask them. In 2022 many stays were with Sophia's church members. MIT alumni association helped: one LF and many stays came through alum Sam Ford (Kentucky).

**Warm Showers:** make a new account; state in the profile that you have a support car. Start contacting hosts in **February**. You are a large group: show flexibility and appreciation (e.g. "we can sleep on the floor"). Only contact hosts who can take 2+ people (desperate exceptions OK). 2026 contacted only hosts who could take 4+, which usually meant surprisingly nice houses (guest rooms/basements). Once confirmed, ask for phone number and address (app can be buggy) so the driver can reach the host; get the address early so the route planner has it the night before. Re-confirm a week before arrival; driver coordinates day-of.

**Campsites:** start booking in **February** (national parks fill fast). Prefer sites with showers.

**Learning Festivals:** coordinate with whoever contacts LFs to ask if the LF can provide lodging (especially where there are no Warm Showers hosts).

**MIT connections:** 2026 emailed all MIT alumni clubs along the route; only 3 responded, but still worth it (one donated; Sam Ford of Kentucky was met this way).

**Churches:** great: big kitchen, many bathrooms, sometimes snacks/meals. Look for churches where no WS host is confirmed. Call AND email every church (usually no response).

### Kentucky
Overview: great hosts and hospitality; many stays coordinated with Sam Ford (MIT alum network), who also organized the Bowling Green LF. Coal-mining history; towns relatively poor. **Dogs chase cyclists**: on the first day in Kentucky a 2022 rider crashed while chased by a dog. Carry air horns.
Favorite stays:
- **Wayland, KY - Jerry and King Fultz.** Jerry was mayor of a coal town of ~400; learned history of coal mining and basketball player "King Kelly Coleman". Phone (606) 226-2012; 46 Kentucky Rt 1086. Hosted 2022.
- **Liberty, KY - Don Cravens** (cousin of Tom Cravens of the Hazard LF). Cooked a feast and chocolate gravy; cow farm tour; dog Gunther. 1673 E State Hwy 70, Liberty KY 42539. Hosted 2022.
- **Henderson, KY - Karen and Roy Brunner** (friends of Sam Ford). Cabin in Audubon State Park (4 beds, big kitchen); took team to dinner; stocked snacks for weeks. Phone 27086008542 (as written); 904 Kelly Court, Henderson KY 42420. Hosted 2022.

### Colorado - Negative

### Utah
So many national parks. Do not bike into Arches from Thompson Springs: very little shoulder and high traffic; 2022 had to shuttle. Bryce Canyon to Cedar City was one of the most beautiful rides (mostly downhill through gorges and rivers).

### Nevada
Much is desert with no services for hundreds of miles. Baker NV to Carson City is "the Loneliest Road in America"; stock up on food, snacks, water (Ely is a good place to stock up). Eureka-Austin-Fallon is especially void of services. Collect stamps in a Loneliest Road booklet at gas stations and trade for a pin. Middlegate has a famous burger stop; look for messages from previous Spokes years.

### California
Spend a day at Lake Tahoe (lovely water/camping; couldn't find a "Welcome to California" sign near South Lake Tahoe). Lake Tahoe to Sacramento: Highway 50 quickly becomes a freeway with "no cyclists" signs; take side roads. Many gas stations north of San Francisco (before Sausalito) lack water/bathrooms; use grocery stores such as Whole Foods instead.
