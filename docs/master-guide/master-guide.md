# How to Use the Master Guides

We created some master guides to help with planning your trip! The master guide includes knowledge compiled from multiple years of Spokes. We hope that it will be useful for planning your trip. Once your trip is over, please update the Master Guides to include knowledge from your year and delete outdated information so you can pass it down to the next generation of Spokes. This Doc includes tips for different topics and links to other documents for topics that require more details. See table of contents for different topics.

# How to Start Planning

1. Get to know each other
2. Figure out what needs to be done
   1. Money
      1. Grant applications (the earlier, the better)
      2. Crowdfunding (must be through MIT, not GoFundMe, etc.)
      3. Talk to Marvelin Higginbottom (marvelin@mit.edu), Sandra J Lipnoski (slipnosk@mit.edu) and Chris Mayer (mayerc@mit.edu), "the light of our lives"
   2. Bikes/Gear
      1. Strongly recommend getting matching gravel tubeless bikes for the whole team
      2. Get bike fits with Brendan Connor through CycleLifePT, he will come on campus to do your bike fits + PT, "it will save your life"
   3. Route
      1. Stops
      2. Places to Stay (Warmshowers)
   4. Curriculum
      1. Contacts with Schools/libraries - set up dates for Learning Festivals
      2. Design workshops
      3. Get materials, refine curriculum
3. Figure out who is doing what
   1. Fundraising: start **EARLY** and go crazy
   2. Route/Hosts: See epic spreadsheet, WarmShowers
   3. Learning Festival Setup
   4. Bikes/Gear
   5. Curriculum (all)
4. Set up regular meetings and track list of actionable things from each
   1. Have a note taker and moderator for meetings!
5. Familiarize yourself with the Spokes Google Drive - wealth of info
6. Ask us for help whenever!!! We could talk about Spokes forever
   1. Email to us all: spokes-2026@mit.edu
   2. Text/GroupMe/whatever/email us individually - contact info in "Past Members' Information" below
7. Create a timeline!!! (IMPORTANT)
8. Use MIT Edgerton Center contacts for help/resources (Sandy, Chris, etc.)
9. No need to reinvent the wheel. But you can if you want! Tons of email templates, etc. in the Drive

# Timeline

This timeline is roughly what 2026 did, or rather maybe what we should have done :) It's just a recommendation, and some of these things are rather flexible (some gear purchases, etc). More details about each step should be in the master guides, but reach out if you have questions. This list is also not exhaustive, but just some things to keep track of as you go.

**November**
- First meeting!
- Read all the master guides
- Try to meet as a group before Thanksgiving
  - Assign roles
- Start fundraising ASAP!
  - Fundraising is the top To Do each month even if I don't list it explicitly
- Introduce yourselves to Edgerton

**December**
- Pick rough start and end dates
- Set up crowdfunding page and website
- Reach out to departments, labs, and centers for sponsorship
  - In December they often have leftover funding and can help out
- Meet with Edgerton (can do in IAP too)
- Start training/generally moving your body!

**January**
- Still meet during IAP!
- Start to generally plan route
- Make shared GCal <3
- Finalize trip dates
  - Decide if getting 1 way or 2 way rental
- Can start to have individual budget, social media meetings, etc
- Make a (very rough budget) for fundraising purposes
- Apply for 2 pro cards and 2 travel cards
- Start reaching out to learning festival locations

**February**
- Weekly meeting schedule
- Finish website updates
- Start thinking about/planning LF ideas
- Once trip dates are finalized: start car rental process
  - Start background checks for drivers
- Biking purchasing process around end of month
- Go to bike lab to learn about bikes!

**March**
- Apply to J-WEL Education Innovation Grant!!!!!!!!!!!
  - Where Spokes 24 got most of our $
- Post a blog introducing Spokes members
- Participate in MIT 24 Challenge for crowdfunding
- Order biking gear
- 2nd round of budget based on how fundraising is going

**April**
- Finalize gear list and start ordering
- Do the Midnight Marathon! Can fundraise by making a bingo card with dares
- LF materials list
- Finalize LFs EARLY so you have enough time to buy supplies
- Design and purchase Spokes shirts to wear at LFs
- Design and buy Spokes bike jerseys!
- Hear back from J-WEL at end of April
- Participate in Edgerton Center Showcase!
- Start getting housing in place for first few states

**May**
- Finalize all housing in advance
- Finalize budget before trip
- Get your tetanus shot!
- Discuss and assign roles for trip
- Train! Get comfortable on your bike
- Check in with Edgerton
  - Get photo release forms from them
- If desired: have a local LF!

**June (first week of trip)**
- Get all drivers listed on rental by going in person to rental car agency
- Organize car, purchase plastic boxes for storage
- Buy shared toiletries, sunscreen, etc
- Set up bike computers before the biking starts
- Crash course on bike repair and maintenance as needed
  - Let us know if you want us to share more info
  - Also: crash course on car maintenance!
- Have fun! It'll be awesome

**During the trip**
- Check in with each other! Even if it's awkward, have check in meetings to talk about how logistical stuff is going and to improve how the team is functioning
- Bike until your legs fall off
- Learn how to hold your bike above your head
- Obviously more stuff but you'll be a pro by then

# Roles

Here are the roles Spokes 2026 had and their rough responsibilities, as well as recommendations for roles. The bracketed number is the recommended number of people in that role. Roles generally become kinda flexible because of how small the team is, but don't be afraid to give people firm responsibilities.

**Fundraising [2]**
- Track fundraising totals on spreadsheet
- Make approximate timeline for fundraising goals
- Organize fundraising events (bike raffle, bake sale)
- Make email drafts for fundraising
- Meet sometimes outside of weekly meetings to discuss progress and what should be done
- Keep track of grant deadlines and delegate work
- Keeps others accountable on fundraising responsibilities
- Set up crowdfunding site with MIT
- Design and execute campaigns

**Finances & Budget [2-4]**
- Note: This role was assigned to two people but it is also the full responsibility of the 4 card holders. I would have 2 of the card holders automatically be the Finances team
- Acquire travel and pro cards
- Make sure tax exemption forms are up to date
- Keep track of receipts, purchasing before and during trip
- Communicate with Edgerton center when they ask about purchases
- Communicates with Gear team to purchase gear, and Learning Festival developers to purchase their supplies
- Make itemized budget
- Keep track of spending during trip
- Finalize spending report at end of trip
- Makes decision on purchases to ensure team stays within budget

**Edgerton Contact [1]**
- Basically the go-to person for answering Edgerton's emails
  - Can delegate responsibility to other team member if it makes sense

**Car Rental [1]**
- Communicate with Edgerton Center to rent car
- Make sure background checks, other necessary documents are filled out
- Help to handle reporting in case of accident or damage during the trip

**Route Planning [3]**
- Plan the overall route, taking the group's requirements and preferences into mind
- Resolves route issues during the trip as needed
- Makes the daily route for the trip
- Marks out water and lunch stops
- Uses Strava, RidewithGPS, google maps for route

**Learning Festivals [2]**
- Make timeline for LF development during the semester
- Checks in with people to see if they need assistance making their LF activities
- Communicate with Route Planning to finalize festivals and dates
- Communicates with LF hosts

**Housing [3]**
- Communicates with MIT alum, WarmShowers hosts, churches to acquire housing
- Books campgrounds

**Social Media [2]**
- Runs social media!
- Makes posters and other graphics
- Communicates with Sonny Oram, Edgerton Communications Officer
- Design Spokes t-shirts and jerseys

**Gear [2]**
- Note: it probably makes sense for a gear person to hold the pro card (card to purchase stuff not related to travel). We had one person but you could have like one gear person and then one gear person who is also on finances.
- Creates list of gear needed (bike gear, camping gear, etc)
- Communicates with pro-card holders to purchase gear during the semester
- Communicates with Edgerton and MIT to purchase bikes

**Website [1]**
- Updates website
- Posts sponsor graphics
- Handles website purchasing/subscription as needed

**Meeting Moderator and Notetaker [1]**
- Keeps their eye on the clock during weekly meetings
- Ensures agenda items are discussed
- Sends out agenda
- Main notetaker during weekly meetings

**Training [1]**
- Sets training plan and holds training events

**Additional roles during the trip**

*Driver [alternates]*
- Goes grocery shopping/other shopping during the day
- Gives water stops to bikers when needed
- Often will do other miscellaneous tasks that are needed

*Laundry [alternates]*
- Does the team's laundry
- Use laundry loops to keep people's stuff together

*Thank you card*
- Writes each host a thank you card

*Dinner Lead*
- Past teams have cooked a majority of their own meals
- Spokes 2026 team cooked 0 meals the whole summer and instead we had one person responsible for calling places every night and asking for free food (extremely successful)

*Blog Lead*
- Keeps track of blog progress, bumps people as needed
- Blog writer rotates everyday, make sure some blogs are drivers, passengers, bikers, etc. to get different perspectives

*Documentation*
- Note: we also didn't have this role but could be good too because it's easy to forget stuff
- Writes down advice for the next team, any large problems the team has, route recommendations, etc

# Food and Nutrition

Eating the right food for fueling yourself and for recovery is important. Eat carbs in the morning and while you are biking. Make sure to eat enough protein for dinner / later in the day to help with recovery. Drink electrolytes while biking to prevent cramping and replenish electrolytes. Below is a list of snacks, breakfast ideas, lunch ideas, dinner ideas, electrolytes, and a list of protein sources we recommend.

## Breakfast

Usually our hosts would provide breakfast for us. When we're camping, we have to make stuff for ourselves. Here are some of our good things to do for breakfast that we "mostly" recommend.

- Bagels and cream cheese: Fast and easy! Get some smoked salmon if you're feeling fancy
  - Also PB and Nutella for protein, fat, and carbs
- Yogurt + Granola: Yum
- Overnight oats are good. Once we made overnight oats in ziplock bags and ate them in the car on the way to a learning festival. Yum. Don't even need a fridge
- Apples and PB: Yum
- Protein pancakes: Personally not a huge fan because it requires setting up the stove, cooking each pancake one at a time, and cleaning a lot more stuff. Takes too long. But also I'm not a big pancake fan.
- Spokes 24 classic: powdered milk + instant coffee + cereal + powdered PB (optional)
- Protein powder/powdered PB + powdered milk
- Cottage cheese if you get sick of sugary breakfast items

## Lunch

**Spokes 22:** For lunch we tried to include a mix of protein and carbs. We try to make these meals easy and fast. Often times we just use paper towels as plates.
- Sardines and tortillas
- Rotisserie chicken with Hawaiian bread
- Deli meat sandwiches
- Hummus with veggies
- Apples and PB

**Spokes 24:** We had bagel sandwiches for approximately 74 days in a row. Towards the end of the trip, we also discovered the majesty of French bread, which is super cheap from Walmart and tastes better. Our sandwich supplies were usually:
- Deli meat (+ tofurkey meat)
- bagels/bread
- Hummus (very important)
- lettuce/tomato (the water from tomato feels divine on a hot day)
- Cream cheese
- Mayo
- Sliced cheeses
- pickles/jalapenos
- Potato chips (for crunch)

**Spokes 26:** We found the Venn diagram of snack that most people on the team liked and spammed those throughout the whole summer. In parts of the summer it meant sandwiches like past teams, sometimes we did a liquid diet, sometimes a bunch of fruit and snacks.

## Snacks

Always bring snacks with you while biking! Good to prevent bonking (running out of energy while biking).

- Dried dates: Costco sells huge bags of dates for really cheap! Dates carried the 2022 team. Cheaper than buying a ton of Larabars
- Dried fruits: dried mango, figs, and apricots are all good too.
- Bars: Our favorites are Clif bars and Lara bars. Some chocolate ones melt so might want to stay away from them
  - The best snacks for Spokes 24 were the Nature's Bakery chocolate brownie bar. Heavenly
- Nuts: Go nuts
  - Trail mix really hits the spot but will melt super quickly and you have to eat it from the bag like a grazing cow
- Electrolytes! They are real and important! We like Nuun, Liquid IV, Propel, and Gatorade.
  - Buying a tub of Gatorade powder is by far the cheapest, would recommend. Still good to have packets that people can take with them on the bike
- Gas station snacks: sometimes we get snacks from gas stations when we stop there for water stops. Personally not a huge fan of their selection but others like it.

## Dinner

Spokes 2026 did not cook a single time. We called places and asked for free meals if our host could not feed us. If you do want to cook, here are some meals that you can make using just 2 pots that are easy to make and packed with protein.

- Jambalaya: One pot rice dish with tomatoes, red beans, sausage, and meat. Good for camping days
- Feijoada: Easy black bean stew with pork. Good for camping days
- Chicken Salad: Made with canned chicken, or canned tuna. Good for hotel stays where we don't have access to stove
- Tacos: Easy dinner, can use beans, chicken, and other meats as a filling.
- Chicken and Rice: Easy. Pan fry some chicken and finish it off in the rice. Throw whatever seasonings you want. Replace chicken with any meat you'd like. Ideas:
  - Spam, pineapple "fried rice"
  - Bell peppers
  - Onions
  - GARLIC
  - Craisins and nuts
  - Lemon
- Spring Rolls: Can buy shrimp and tofu and just season them, and then make a sauce and cut up some veggies. No stove!
- Pasta: Find a good one pot pasta recipe and then make some meat/tofu on the side.

## Protein Sources

Protein sources the 2022 team used the most on their trip:
- Sardines (canned): Great for lunch! The canned variety from Costco was very good. We did eventually get tired of it after eating sardines every day for lunch
- Textured Vegetable Protein (shelf stable): Found at Whole Foods. Pure protein granules that can be added to dinner foods for extra protein. Takes 5 mins to cook
- Tofu (can be shelf stable): the shelf stable variety is great!
- Canned chicken: Good to add to so many meals
- Unflavored protein powder (shelf stable): found at Whole Foods. Good to add to savory foods as a thickener; also shakes or sweet dishes
  - 24 had chocolate powder which was great!
- Fresh meats: so much better than canned, but requires constant refrigeration. Good for dinner
- Rotisserie chicken: Great for a fast lunch. Goes well with Hawaiian rolls. Good for dinner too.
- Deli meats: Good for a lazy lunch
- Jerky (shelf stable until opened): Costco has them! Great. Expensive though
- Soymilk (shelf stable)
- Peanut butter (shelf stable)

## Tricks of the Trade

- Use Minute Rice! It makes cooking rice dishes so much faster and easier
- Costco is your friend
- Walmart is also your friend. You will miss it when you get to more remote areas
- Baby wipes are REALLY useful for cleaning your hands. Also, we prewipe our dishes with them and clean them with soap later.
- Paper towels as plates for fast cleanup
- A pocket stove is good if you need to cook something small but don't have to pull out the behemoth of a camping stove we have. Spokes 2022 used it for boiled eggs! We could cook up to 9 eggs in them at a time!
- The driver picking up some cold soft drinks/iced tea can be life saving on a hot day. Ice becomes a luxury

# Packing Guide

For Spokes, it's important to pack light because there will not be a lot of room in the car. We were able to do laundry pretty much every day or every other day. That means that you don't really need that many pairs of clothes. I would recommend having 2 or 3 sets of biking outfits (No more than 3), and 2 sets of non-biking outfits (including the Spokes T-shirt). Also, bring some clothes for cold weather (temperature can get down to the 50s in the Rockies). Get easy to dry clothes like synthetic or wool. Faster drying means less mildew. People often brought backpacking bags, but I've found that having a small carry-on suitcase works better because things will be easier to access and the bag will be easier to stack inside the car.

## Sample Packing List

This is based on what Franklin brought in 2022. Franklin packed relatively light. Had 1 small carry-on luggage, backpack, and fanny pack.

The suitcase has two layers of stuff. The bottom layer has camping gear and warm weather clothes that are used less often (left image). On top of that, biking and non-biking outfits used every day (right image). [Two photos of the packed suitcase in the original; not captured.]

Brought: 1 large bag (suitcase), backpack, bike saddle bag, bike handlebar bag, and sleeping bag.

- Large bag (carry-on suitcase recommended; a backpacking bag works too)
  - 2x biking outfits
    - Socks
    - Biking jersey
    - Padded bike shorts (Make sure it's padded! Also you don't need to wear underwear underneath)
    - Sun legs and sleeves (Either use a lot of sunscreen or use sleeves)
  - 2x non-biking outfits (shorts, synthetic T-shirts, socks, underwear)
  - Cold weather clothes
    - 1x thermal leggings
    - 1x long pants
    - 1x warm wool socks
    - 1x puffy jacket (down insulated)
- Small backpack. Mainly to carry laptop and things you want easy access to. Useful for days when not biking.
  - Toiletries
  - Laptop + charger
  - Frisbee! (some would say this is not necessary, but they are wrong)
  - Crocs + Shoes (keep inside/on the bag so they are not loose all over the place)
- Front handlebar bag and saddle bag (fanny packs are great as front handlebar bags). Keep things needed while biking in the fanny or saddle bag:
  - Maxwell's note: you can also buy a purpose-made handlebar bag (Amazon link in original: https://a.co/d/etvGGPf). He carried his large camera in this bag while riding.
  - Snacks + Snack bag
  - Phone + Power bank + Charger (optional)
  - Water bottles
  - Portable bike pump + Bike tools
  - First aid kit
  - Air horn and mace (dog protection)
    - "We got chased by dogs a lot in Kentucky. Carry an airhorn in easily accessible spot while biking." (2022)

Note: Spokes 24 stored all camping gear together as a team, but we still recommend generally bringing one larger suitcase/duffel and one smaller bag!
