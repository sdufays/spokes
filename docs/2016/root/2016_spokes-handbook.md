# Spokes Handbook (as edited over the years; entries are labeled by year)

**Purpose:** distil what is not worth re-learning every year. Spokes America is an organization under the MIT Edgerton Center; students (mostly Boston area) bike across the US in summer teaching STEM workshops. There is also a Stanford Spokes. Each year's team may reshape the vision. Past vision links: 2013 MIT admissions blog "how-a-group-of-mit-students-are-trying-to-change-education"; 2014 Indiegogo "spokes-america-2014"; 2015 tumblr spokesamerica15 "the-spokes-vision"; 2022 team mission statements.

## Team size
- Consensus: **no more than 7-8 members** (recommend 5-7).
- 2015: 7 members (limits: fitting everyone in the van, team dynamics). 2014: 7 (cost per person drops with size except when you must buy two campsites once you exceed 4 or 6 people). 2013: 8. 2022: 6 members (7 planned; one dropped for health). 6 worked well for shuttling: could not get a minivan from Budget, used an SUV with a Saris Bones EX rack holding 3 bikes, so thunderstorm pickups = 2 trips for 6 people.
- (2016 itself had 10-11 people in two planned groups; see other 2016 docs.)

## Member roles (2015, no official titles; most to least work)
Funding; daily travel itinerary; organizing festivals with schools/libraries; buying equipment; planning workshops (everyone); social media; setting up pro card + travel card with MIT; website maintenance; thank-yous for donations.

## Route
- 2015: **DC to SF**. Reasons: landscape gets prettier westward, end in California, Outside Lands festival 2nd week of August, prevailing west-to-east winds are largely a myth (few windy days; south-to-north winds regardless). 2014 and 2013 went SF to DC.
- Highly recommended: reuse the 2015 route (largely follows TransAmerica): route planning is done, ~11 festival hosts (schools/libraries/museums) are already known to work, the route was great. Caveat: the van carried everyone longitudinally a few times to reach festivals, then returned to where they left off to keep "integrity" of the ride.

## Support van
- 2015: a team member's 2004 Honda Odyssey, "10/10 every Spokes team should have one": carries ~20 laptops/teaching gear, picks up injured riders / broken bikes / bad weather, shuttles to festivals. Drivers rotated daily (each drove about once a week). 4 bikes on roof, 3 on rear rack. Expensive to rack and maintain (broke down in Nevada desert) but cheaper than renting. Do not drive under low awnings with bikes on the roof. Member drove it back east.
- 2014: rented van (expensive). Opinions: Cali 3/10 would not do again; Sam 10/10 for Spokes (0/10 for a personal tour); Evan 7/10 if same SF-DC route, depends on route and team feelings.
- 2013: Honda van owned by a team member.

## Teaching
- 2015: 3 workshops (Scratch computer science, model rockets mechanical eng., Spout robots electrical eng.), each taught by 2-3 people, 3 or 6 hours each, hands-on/constructivist. Festivals 1-1.5 days, workshops run in parallel so students try all three. 11 festivals, avg 35 students (10-80). Advertised via school/library email lists, Boys & Girls Clubs, newspapers. Consulted TFA for prep ("not very helpful"); better to ask former Spokes teams. A small practice festival in Vermont before the trip helped.
- **2019**: ten festivals: Challenger Learning Center (Hazard KY), Lab: Revolution (Farmington MO), St. Louis Homeschool Connection, aSTEAM Village (Kansas City MO), Newton Public Library (KS), Boys and Girls Club of Denver, Christa McAuliffe Space Center (Pleasant Grove UT), Ely Public Library (NV), Churchill County Library (Fallon NV), Boys and Girls Club Carson City. 30-50 students each, mostly grades 3-7. Four workshops: *Design, Test, Repeat!* (rapid prototyping: ping-pong-ball keeper, carnival game), *Decoding DNA* (strawberry DNA extraction, gel electrophoresis, mystery solutions), *Intelligence: from the Brain to Self-Driving Cars* (EMG arm-control demo, build a neural net to classify nice vs mean sentences), *Fun with Chemistry* (Whoosh bottle, elephant toothpaste, scientific method hypotheses). Dinner/lunch socials with students to talk about STEM and college.

## Fundraising
- 2015: **grants didn't work**; focus on corporate sponsors, Indiegogo, and personal donors (professors, families) via MIT Giving. Each team member personally emailed 10 people.
- 2014 (corporate): list STEM/engineering/software/financial firms (pitched Silicon Valley Bank, banks); keep a spreadsheet of contacts + status; one person owns each email chain, don't CC the team. Use professor/alumni connections; send TWO emails together (personal intro + formal email with PDF pitch letter) so the contact can forward the formal one. Pitch letter: who/what/when/why, <2 pages. First asks 5-10k from huge companies (TI, IAC); then benchmark on prior sponsors (IAT wanted 2-3k, gave 5k after hearing IAC gave 10 and TI 5 plus jerseys plus LaunchPads). Be careful sharing other sponsors' amounts until committed. Deliverables: small sponsor (IAT) paid lots of attention; huge one (IAC) never contacted again after the check; don't over-promise deliverables (IAT asked for index cards with politician quotes). Only real obligation is to mention sponsors.
- Harvard SEAS: formal grant mechanisms didn't fit; asking a senior professor with a pitch letter produced $5,000 from an anonymous SEAS donor. Don't force formal grants; ask grant coordinators candidly first.

## During the trip
- Places to stay (2015): campsites; local parks (call city hall / fire / rec dept); Warm Showers; churches; motels; MIT/Harvard alum families (parent FB groups, alumni lists); friends; bivvy.
- "Spokes' lifeblood is its reputation": team changes yearly so reputation is all it has. In 2015 rude behaviour in a small town got back to the makerspace host. Be nice to everyone.
- Team bonding (2015): Harvard FOP-style games (Hot Seat, NYT 36 questions, Moth stories, 5-minute autobiographies). Free days: 1 in St. Louis, 1 in Kansas City, 2 in Denver.

## After
- Recruiting: little recruiting historically; spread word on FB/email lists without overselling, the interested find you (natural filter).
