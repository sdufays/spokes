# Gear and Car Master Guide (updated 2026)
## Choosing and buying bikes
- Trek Cambridge (Alewife) gives 20% on all gear bought through them (use for big purchases); negotiate. Cambridge Bicycle sells at wholesale but smaller selection (small purchases); one note says do NOT go to Cambridge Bicycle (overpriced). Landry's gave discounts on gear (not bikes).
- Recommendations: go tubeless (fewer flats, easier repairs); everyone gets the same bike (swap parts); gravel bikes, disc brakes, drop bars, tires 35 mm or wider; shop around.
- Spokes 2026: Trek Checkpoint ALR3 gen3 for everyone plus bike fits with Brendan Connor at CycleLifePT. Chosen for 1x10 gearing (no front derailleur, huge rear gear for Appalachia), wide tire clearance (significant offroad time), and Trek warranty help (several hundred dollars of warranties used during trip).
## Gear storage
Previous gear, camping supplies and LF supplies are stored in Milkdrop N52 (coordinate with Edgerton).
## Bike gear
- Metal water bottles (2026 learned in heat that plastic does not keep water cool; Camelbak Podium metal tall); get as many bottle cages as fit (JD used 3 in Utah/Nevada deserts). Spokes 2024 started with 48 oz and upgraded to 72 oz in Utah.
- Frame/saddle bags, small first aid kit, optional Swiss army knife, spare tubes matching tire size, multitool with allen keys, frame pump, portable charger (strongly recommended).
- Must-have: front and rear lights, clip-in shoes and pedals, helmet, first aid kit on at least one bike, allen keys, bottles/cages, frame pump plus floor pump with gauge, snacks, sunglasses, gloves, padded shorts, tire repair kit (tubes, levers, patch kit; tire plugs if tubeless). Upgraded seat (Terry Liberator in 2019 and 2022; 2024 and 2026 did not and were fine).
- Nice-to-have: sun sleeves/legs, chamois butter (used less than expected), fenders (2026 skipped; personal preference), handlebar bag (2022 converted fanny packs), battery pack, open-ear/bone-conduction headphones (Shokz; helpful in Kansas/E. Colorado).
- Keep in car: chain cleaning tools (rag, degreaser, lube, brush), allen keys, floor pump, extra sealant, extra tires/tubes, bike rack (check compatibility), locks.
## Maintenance
Clean and lube bikes about weekly. Everyone should minimally be able to fix flats, tune derailleur, adjust brakes, clean and lube chain before leaving Boston.
## Car rental
- MIT has discounts with Budget and Avis. Talk to Sandi (slipnosk@mit.edu) and Chris (mayerc@mit.edu); Chris main contact in 2024. Reserve as soon as you know when you leave Boston. Preferred Budget: Central Square, 350 Mass Ave. Recommend minivan. 2022 got a Ford Explorer (6 people so it worked) with Saris Bones EX bike rack; 2023 and 2024 used a 2022 Chrysler Pacifica. The 2024 bike rack bent and fell near trip end.
- Avis/Budget judged a bad company but the only one MIT has an agreement with covering drivers under 21; recheck. If problems, talk to Edgerton and Jessica Tam (VP Finance office, https://vpf.mit.edu/jessica-tam) who got a discount after rental troubles. Account of Avis issues: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1mmLRVGplxzhjIGy_oJITyBfQRbbyaXRd/view
- 2023, 2024, 2026 also rented a truck/van for first days to carry bikes, supplies, people to DC area (booked via Edgerton). 2024: Toyota Tacoma (3 bikes in bed), returned at Dulles (about 50 min from DC, near Oakton start). 2026: cargo van. You cannot choose make/model.
- Car size: with luggage, LF supplies, gear, food only two seats usable; minivan has 7 seats vs team of 8. SUV has 8 seats and more space but costs more and is harder for novice drivers (more incident risk; Sandi advised against). Don't go smaller than a minivan. Stanford Spokes 2024 used an 11-seater van and said it was fine.
- One-way vs two-way: 2023 and 2024 did one-way, dropped at SFO; costs a few thousand dollars more and required shipping gear/bikes back (2 days, very expensive). Past teams did 2-way with East Coast members driving back with bikes/gear. Airlines may allow checking bikes.
- Background checks: start early via Edgerton; emailed forms; cannot drive MIT rental without one. Edgerton books and pays directly (not the travel card; travel card can book a car in an emergency). For MIT discount/insurance see https://insurance.mit.edu/services/insurance-coverage-claims/automobile/rentals/
- IMPORTANT: have 4 drivers go to an Avis/Budget service desk (Boston or Dulles) to be added to the rental (max 5 drivers incl. renter) so they can request roadside assistance. Once roadside refused because the renter was biking.
- At pickup: ask last service date, walk around and photograph scratches, check engine/tires, check for recalls.
## Car troubles / accidents
- 2019, 2023, 2024 had car trouble (mostly first week). Rental cars lack repair tools. Call 24h roadside number on keys; if no eligible person present, call Edgerton (2024 got tow authorized this way). Avis usually swaps the whole car (inconvenient mid-country). 2026 notes: don't speed in Vail CO; had to rip off a dragging oil pan cover in Kansas.
- Accident: call Edgerton; MIT has no physical insurance card: show rental agreement, say company car through school, insured by MIT (do not give personal insurance). Get written proof of insurance for glove box. Fill out Avis/Budget incident form and send to MIT promptly.
## Day-to-day car
Buy a small hand vacuum and air fresheners; hard-sided plastic boxes from Walmart instead of Tetris packing; learn to inflate car tires (a tire often deflated); car sits low when overloaded (2022 got stuck on a bumpy road, see blog day 52, https://www.mitspokes.com/post/day-52-ah-shit-here-we-go-again). Keep food, water, first aid, pump, spare tubes accessible. 2026 note: Sandi can be unreasonable; respectful pushback sometimes needed.
