# Spokes Fundraising and Budget Master Guide (updated 2026; originally Onboarding 2023)

Resources (links in the doc): 2026 Fundraising sheet https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1FPTyT0dNyo_6VWgB5UZjfpRAu7M1nz7qJ5W9j5wu9bM/edit ; 2024 Fundraising sheet https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1F8IRfUqSX1TelRrPAD3O6pD0ET4618Zk-kJJ1b5gNas/edit ; 2022 Fundraising Tracking sheet https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/17v31-8H78ytmBIgOeT0rD-tKq4iMF3XWavwFf2EgS6M/edit ; 2026 Expenses + Budget https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/10qw-YwBw6pqD9v3xrn6hnOZSJoMLIgIy77G7KqC9vIM/edit

## Fundraising
- Start early. Need more than one person on fundraising. By spring, excess corporate money (Texas Instruments, Microsoft, Dell, etc.) is already gone.
- Start with MIT departments: cold-email department heads/administrators. Template emails are in the "Fundraising" folder of the Spokes Drive.
- Grants inside MIT: TBP funding historically $2000 after application. Baker Foundation $2000 (we planned/hosted a bike repair and safety workshop for them). "Mad Money Grant" gave 2019 a few thousand for a pamphlet with trip info.
- Alumni groups: usually donate little but great connection and helped get housing in some states.
- Bake sale + raffle for bike tune-ups raised around $2,000 total.
- 2024: most money ($45,330) came from the JWEL Education Grant (https://www.jwel.mit.edu/grants). Applications open in February; decisions not until April, so pursue other sources in parallel. Highly recommended to apply again.
- Outside MIT: many corporate/nonprofit grants. Funded by St. Anthony Educational Foundation ($4000).
- Companies: Lightforce and Desktop Metal (3D-printing companies) helped with the 3D-printing workshop with real printed parts; Lightforce also sponsored $5000 in 2022.
- Companies must register with MIT to donate (conflict-of-interest check). Went through this for Lightforce. Talk to Sandi when you get a corporate donation.
- Crowdsource from family/friends/coworkers; everyone in the group participates.
- Tips: emphasize the education portion; offer sponsor benefits (website/T-shirt recognition, in-person visits if local); cast a wide net; use Word mail merge to send many emails.

## Budget
- Budget determines how much to fundraise. Itemize as much as possible (e.g. list each bike-gear item at a local bike shop price and sum). Transportation and food costs were guessed; consult prior-year trip finances.

## Finances: two cards
- Travel card: for expenses during the trip (food, gas). Procard: for gear and learning-festival supplies; if it would have made sense to buy at MIT before the trip, use the procard. "Health and beauty" products (shampoo, body wash, sunscreen) cannot go on either card.
- Getting a pro or travel card requires online training and an application. Spokes 2026 had two people with pro cards and two different people with travel cards.
### Travel card
- Holders must file a Concur report at the end of the trip (tedious; upload receipts during the trip instead of after).
- Upload receipts for purchases >$75 and any food purchase. Catalogue other purchases too (gas can look like groceries on the Concur report).
- Lost receipt >$75: attach a missing-receipt affidavit and mark expense Non-Recoverable (per Sandi Lipnoski, MIT administrator). Worst case is an auditor inquiry.
- JWEL account is a "sponsored account" (extra approval when outside funds used); the Spokes account is a department account, so travel charged to SPOKES account is non-sponsored activity.
### Pro/purchasing card
- Send receipts to Marvelin within two weeks of purchase; you get emails from procard@mit.edu listing pending transactions.
- Occasional audits: respond promptly explaining what was bought. Reduce audits by not buying food, gas, or health & beauty on the procard.
- Lost receipt on procard is worse: card may be deactivated if audited.
- Misc: purchases are tax exempt in MA but not elsewhere (text truncated).
