# Learning Festival & Workshops Master Guide (updated 2026)
## Email templates (2026 versions)
**To past location (example: DC youth corrections / YCS office):** introduce yourself as member of MIT Spokes, describe program ("a team of 7 MIT students on an 80-day biking trip across the US to rethink STEM education; free Learning Festivals with hands-on project-based workshops at corrections facilities, schools, libraries, camps"), note previous Spokes members loved hosting there, propose a date (DC start: June 1), offer curriculum details later, link https://www.mitspokes.com/ , sign with all team names.
**Follow-up (example Challenger Learning Center, Hazard KY, 6/14/2026, 1 Community College Dr, Hazard KY 41701, K-12, 9:45am-3:15pm):** cover (1) Registration graphic + Google Form link (give host edit access), (2) workshop descriptions graphic, (3) schedule, (4) Space (record: 3 college classrooms with projector/HDMI, tile floors, tables, outlets; separate 20x30 carpeted briefing room with round tables), (5) Students: capacity, age range, when to close registration, (6) Food: lunch for students and team; plus dinner/breakfast needs for the night before and after, (7) Photo release for parents, (8) Housing (Harmony House BNB in Hazard).
## Typical schedule
4 workshops run in parallel, 4 sessions. 9-9:45 setup; 9:45 students arrive; 10-11 session 1; 11-12 session 2; 12-1 lunch/break; 1-2 session 3; 2-3 session 4; 3-3:15 wrap up; 3:15 students leave; 3:15-4 cleanup. Build breaks between sessions (snacks).

Housing: Mineral Fire Department (often hosts cyclists). Contacts: louisabranch@jmrl.org, Sadie Evergreen severgreen@jmrl.org, Ophelia Payne opayne@jmrl.org

- St. Louis MO, Ferguson Middle School and Johnson-Wabash School (new; summer school): mostly African American students; see-through backpack rule; connected via Tom Cravens -> Tasmyn (tasmyn@clcstlouis.org, Challenger STL) -> Jontae vice principal (jgovan@fergflor.org, 314-506-9600). - Denver Public Library (jdewey@denverlibrary.org): did not happen (never heard back); became rest day.

## 2019 reflections (summary)
Middle/early high school felt most impactful; many elementary kids need most energy. Team disliked Farmington/St. Louis and some disliked Kansas City LFs. LFs clustered at start/end; middle had dry spells; target small towns in Kansas, Colorado, Utah. Keep a status spreadsheet and CALL hosts bad at email. Class prep started too late (money struggles into April; people too busy by May); finish before Spark 2019 (ESP). 2019 LF sheet: https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1jiUDzLexF_eeIJCIWBkugcuJuLIMSP6VojC0Z2_YdtY/edit
Per-site 2019: Hazard KY Challenger (registration problem so fewer kids, easier first run; recommend a small practice LF before Hazard; interns helped; 3 workshops day 1, 1 on day 2, social time); Lab: Revolution Farmington MO (makerspace in refurbished factory; hosts paid hotel when hostel full; younger kids); St. Louis Homeschool Connection (church; ~30 kids already had STEM opportunities); aSTEAM Village Kansas City (hard to find; 80 kids; classroom-control struggles; poor lunch; camp cost money); Newton Public Library KS (~30 kids, lunch and dinner provided, helpful coordinator Marianne); Boys and Girls Club Denver (little kids, organized, good place to teach, no lunch); Christa McAuliffe Space Center Lindon UT (~20 kids; picnic lunch; space simulation); White Pine County Library Ely NV (conference room split in half, ~20-25 kids); Churchill County Library Fallon NV (table setups, elementary kids); Boys and Girls Club Carson City NV (less organized).

## Workshops
Teach in pairs; design own curriculum; hands-on; adaptable on the fly to ages; revise after first teaching. 2026 slide decks: https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1LlKDMpANFpUCAMzIRAG_is_0tYG9JtDM
**2022 workshops:** Electronics (Franklin Zhang, Mariela Perez; v1 breadboarding LED circuits with buttons/buzzers/potentiometers; v2 paper speaker with coils and magnets); Physics of Music (Robert Henning); Chromatography and Color (Simone Lassar, Sophia Fang; physical vs structural color, painting, leaf chromatography); 3D Printing (Maxwell Yun; applications, technologies, real printed parts, live print); Solar Cell (Sophia Fang, Simone Lassar; dye-sensitized cell with raspberry/blackberry juice, measure voltage with multimeter; better for high school).
**2019 workshops:** Design, Test, Repeat (rapid prototyping; ping pong ball device; carnival game; Leah Yost, Caralyn Cutlip); Decoding DNA (strawberry DNA, gel electrophoresis; Maile Jim, William Kuhl); Intelligence: from the Brain to Self-Driving Cars (Evan Tey, Edgardo Letona); Fun with Chemistry (whoosh bottle, elephant toothpaste; Devin Seyler, Erin Leydon).
