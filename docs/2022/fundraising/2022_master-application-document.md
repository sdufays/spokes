Concise summary of project that includes only the most important points
The Spokes team will be biking from Washington D.C to San Francisco to engage with students in rural communities this summer. We will stop at schools, libraries, and camps as we travel throughout the country to hold free Learning Festivals where we will teach hands-on, project-based workshops inspired by our passions in STEAM. Students range from elementary to middle-school students, and workshops have included introductory electronics, software, and chemistry, among other topics. We hope to inspire students to pursue STEAM and higher education, as well as encourage them to stay athletic and active from coast to coast.

Description of the project and why it's needed, including data/evidence to support your view.
Programs that have the most success in provoking student interest in STEM includes summer programs. However, there is an outreach disparity in these programs which generally denies rural students in favor of “at risk” or “gifted” students [1]. It is important to get rural students to pursue STEM fields because many of the projected job increases in the next decade are in STEM fields. The median annual wage for STEM occupation is $89,780 compared to $40,020 for non-STEM occupation and the percent employment change from 2020-30 for STEM and non-STEM are 10.5% and 7.5% respectively [2].

(1) Harris, Rachel S., and Charles B. Hodges. "STEM Education in Rural Schools: Implications of Untapped Potential." National Youth-At-Risk Journal 3.1 (2018): 3-12.
(2) Labor, U. D. "US Bureau of Labor Statistics." Retrieved from (2022).

Describe expected outputs, outcomes and impacts (if applicable) of the proposed project. 
By the end of this program we expect to successfully bike from Washington D.C. to San Francisco, design four workshops to teach at the learning festivals, hold at least ten learning festivals and have at least 500 students attend these festivals. We hope that we will receive positive testimonials from our hosts and the attendees, and that in each of the communities where the learning festivals were hosted, a greater number of students will pursue STEM fields and apply to colleges such as MIT. In the long term the goal is to promote educational equity and increase representation of students from these more rural areas in STEM related careers.

Organizational Capacity: Discuss your organization's and personnel's prior experience/results in implementing initiatives similar to the proposed program. 
The Spokes program has done the cross country journey almost every summer for more than ten years. A handbook has been created based on the experiences and lessons learned from previous years and includes guidance on trip planning, design workshops, hosting learning festivals, recruiting, fundraising, and much more. We will use the information provided by the previous years in order to make this year’s program even better. The organization consists of 7 students and graduates from MIT with a variety of prior experience in teaching and organization. Our members are teaching assistants and recitation instructors for college level courses, hiking/backpacking trip leaders, caving trip leaders, mentors to refugee youth, teachers, CovEd mentors and more.

Describe methods that will be used to measure the project's outputs, outcomes and (if applicable) impacts. (See information icon above for definitions.) For example, collection of specific data such test scores, % increase in skills, survey results, etc.
We will use surveys in order to assess the outcomes of the workshops. The surveys will include questions that gauge a student’s interest in specific fields in STEM before and after each workshop and solicit feedback for each workshop, including a numerical ranking. This will allow us to gauge whether the workshops were able to get students more interested in STEM and allow us to better understand the quality of our teaching.

Plans for Publicity/Communication of Results: Please describe the before, during, and after plan to publicize the proposed project. For example, actions you'll take prior to the project's start to ensure sufficient attendance/participation; how you'll publicize milestones that occur during the project; and how you'll communicate results once the project has concluded 
Prior to arrival, we will work with our community partners/ hosts to publicize the learning festivals. Our community partners include local libraries, learning centers, boys & girls clubs, and other organizations. These organizations have existing channels of communications that we can leverage to publicize the learning festivals. Throughout the journey, we will have daily posts on our website, blog, and social media accounts. We will conduct surveys at the end of each learning festival which will help us understand our accomplishments. Along with these accomplishments we will post testimonials on our website.

Describe targeted population, including specific location and estimated number of people to be served.
Target audience is elementary school to high school students in rural areas across the country. Previous years we have hosted learning festivals in Kentucky, Missouri, Kansas, Colorado, Utah and Nevada. We hope to continue hosting learning festivals in these areas and also expand to other states. The estimated number of students that will attend these learning festivals is 500.
