MIT Spokes
Keaten Clarno, Sophia Fang, Robert Henning, Simone Lassar,
Mariela Perez Cabarcas, Maxwell Yun, Franklin Zhang

Summer 2022

Abstract 
MIT Spokes is an eight year old student-run initiative that takes MIT students on a cross-country bike ride as an effort to rethink STEM education. Along the way, the Spokes team hosts 8-hour Learning Festivals at schools, libraries, museums, and this year youthful offender facilities, encouraging young people from all across the country to engage with the scientific world around them. More specifically, Spokes targets rural communities that may not have a strong STEM curriculum in place. Our trip will lead STEM Learning Festivals in towns located in DC, Kentucky, Missouri, Kansas, Colorado, Utah, and Nevada. Moreover, our trip will defy stereotypes about what it means to be a scientist, teaching students that scientists and engineers can be athletic and engaged with their communities too. Ultimately, we hope to inspire the next generation of scientists and engineers.

Safety Statement 
Our primary safety concern is with the logistics of biking across the country; long-distance biking is never completely safe. To mitigate safety risks, we will keep a tight schedule to avoid arriving at any locations after dark. This will enable us to scout locations thoroughly before we spend the night and ensure that we don’t have to cycle in the dark either. We will also purchase and use all the biking safety gear, including helmets, reflective vests, and lighting. Along the way, we will use the van to ensure additional safety: if we are uncomfortable with riding in particular conditions, we will stop to minimize risk. We’ve discussed taking a train in parts of the country that are especially taxing on the body (i.e in the Rocky Mountains) to avoid injuries. Previous teams have compiled extensive information about the route, including difficulty, locations, and conditions along the way. As a result, we believe that the MIT Spokes trip will be safe considering the length and difficulty of prolonged strenuous activity.

Background and Motivation
Early exposure to science, technology, engineering, and math is essential to any child’s education. Unfortunately, many children in rural towns across America lack both access to STEM resources and role models to guide them to pursue their passions. STEM education in rural communities like these is imperative, and through Spokes, we will reach out directly to these communities. Because Spokes has visited many of the same communities in past years, we can see the lasting effects of our efforts: educators across the country are incredibly excited to have the program return, most of the time promising large numbers of attendees. Parents, teachers, and host families across the U.S. have reached out directly to Spokes and have shown a genuine interest in getting their kids excited about STEM. Because of Covid, Spokes has not been able to run since 2019. However, we’ve had numerous educators reach out to our team 3 years later to ask if we will be returning to host Learning Festivals. 
Finally and most importantly, many of the educators past years of Spokes have worked with reported that they’ve seen an engagement in their STEM-related curriculum increase after Spokes. We believe that the benefits of Spokes program are long-lasting and almost immeasurable; we are truly passionate about inspiring the next generation of scientists and engineers in traditionally underrepresented communities.

Project Plan
Beginning in Washington, D.C., and ending in San Francisco, California, the Spokes team will bike across the country (all 3,500 miles in 75 days) and host over ten Learning Festivals along the way. Our curriculum is designed to complement the school’s curriculum and highlight MIT’s hands-on approach to education. The team will form four pairs of instructors responsible for hosting two 2-hour workshops over the course of a day so every student has a chance to encounter each concept. We will reuse much of the material from each workshop in order to save money; in fact, many of the projects are easy to set up and break down, ensuring a quick turnover between workshops and Learning Festivals. 
This year, Spokes will be working with TEJI (The Educational Justice Institute at MIT) to bring our workshops to incarcerated youthful offenders in states along the route. In the United States, there are over 1500 youthful offender facilities, and those in these facilities often do not have access to engaging science education. TEJI provides these sorts of educational classes for incarcerated students through the inside-out program, in which incarcerated folks can take MIT classes with MIT students. Simone, one of our teammates, has worked closely with this program, serving as a TA for two years. We are working with TEJI to locate facilities to visit and adapt our learning workshops to fit the needs of these facilities. 
For most of our trip, we’ll be on the road cycling. We’ll be staying in the homes of our generous hosts whom we have contacted through either personal connections or the cycling site https://www.warmshowers.org. Many of our Warm Showers contacts have hosted past Spokes trips, and they have kindly extended offers to house our team this year. Along the way, we will forge close connections with the hosts and their communities, sharing our interest in and excitement for STEM along the way. We hope to inspire the next generation of scientists and engineers to pursue their passions with pride, and we take immense joy in the fact that we are continuing the work of past years. Because future teams will go through the same regions, we are able to see firsthand the impact of our work on the communities we visit. 
We have been interfacing closely with the MIT Student Activities Office to ensure that safety criteria are met. In addition to the safety paperwork, we have purchased all of the necessary safety equipment: helmets, lights, bright jerseys, first-aid kits, etc. are necessary for the completion of this journey.

Proposed Timeline
Our team started working on this project over this past Janurary. We’ve raised a significant portion of the funds we need to complete this trip from various MIT departments, grants, and crowdfunding. We’ve established the route we will be taking and have begun the process of reaching out to hosts along the way, as well as connecting with Learning Festivals. To date, we already have one learning festival in Hazard, KY confirmed. We’ve reached out to additional towns to set up Learning Festivals, and we’ve also had towns reach out to us since they have enjoyed Spokes visiting so much in the past. 
Our learning festival workshop topics have been selected. We will be leading workshops on 3-D printing, the physics of a car race, leaf chromatography, creating a homemade solar cell, the music of math, and philosophical paradoxes. The MIT Experimental Study Group (ESG) has offered help to practice our workshops before we embark on the trip, so we will have time to develop our teaching abilities. 
We hope to have finished our planning stage by Late May, nearly two weeks before the trip; this extra time allows us to make any last-minute adjustments and ensure that the logistics are fully confirmed before we leave from Washington, DC.
