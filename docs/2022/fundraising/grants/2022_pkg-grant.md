Community challenge
Early exposure to science, technology, engineering, and math is essential to any child’s education. Unfortunately, many children in rural towns across America lack both access to STEM resources and role models to guide them to pursue their passions. STEM education in rural communities like these is imperative, and through Spokes, we will reach out directly to these communities. Because Spokes has visited many of the same communities in past years, we can see the lasting effects of our efforts: educators across the country are incredibly excited to have the program return, most of the time promising large numbers of attendees. Parents, teachers, and host families across the U.S. have reached out directly to Spokes and have shown a genuine interest in getting their kids excited about STEM. Because of Covid, Spokes has not been able to run since 2019. However, we’ve had numerous educators reach out to our team 3 years later to ask if we will be returning to host Learning Festivals. 

This year, Spokes will be working with TEJI (The Educational Justice Institute at MIT) to bring our workshops to incarcerated youthful offenders in states along the route. In the United States, there are over 1500 youthful offender facilities, and those in these facilities often do not have access to engaging science education. TEJI provides these sorts of educational classes for incarcerated students through the inside-out program, in which incarcerated folks can take MIT classes with MIT students. One of our teammates has worked closely with this program, serving as a TA for two years. We are working with TEJI to locate facilities to visit and adapt our learning workshops to fit the needs of these facilities. 

Finally and most importantly, many of the educators past years of Spokes have worked with and reported that they’ve seen an engagement in their STEM-related curriculum increase after Spokes. We believe that the benefits of Spokes program are long-lasting and almost immeasurable; we are truly passionate about inspiring the next generation of scientists and engineers in traditionally underrepresented communities.

Work plan 
Our bike trip is meant to promote STEM education to underserved communities across the US, including juvenile offenders. There are a few components for a successful Spokes trip: fundraising for the trip, logistically planning the biking component, connecting with communities across the country, and leading engaging learning workshops. 

To touch on the first goal first: Fundraising. Spokes started fundraising in January for this trip, and so far we are about ¾ of the way to our goal of $30,000. We have contacted MIT departments, reached out to companies, and applied for grants. Simone, along with a few other members of the team, have taken a lead on fundraising. We keep track of what grants exist both internal to MIT and external, and we have applied to all of the grants pertinent to our mission. 

Secondly, the logistics of the bike trip are underway. A group of four of us (Mariela, Sophia, Franklin, and Robert), have taken the lead on crafting our route as well as contacting towns to host Learning Festivals in. Our route is nearly finished, and can be found at this link: https://www.google.com/maps/d/u/0/edit?mid=18s7MfpbjoPX_jUfx8jpLGZ-WLoe9SySc&ll=35.53495371190421%2C-103.71733575266768&z=4

And we’ve also begun contacting Learning Festivals. Franklin has reached out to all of the locations we have previously hosted festivals for, and Simone has begun the connection with juvenile offender facilities along our route. Lee Perlman, co-director of TEJI, connected us to a facility in DC that we will likely be hosting a STEM workshop for. Ironing out the details of these festivals will likely take us right up until the trip leaves. It is often hard to nail down these plans, so we are ready to be adaptable to when and where we will host festivals. 

Finally, our goal of leading engaging workshops is probably our most important. At its core, Spokes is about engaging the next generation of thinkers. We have a rough idea of a few workshops we would like to lead. They are on the topics of: the physics of a mousetrap car, 3-D printing explorations, how to DIY a solar cell, the world of leaf chromatography, and the paradoxes of philosophy. We’ve been in communication with MIT ESG who have offered to help us practice leading these workshops, or provide us with guidance for engaging students. We will measure the success of this by the response we get students. Our workshops are as hands-on as possible, and we think it will be clear if there is excitement around these activities from the students.

STEM advocacy is always something that has been extremely important to me. I’ve taken and TA’d “Inside Out” classes for the past 2 years, meaning classes where MIT students take classes with incarcerated students. I’ve observed first hand how access to education can change the trajectory of an individual's life, and I think it is so important that we spread that to those who don’t have access to science education. 

Often, the folks in juvenile offender facilities will go on to become incarcerated and will continue to not have the education access they deserve. By partnering with these facilities, the Spokes team will spread some of the excitement around the excellent education we have received while at MIT. 

Personally, I am interested in pursuing a teaching role, with a focus on the US’ incarceration system.
