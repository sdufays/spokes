Mission Statement (500/500 characters!)

MIT Spokes will bike across the US in Summer 2022, hosting educational STEM workshops along the way for middle school students. In its eighth year running, Spokes is a group of seven MIT students eager to share our enthusiasm for science, tech, engineering, and math (STEM). Over 3800 miles and 82 days, we will host hands-on STEM workshops at schools in underserved areas across the US, teaching electronics, chemistry, and physics. We hope to inspire the next generation of students to pursue STEM.

Please briefly describe the programs and services your organization provides.
MIT Spokes is an 8-year old completely student-run group. Biking across the United States, we seek to inspire students in rural communities through our STEM-based workshops. Our exploratory mens-et-manus-inspired workshops spring from our own experiences at MIT. Workshops at our learning festivals include making solar cells from berry juice, puzzling through the great paradoxes of philosophy, building circuits, and exploring the world of 3D-printing. We plan on hosting twelve Learning Festivals across the country, and attendance has ranged from 30 to 100 students per Learning Festival. Because MIT Spokes often returns to the same rural and underrepresented communities, educators across the country are excited to have us, often promising large numbers of attendees. The MIT Spokes project ultimately hopes to connect to students across the US in an adventurous, educational, and exciting nature. 

How does your organization prioritize building an inclusive workplace? Please describe your approach. *
Many rural towns across America are underrepresented in STEM fields because children often lack access to both STEM resources and role models to guide them to pursue their passions. MIT Spokes aims to improve representation in STEM from rural areas by hosting twelve STEM "Learning Festivals" across the US, especially in rural and underserved areas. We aim to inspire middle-school students by leading hands-on workshops in fields ranging from 3D-printing to material science. Because Spokes has visited many of the same communities in past years, we can see the lasting effects of our efforts: educators across the country are incredibly excited to have the program return, often promising large numbers of attendees. Parents, teachers, and host families across the U.S. have reached out directly to Spokes and have shown a genuine interest in getting their kids excited about STEM.

This year, Spokes will be working with TEJI (The Educational Justice Institute at MIT) to bring our workshops to incarcerated youthful offenders in states along the route. In the United States, there are over 1500 youthful offender facilities, and those in these facilities often do not have access to engaging science education. TEJI provides these sorts of educational classes for incarcerated students through the inside-out program, in which incarcerated folks can take MIT classes with MIT students. One of our teammates has worked closely with this program, serving as a TA for two years. We are working with TEJI to locate facilities to visit and adapt our learning workshops to fit the needs of these facilities.

We would like you to share some recent program highlights and success stories. Please describe a program or initiative your organization has successfully executed in the past.

MIT Spokes has run successful trips for eight years continuously, excluding 2020 and 2021 due to the COVID-19 pandemic. Over the years, we've interacted with thousands of students in rural and underserved areas across the US, as well as established long-standing relationships with local schools and institutions. For example, we have been teaching at the Challenger Learning Center in Hazard, KY since our inception in 2013. We consider our program to be successful if we inspire students across the US to pursue STEM. Given the overwhelming positive feedback from parents and learning festival hosts, we believe we have made a positive impact in rural areas.

Is your organization managed by a board of directors, council or committee? Please describe.

MIT Spokes is completely student run by the seven members of our team. We are an on-campus student club and have administrative support from the MIT Edgerton Center.

Akamai corporate volunteers can help staff our learning festivals and share personal anecdotes about working in a STEM field.

What cities/regions does your program serve?

Along our 82-day, 3800-mile route, we will host 12 Learning Festivals. Workshops have a typical attendance of 30-100 students. The 2022 schedule includes:

* Boston, MA for incarcerated youth
* Washington, DC for incarcerated youth
* Louisa County, VA
* Hazard, KY
* Bowling Green, KY
* St. Louis, MO
* Newton, KS
* Garden City, KS
* Denver, CO

* Inspire students across the United States to pursue STEM

How will you measure success of predicted outcomes?

Our program intends to inspire the next generation of scientists and engineers who may otherwise have never considered exploring these fields. We consider ourselves successful if we’ve inspired students to further pursue STEM, and maximized our engagement with people along our trip. Spokes has successfully, safely run trips for seven years, and we hope we can continue this program well into the future.

What is your plan to eliminate barriers to access and use of technology for your STEM program participants? Please explain.

Our Learning Festival workshops are very technology-forward, including interactive exercises like programming and designing 3d-printed parts. Our team carries a small fleet of donated Chromebook laptops for students to use at our Learning Festivals. As representatives of MIT traveling across the US, we aim to lower technology barriers for students across the country.
