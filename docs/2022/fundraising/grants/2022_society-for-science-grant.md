Grant closes May 17, 2022

What is your organization's mission?

MIT Spokes will bike across the US in Summer 2022, hosting educational STEM workshops along the way for middle school students. In its eighth year running, Spokes is a group of eight MIT students eager to share our enthusiasm for science, technology, engineering, and math (STEM). Over 3000 miles and 70 days, we will stop at various schools across the US and lead hands-on, interactive workshops to get kids excited about STEM -- in particular, electronics, computer science, chemistry, and physics. We reach students in rural towns who often do not have early exposure to STEM and visit many of the same communities as in past years, leaving a lasting impact. We hope to inspire the next generation of students to pursue STEM, as well as encourage them to stay athletic and active from coast to coast.

Provide a brief narrative to describe the core programs and activities of the organization.*

The Spokes team will be biking from Washington D.C to San Francisco to engage with students in rural communities this summer. We will stop at schools, libraries, and camps as we travel throughout the country to hold ten free Learning Festivals where we will teach hands-on, project-based workshops inspired by our passions in STEM. Students range from elementary to middle-school students, and workshops include introductory electronics, chemistry, philosophy, and 3D-printing, among other topics. Workshops have anywhere from 30 to 100 attendees each. We hope to inspire students to pursue STEM and higher education, as well as encourage them to stay athletic and active from coast to coast.

Approximately how many people will this organization or project serve?

We estimate serving at least 500 students as we cross the US. There can be anywhere between 30 - 100 attendees per Learning Festival, with 10 Festivals on our trip.

Are you able to sustain your work without this grant? Please explain why this funding is needed.

The STEM Action Grant would cover educational supplies for our workshops, bike gear for each participant, and travel and lodging expenses along our route. We have raised approximately $25,000 out of our $37,000 budget -- we have almost enough money to complete the trip, but not enough for all of our educational supplies, or emergencies and bike maintenance.

Describe how the funds will be used.

Funding will cover educational supplies, bikes and bike gear for each participant, travel and lodging expenses for the trip (we will mainly camp or stay at hosts' houses), as well as renting a support vehicle to follow the group. Educational supplies include chemicals for an interactive solar-cell building activity, film and developing supplies for a camera obscura activity, and 3d-printed parts for an introductory 3d-printing workshop.

What are the goals of the project or use of funds? In one year, how will you measure the success of the project?*

We aim to inspire students in underserved parts of the US, and we measure our success by the longstanding relationships we've established with communities. We have been teaching at the Challenger Learning Center in Hazard, KY for over five years, as well as other schools around the US. We're simultaneously exploring different teaching opportunities -- we will be partnering with the MIT Educational Justice Institute to teach at juvenile incarceration facilities in Washington DC. Ultimately, we measure our success by the students we teach; we consider ourselves successful if we've inspired a student to think about, even pursue, STEM in their academic careers. We also plan to establish MIT Spokes as a long-running organization and will help establish and prepare the Spokes 2023 cohort for their journey!

How is this project or organization entrepreneurial? Was this program created because you saw a need in your community?*

MIT Spokes was created in 2013 to serve underrepresented students in STEM fields. In rural places all across the United States, STEM is traditionally not emphasized as a career option, and students have few role models in STEM to look up to. We believe that hosting workshops with fun, interactive content can be the spark that ignites an interest in STEM. Simultaneously, we will be biking across the United States, an opportunity to inspire students to stay physically fit and show that engineers can also be athletes. Spokes was established to fill this niche: inspiring students in rural places, as we bike across the United States.

What is the timeline for implementation?*

Our trip is planned to leave Washington DC on June 1 and arrive in San Francisco on August 20. Along the way, we will host ten Learning Festivals at towns and cities spread across the United States. In the meantime, we've finalized our route and are planning workshop curricula.

Our workshops are:
* May 31: Washington DC
* June 3: Louisa County, VT
* June 15: Hazard, KY
* June 22: Bowling Green, KY
* June 30: St. Louis, MO
* July 11: Newton, KS
* July 21: Denver, CO
* August 6: Cedar City, UT
* August 10: Ely, NV
* August 14: Fallon, NV

How will this project support the Society for Science mission.*

We aim to inspire interest in science, technology, engineering, and math (STEM) in rural and underserved places across the United States. We will host interactive, fun activities for middle-school-aged students in fields ranging from materials science, electrical engineering, mechanical engineering, and even philosophy. We align with Society for Science's mission of "promoting the understanding and appreciation of science and the vital role it plays in human advancement: to inform, educate, and inspire"

How will the Society's participation as a sponsor be acknowledged?*

We will recognize Society for Science on our team apparel, as well as our publicity media and website. We would also be proud to recognize Society for Science on our social media (Instagram: @mitspokes). We could also spread word about STEM competitions like MASTERS to our middle-school workshops, to raise awareness of the competitions in rural and underserved areas.
