Congratulations, Spokes 2022! 
Planning
Split up tasks ASAP. 2019 had 3 people on fundraising, 1 person on gear/bikes, 2 people on route, and 2 people arranging learning festivals (though each person/pair plans what they teach). For a task list, check out How To Plan.
Packing
What should I bring?
As little as possible! It has to fit in the car along with 7 other people’s stuff, camping gear, teaching equipment, bike repair tools, food, and everything else you need to live for 2-3 months. 

We should all take inspiration from Evan, who packed by far the lightest in the 2019 group and shared what he brought in this blog: https://mitspokes.wixsite.com/2019/post/day-48-packing-light (note Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance, which several members of several consecutive Spokes teams have bought independently. It’s a good read.) Additionally, past teams have compiled packing lists: Spokes 2019 Spokes 2018. 
What gear is provided?
You will fundraise for your bikes, biking shoes, and one set of biking clothes (jersey, padded bike shorts, gloves, sunglasses).

Previous spokes teams have tents, sleeping bags, sleeping pads, a coleman stove, bike tools, a bike rack for three bikes (no car hitch needed), bins, a cooler, and miscellaneous things. 
A Day in the Life
What does a “typical” day look like?
While the day-to-day specifics are up to your team, you can expect a typical day to look something like this.
For the cyclists:
0600-0700: Wake up, eat breakfast, pack up things, prep bikes, get ready for the day. Generally, the earlier you can get out, the better. It’s less windy and hot in the mornings. This is the time to plan out riding groups and rest stops, if you haven’t already.
0700-1300ish: Ride bikes! Remember to refuel every hour or so. If it will be longer than an hour between towns, plan to either bring snacks on the bike or have a van-sponsored rest stop at a reasonable point. Eat a good lunch if you ride past lunch. If you’re curious about how long rides were in the past, Delia (Spokes 2018) logged a bunch of them on Strava for a viewer’s entertainment.
1300-Bedtime: Don’t ride bikes! Help set up camp, make dinner, do bike maintenance, read books, take a nap, stretch, blog, explore the area, hang out with your host family, talk about the next day’s route, etc.
For the person driving the van, your responsibilities are:
Prepare the campsite if camping.
If needed, break down the campsite after a quick morning.
Lead or follow the cyclists and stop for rest stops if needed.
Do any miscellaneous required tasks, like grocery shopping, laundry, bike maintenance, etc.
Be nearby and ready in the event of an emergency!
Enjoy the time by yourself

(2018 had one person drive per day, and started earlier in general with shorter breaks. 2019 had two drivers that switched at lunch, started later in the day in the beginning, and would take a longer lunch break)

As you can see, a day being a Spoke is remarkably simple. Your only job is to get from Point A to Point B, with the fun side quest of meeting new people and talking to them, if you so choose. It’s very fun.
Fundraising
What am I responsible for paying for?
You are responsible for paying for bike apparel outside the one set that Spokes provides, leisure activities on rest days, personal hygiene items/medicine, as well as your return flight from SFO to Boston. 

Who should I ask for money?
MIT departments, grants, crowdfunding, commercial sponsors, etc. If you think someone might want to give you money, you should ask for it! Sandy Lipnoski of the Edgerton Center is your go-to for questions about how to use the MIT fundraising platform (and she will help you get a purchasing card, set up the Budget rentals)

How much to fundraise?
Our budget was approximately $30,000, to cover the bikes + gear, rental car, travel expenses, and educational supplies for Learning Festivals. You should try to raise that much, and pass on any extra budget from past years to future years.
Timeline
How long is the trip?
The trip should take your entire summer. If you are graduating, you can choose to stick around for graduation and then fly out to meet with the team, skip graduation and start riding with the team, or something else entirely—it’s up to y’all. Here is the Spokes 2019 route, which has the start and end dates, locations, and the day that Evan (a graduating senior) joined up with the team. The gap between finals and graduation is also a little smaller than it used to be.

When should we start fundraising?
ASAP. Seriously, do this as quickly as possible. It should be the first thing on your mind, and you should probably designate two - three people to take point on this (and keep each other accountable). This is a huge responsibility, and everyone should be involved, but it helps to have two people who know who’s been reached out to. 

When should we get bikes?
As soon as you have the money. This will likely be your first major purchase. 

How should we get bikes?
It’s a bit challenging to buy bikes at the moment. Both Spokes 2018 and Spokes 2019 bought a fleet of Jamis Renegades from Cambridge Bicycles, but this is NOT a recommended approach. Cambridge Bicycles is a crappy bike shop, and we no longer like them. If you’re feeling adventurous, you can acquire bikes on Craigslist, Facebook Marketplace, or other bike swap websites. There also are many other bike shops throughout the Boston area.

Spokes 2022 bought Giant Revolt 2 bikes ($1400) from Landry’s Bicycles in Boston. Landry’s is an excellent bike shop - they helped us size bikes, gave us plenty of advice, and were friendly people in general. Landry’s gave us a 15% discount on gear, but not the bikes themselves. The Giant Revolt bikes were great bikes that suited us pretty well for the trip. They are a bit pricey; you should target bikes in the $1000-$1200 range instead. We also had a very limited selection of new bikes + inflated prices due to the pandemic bike shortage, but are glad we got these bikes.

It’s also important to shop around and get quotes for bikes from multiple bike shops – Landry’s, Cambridge Bike, Ace Wheelworks, Papa Wheelies – and see which bike shop will give you the best deal.

Desired Bike Characteristics:
Disc Brakes (they work much better in the rain)
Big rear cassette (makes climbing hills easier)
Consider getting a 2x8 or better drivetrain – two front gears, eight+ in the rear

Essential Bike Characteristics:
Drop Bars (the fancy, road bike bars)
Buy clip-in SPD pedals! The Shimano PD-EH500 is a good choice because it has clips on one side and a conventional flat pedal on the other side.

Route
Where should I stay?
Warmshowers is the best! Contact potential hosts about 2-4 weeks ahead of time and remind them 2 days before, so if they cancel you have time to find somewhere else. You will have to devote time to this in the afternoons throughout the trip. 2 people shared this duty in the 2019 group to make sure we were covered. 

After Missouri, warmshowers became uncommon outside of major cities. You can start camping, asking churches or fire stations, or community centers for places to stay. Smaller towns seem to be nicer about more unusual places. You can also stay with family friends if you know anyone in the city. 

How do I plan the route?
You can use old routes as a guide or throw them out the window. I’d start by deciding an overall route (north (popular for cyclists, more isolated than middle), middle (standard for spokes), south (kinda hot, would recommend against)). Then figure out if there are any learning festivals you really want to do, or go to a particular city or national park. Then connect the dots together day by day with good spacing to sleep + good roads along the way. 

See 2019 Route details for 2020 for detailed, day to day notes on the 2019 route. Also, the Adventure Cycling Association has a wealth of routes and maps that are pretty cool.

How to Ride a Bike
How should I prepare?
While cycling experience is absolutely not necessary to do Spokes, it’s best to (a) buy your bikes as quickly as possible, and (b) get as much time in the saddle (fancy word for bike seat) as possible before doing Spokes. These miles should be very, very easy—you’re not trying to set any land speed records, you’re just trying to build a fitness base, get used to using clips, and figure out how to mitigate pain points on the bike. 

What should I do while riding?
Here are some dos and don’ts for group rides:
DO use hand signals to indicate where you’re going. Also, point (with your hand) to road hazards, so the people behind you can see them.
DO keep a constant pace so you can draft and feel comfortable riding inches behind the person in front without being afraid of hitting them. 
DON’T send out your slowest person in the front of the group. Instead, put them somewhere in the middle so that they can draft behind a stronger rider. If you are the slowest person, make sure to communicate when a gap opens up so that the person in front of you can slow down. This is much more energy efficient, and as you practice, you’ll get stronger and more effective at riding in a group! Look back often to make sure you are together. 
DO trade off taking “pulls” at the front. This means that you can spread out the load of eating all that wind.
DON’T ride alone. Groups of 2-4 are the best, as they increase road visibility but aren’t a hindrance to other vehicles on the road. 
DO use Chamois Butt’r (or similar anti-chafe cream) before you think you need it. Also, if something on your bike is causing you pain, don’t hesitate to make small adjustments to the “fit.” The best way to know how to adjust your fit is by getting miles on the bike before Spokes. 
DO take the time to stretch and roll out. Overuse injuries are pretty common during Spokes. If something feels wrong, it’s probably not the best idea to ride through it. Take some time off the bike as the van driver, and things will sort themselves out!
