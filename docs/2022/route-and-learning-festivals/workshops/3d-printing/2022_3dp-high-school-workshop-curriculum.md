Definitions
Translating a digital file into solid 3D objects, via depositing, fusing, or joining material under computer control
New type of manufacturing
Subtractive manufacturing (conventional). Material is removed from a solid block of material via lathing, milling, etc.

Additive manufacturing - adding material together to become the final object, just like building things out of Legos

Hardware
Parts are made layer by layer. Imagine slicing, say, an apple, into infinitely thin slices.
FDM
Melt plastic then squirt it out a nozzle, depositing it in layers
Pass around FDM Benchy’s
SLA
Vat of light-sensitive resin that polymerizes / becomes a plastic when exposed to light. Selectively expose the resin to light, layer by layer
Better surface finish, precision than FDM
Pass around Tough springs, millifluidics models, architectural models
SLS
Start off with a vat of powder. Fuse the powder together using laser, layer by layer
Pass around the Formlabs briefcase, bike seat + show the Rubik’s cube
Binder-jet printing
Start off with a vat of powder. Deposit a binder material, layer by layer, to bind the powder together. Post-treatment
Pass around DM wrench, bracelet
Material-jet printing
Much like your inkjet printer, a printhead deposits light-sensitive resin and scans it, layer by layer. Most expensive printing technology but really really good quality + multi-material

What type of printer is my printer?
Explain types of FDM printers
Conventional X/Y: larger build area, but slow b/c heavy moving parts
Delta printer: smaller build area, but fast b/c lightweight! 1.8x faster than the comparable Monoprice X/Y printer
Consider doing the shuttle run (PE drill) as an example. It takes a lot of energy to start up/slow down, and you can’t instantaneously accelerate
