Safety Plan

*Only solar cell workshop needs safety plan

Title: Making Solar Cells
Description: Students learn the basic working principle of dye-sensitized solar cells and build their own solar cell using titanium dioxide sensitized with berry juice. We will compare this solar cell with commercial silicon solar cells. We will conclude with a discussion about the importance of renewable energy.
Age Range: 7th-12th grade
Primary Contact: Sophia Fang
Safety Plan: Students will wear gloves and safety glasses to avoid staining clothes and hands with electrolyte, and to avoid TiO2 nanoparticles getting on their hands. A binder of SDS safety sheets of all chemicals will be available.

Safety Concern | Mitigation
Transporting chemicals, and potential spills during transport and throughout the workshops (chemicals: potassium iodide/iodine electrolyte in ethylene glycol, titanium dioxide suspension in vinegar, isopropanol, vinegar spray bottles) | Chemical bottles are labeled and safety data sheets are in the clear storage bin. Will check that caps are fully closed before putting bottles away. Chemicals will be stored upright inside secondary containment (jar or bin) with spill pads in the clear storage bin. Spills will be cleaned up right away with spill pads or paper towels, wearing gloves and safety glasses. The small bin will be used to store the chemical bottles during the workshops.
Chemical exposure | Instructors and students will wear safety glasses and disposable gloves. Dirty gloves will be replaced with new gloves.
Iodine/potassium iodide electrolyte (in ethylene glycol) | Contained in a dropper bottle. Gloves protect from staining. For middle schoolers, instructors will handle all steps with the electrolyte. The electrolyte can leak out of the finished solar cells, so we will super glue it. We will talk to parents and teachers to check if it’s okay for students to take the solar cells home.
Candle flame | Instructors will keep distance from students while using candle flame to deposit carbon on the glass slides. Candles will be used away from any chemicals, paper, cardboard, and other combustible items to prevent fires. Instructors will hold slides with tweezers to prevent burns. Will wait for slides to cool before giving back to the students. Candles will be blown out immediately when finished.
Candle flame or hot air gun | Instructors will keep distance from students while using candle flame or hot air gun to heat TiO2 film on glass slides. Candles will be used away from any chemicals, paper, cardboard, and other combustible items to prevent fires. Instructors will hold slides with tweezers to prevent burns. Will wait for slides to cool before giving back to the students. Candles will be blown out immediately when finished.
Nanocrystalline TiO2 (danger of inhalation from dried out nanoparticles) | Instructors will drop TiO2 paste and wipe away any dried particles with vinegar. Glass slides used to spread TiO2 paste will be thrown away into a waste container before leftover paste dries. Tape will be peeled off while the TiO2 film is still wet and before heating to minimize powder formation. Stock bottle of TiO2 will be cleaned using vinegar (especially the outside, rim, and lid) to minimize powder. Spatulas will be cleaned with vinegar before the paste dries.
Ingestion of contaminated berries | Students will be instructed that the berries are not to be eaten.
