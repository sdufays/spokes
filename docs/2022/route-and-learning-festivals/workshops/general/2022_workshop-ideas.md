The Mathematics of Music Workshop
Description: Harmony and rhythm. Students learn about harmony and its connections with the harmonic series through making noises around and with a spectrum analyzer. With this powerful transform-based tool, students can visualize music and notice how timbre and related musical textures lead to patterns on the graph. To learn about rhythm, students can play with a drum machine on a website link while tapping out rhythms with their hands and feet. The timing of the different movements is often linked with dance forms, which is a neat cultural practice that has continued since the dawn of humanity.
Materials:
No phones: 
My iPad and a focus on voices and the physicality of rhythms
Phones:
Students use their phone or pair up with someone who has one
Spectrum Analyzer website (two options): 
musiclab.chromeexperiments.com/spectrogram
spectrogram.sciencemusic.org
Drum Machine website:
musicca.com/drum-machine
Teachers:
Rob, 
Proposed Audience: Highschool students with an interest in music
Make more hands on by building instrument.

Rubber Band and Mouse Trap Car Race
Description: Teams build a race car and compete in various tasks. Teach students about different physics concepts that can be leveraged to build a car. Potential Energy, viscous air friction, momentum, springs, mechanical leverage. Task examples: a) fastest car b) most endurance c) closest to a specified distance. 
Materials: Cardboard, glue, tape, bottle caps, CDs, rubber bands, mouse traps, chopsticks, scissors.
Teachers: Franklin, Simone
Proposed Audience: High School Physics Students 

Hands-on Analog Photography
Description: Students learn to build a pin-hole camera, take pictures, and develop film using either safer chemicals (instant coffee, baking soda, vitamin C, water) or other chemicals to obtain more permanent images. Students will learn about the optics behind a pin-hole camera as well as the chemistry in developing film - so physics and chemistry!!
Materials: 
For camera: matchbox, roll of 35mm film (100-200 speed is ideal), empty roll of 35mm film, some thin cardboard (the box the new film comes in is fine), aluminum soda can, black electric tape, plastic from a spiral binder, nail/sewing pin, scissors, exacto knife, black sharpie
For developing film: instant coffee, baking soda, crushed vitamin C, water OR sodium thiosulfate crystals and water
References: https://www.learnfilm.photography/how-to-teach-your-kids-film-photography/
Teachers: Sophia
Proposed audience: Middle-schoolers OR high-schoolers depending on chemicals used

Make a Spectrometer (+Question It)!
Description: Learn what light is made of, how different wavelengths of light combine to produce white light, and make your own homemade spectrometer. Then, we can point the spectrometer at different ~fun~ light sources to see what wavelengths of light compose that light source. I think it would also be fun to incorporate some sort of philosophical questioning (ie, is this really what light is made of? How do we know? Should we trust our homemade spectrometers? Or should light just be deemed the color we see it as?) just for my own touch. 
Materials:
To build: CD, scissors/box knife, empty cereal box of some sorts, protractor
Fun targets: Different lights (neon, plasma, fluorescent, black light, flame, etc)
References: https://www.madaboutscience.com.au/shop/science-extra/post/homemade-spectrometer
Teachers: Keaten
Proposed Audience: Middle School or High School

13. Leaf Chromatography Art Project: The Hidden Pigments in Plants!
Description: During the summer, leaves we see are generally bright green due to their high chlorophyll content. But leaves have more colorful pigments that are hidden by the chlorophyll. We would go on an outdoor adventure to collect leaves, and then crush leaves up in rubbing alcohol. Once you insert a coffee filter/paper towel into the leaves, the colors will ascend up the paper and separate into different color bands. We can talk about why different colors travel different distances (the size of the pigment), as well as learn about photosynthesis. Also could possibly turn the paper chromatography strips into an art project!
Materials: 
Leaves (collected or brought)
Rubbing alcohol 
Cups 
Paper towel/coffee filters
Scissors, tape, art supplies 
Teachers: Simone 
Proposed Audience: Elementary Schoolers

15. Rube Goldberg Machines 
Description: Rube Goldberg machines are really complicated contraptions to perform a simple task. This activity is all about the engineering design process (what goes into a good design?). In small groups, students could come up with a simple task they want to accomplish (ie. moving a marble, making a paper airplane fly, filling a cup with water, etc.), then they will design a machine that could do this (keeping in mind the materials we have, and trying to include at least 3 simple machines). Students can then build their machines and test them initially. Then students can make adjustments/redesign to meet their goal if they didn’t, and then we can have a rube goldberg machine showcase. 
Materials
hot glue, construction paper, marbles, cups, paper towel tubes, string, paper clips, rubber bands, pipes, duct tape, blocks, dominos, funnels, balls, aluminum foil, plastic containers, cardboard, water bottles, toilet paper, beverage cans 
I know this is a lot on the materials list, but maybe we can ask the school/families to set aside recycling materials to make this more environmentally friendly!? Try to use as many recycled materials as possible 
Teachers: Simone 
Proposed Audience: Middle Schoolers-Early High Schoolers
