Station 1: Bubbles 

Goal: Capture color in bubbles!

Instructions:
Dip your hands in the soapy water and blow bubbles
Have your partner take a picture of your bubble, try to capture as much color in the photo as possible. 
Experiment with different amounts of soap, water, and bubbles for this. 

Tips: Recommended to take pictures/look at bubbles on a darker background, and shine a flashlight on the edges or hold the bubbles up to a light. 

Questions to answer:
What colors do you see in the bubbles? Do you NOT see any colors?
Is this an example of structural color or pigmented color?
Station 2: Marker Chromatography

Goal: Separate pigments in markers using strips of paper and a solution. 

Instructions:
Draw a line of marker along a strip of paper
Place the strip of paper into the solution that the markers are in front of (Crayola goes in water, Caliart goes in rubbing alcohol)
Watch the pigments separate!

Tips: Keep marker line above the water/rubbing alcohol (RA). It works better when the water/RA can travel up the strip of paper, the marker shouldn’t be getting into the cup. 

Questions to answer:
What pigments make up the black crayola marker? 
What pigments make up the orange Caliart marker? 
Any other interesting pigment combinations? 
 Is this an example of structural color or pigmented color?
Station 3: Interference Paint 

Goal: Compare interference paint on white vs black vs colored backgrounds.  

Instructions: 
Draw a square of black on your paper
Draw some other colored squares 
Using a very small drop of interference paint, paint over the black background, colored background, and white background
Compare the results (you can repeat this with different colors of interference paint)

Questions to answer:
Do colors appear better on black backgrounds or white? Why is this? (Hint: what types of light does black paper reflect? What about white paper?)
Is this an example of structural color or pigmented color? 
HARD QUESTION: If the ingredients in these different colors of interference paint are the exact same (Mica and TiO2), what could be creating the different colors? (Hint: It may have something to do with the structure….)
