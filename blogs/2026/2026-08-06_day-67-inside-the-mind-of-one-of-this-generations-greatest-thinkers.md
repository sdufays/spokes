# Day 67: Inside the Mind of One of This Generation's Greatest Thinkers

> **2026** · 2026-08-06 · Eureka, NV → Mt. Airy, NV · 90 mi · 3698 ft climbing
> Aarushi's voice-memo day on Highway 50 from Eureka, about 90 miles (3,698 ft), toward Mt. Airy, NV. The day included a sandy cattle-track detour the riders walked, a wrong turn, and a wildfire in the area. The team camped at a church and RV park concrete pad in Austin, NV, and left at 10 pm to ride overnight.
> 🏠 **Slept:** Church and RV park campsite (concrete pad), Austin, NV
> - 📌 Highway 50 from Eureka to Austin has about 75 miles with no services. Carry food and water.
> - 📌 The sandy cattle-track detour off Highway 50 was not rideable on bikes; tires sank into the sand. Stay on pavement.
> - 📌 Austin, NV has one restaurant (Grandma's). Pizza took over two hours on a busy night, so call ahead.
> - 📌 Austin campsite: concrete pad, one shower and one outlet shared by the team. Plan charging.
> - 📌 Overnight on Highway 50: cars are visible about 5 minutes out. Calling out 'CAR' and riding single file on the shoulder worked.

Eureka, NV to ~~Austin, NV — 75 mi, 3038 ft~~ ~Mt. Airy, NV — 90 mi, 3698 ft

I've heard there is much anticipation for this blog to drop, and for good reason. Today I will share with you every single thought I had while biking 90 miles. This might be an incredibly boring blog or it might be the most fascinating one, depending on how much you care about great thinkers and deep philosophers and cool funny smart hot people (me) and the like. Regardless, this will be the most accurate blog, because whether fascinating or boring, this is what we actually we do all day. Nothing but the thoughts in my head for 67 days straight… I thought surely I should be enlightened by now. But why do I feel the exact same? Has no profound change, no grand realization, no heavenly moment of enlightenment come upon me by now?? What the heck do I think about all day???? Let's dig in.

In the morning, nothing notable happened. It was like every other morning after another extremely mediocre night of floor sleep and slathering chamois butter on our butts and asking ourselves what the heck are we doing, followed by a very sad breakfast of Nature Valley Bar. We set off at 9:30 AM. On the first leg, I was thinking about:

Without plant life to recycle oxygen in the atmosphere, free oxygen and the ozone layer will disappear from the atmosphere allowing for intense levels of deadly UV light to reach the surface. Animals in food chains that were dependent on live plants will disappear shortly afterward. At most, animal life could survive about 3 to 100 million years after plant life dies out. Extinction will start with large animals, then smaller animals and flying creatures, then amphibians, followed by reptiles and, finally, invertebrates. In the book _The Life and Death of Planet Earth_ , authors [Peter D. Eventually, however, all multicellular life will die out. The first sea animals to go extinct will be large fish, followed by small fish and then, finally, invertebrates. The last animals to go extinct will be animals that do not depend on living plants, such as [termites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termite), or those near [hydrothermal vents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrothermal_vent), such as [worms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worm) of the genus _[Riftia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riftia)_. The only life left on the Earth after this will be [single-celled organisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-celled_organisms). A 2026 study by Haq-Misra and Wolf suggests that those plants that use [CAM photosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAM_photosynthesis), like [cacti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacti) could survive until 1.84 billion years from now.”

This situation all makes more sense when you realize that Austin, Nevada is considered a living ghost town with a population of 167. It's technically not even a town but an unincorporated census-designated place, meaning that it has no local government or legal boundaries—it's just a named collection of people living in proximity. Like many towns and former towns in Nevada, it was founded during the silver rush around the time of the Civil War. Settlers flocked, its population boomed, railroads were built to connect it with the transcontinental, and by the time the railroad finished, it was all over, and the city was disincorporated in 1881. The people still left are mostly miners and ex-miners.
