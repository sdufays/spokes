Hello from St. Louis, the edge of Missouri. We’ve been pretty quiet on this blog for the past couple of days, so I figured it’s time for a blog post. Some readers may remember me as the person who was afraid of bicycles at the beginning of the trip. You may also have been wondering if I was going to make it this far. Don’t worry– I had my doubts too. But so far so good: We are halfway through the trip, over 2000 miles across America, and I am proud to say that I have seemed to have gotten the hang of riding one now. I won’t say I’m great at it yet, but I managed to make it over the Rockies and that has to count for something right?

We had a great teaching stop in Atchison, Kansas, which was the home of Amelia Earhart. There was the Amelia Earhart festival while we were there. No, it was not a coincidence. Yes, we planned to be there and crossed Kansas in 4 days in order to make it. But it was so worth the butt pain! We met lots of cool street performers and volunteers. But more importantly, we got to run a learning festival booth in the kid’s section. 

![image](http://33.media.tumblr.com/33d89158fdb8435a42309f8cd15fafd8/tumblr_inline_n98n82hU5C1s3e7lq.jpg)

  
Even more importantly, Cali and I made friends with a snake!

So far, Missouri has not been too different from Kansas: there are still a ton of cornfields and soybeans (which I really enjoy seeing). However, MO is much much hiller than Kansas! Cali and I made a great call to stay on a state route instead of taking the scenic county road and we managed to miss quiet a few very steep and very blind hills. We didn’t miss much variation in the scenery either.

The past few days have been eventful: we have been riding on the Katy Trail State Park, which is made of crushed limestone. At first, we were worried that our road bike tires would not hold up, but we only had one flat on that bike path. Additionally, we have our second guest rider: Turner! The mastermind behind Spokes from last year’s team. He fit right in with us and it’s been exciting to have an additional cycling companion.

![image](http://38.media.tumblr.com/069f1fcb6fb49c25b86c750464c312f9/tumblr_inline_n98n8wuXf81s3e7lq.jpg)

The station we stayed at on the Katy Trail.

Tomorrow, we have a rest day in St. Louis, which I am very excited about. Apparently there is a “museum” around here that is essentially a giant playground. I’m positive that we will have a great time there.

Jamin
