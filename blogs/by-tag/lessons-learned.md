### 2013-03-24 — [Educating Child Prodigies](../2013/2013-03-24_educating-child-prodigies.md)
The author taught an engineering class at MIT Spark to five 6th and 7th graders using the Helios kit the team planned to build on the trip, and reported what went wrong in prep: late-arriving parts, a part-fit failure, and borrowing tools at the last minute.
- 📌 Order parts at least three weeks ahead, especially servo motors from Hobbyking: very cheap but slow, and customer service is weak. *(2013)*
- 📌 Fit-test every component before drilling or cutting; published dimensions were not enough, and six hours of prep were wasted when shafts did not fit the holes. *(2013)*
- 📌 Critical components go out of stock and designs need last-minute changes (pulleys were unavailable), so build slack into the schedule. *(2013)*
- 📌 Keep spare box cutters, clamps and small parts on hand; the team had to borrow them at the last minute. *(2013)*
