# Educating Child Prodigies

> **2013** · 2013-03-24
> The author taught an engineering class at MIT Spark to five 6th and 7th graders using the Helios kit the team planned to build on the trip, and reported what went wrong in prep: late-arriving parts, a part-fit failure, and borrowing tools at the last minute.
> 🧪 **Learning festival:** Not a Spokes Learning Festival: a test run of the Helios hardware class at MIT Spark (ESP) with five 6th-7th graders, supported by TAs Roger and Netia.
> - 📌 Order parts at least three weeks ahead, especially servo motors from Hobbyking: very cheap but slow, and customer service is weak.
> - 📌 Fit-test every component before drilling or cutting; published dimensions were not enough, and six hours of prep were wasted when shafts did not fit the holes.
> - 📌 Critical components go out of stock and designs need last-minute changes (pulleys were unavailable), so build slack into the schedule.
> - 📌 Keep spare box cutters, clamps and small parts on hand; the team had to borrow them at the last minute.
> Tags: curriculum, gear, fundraising, local-people, lessons-learned, place:massachusetts

[Educating Child Prodigies](http://spokesamerica.tumblr.com/post/46143892239/educating-child-prodigies "Educating Child Prodigies")

MIT’s Spark happened this past Saturday, and it was great. I had the opportunity to teach engineering skills to five amazingly motivated 6th and 7th grade students, and was successful in engaging them with the material and completing the class without a single injury.

![](http://38.media.tumblr.com/1f646865da267b4e5f6128f3254c0b8d/tumblr_inline_mk5kjpx8AC1qz4rgp.png)  


I also learned a great deal about the user friendliness of Helios, the kit that will be assembled during our trip this summer, and how to plan a new hardware class.

The first of our lessons is to order materials three weeks in advance of when they are needed… especially if they are servo motors from Hobbyking.

![](http://33.media.tumblr.com/7cc3d5331e503d9be395f8c8d4a453a2/tumblr_inline_mk5kknRms91qz4rgp.jpg)

This supplier is tops as far as providing rock bottom prices, but their customer service is nebulous.

Also, “critical” components will often be out of stock and designs will need last minute changes, as we learned with the absence of sdp-si’s conveniently sized pulleys.

![](http://38.media.tumblr.com/0c1588474e416f3fc8e3b6db68f9ac72/tumblr_inline_mk5klgCCWv1qz4rgp.jpg)

More important than ordering early is to take the time to do a fit test of all components. Naivety had us assume that parts would fit because of their published dimensions… but we were “gently” reminded of the importance of tolerance bands when our shafts refused to fit the holes we made. This was a downer, considering that we spent 6 hours preparing parts that couldn’t be used. 

![](http://33.media.tumblr.com/62437eff0f92d14caa9e7ff0feb281c0/tumblr_inline_mk5kmhsTN81qz4rgp.png)

A wise and grumpy shop wizard once said, “If you don’t have time to do it right, then you don’t have time to do it twice.”

![](http://38.media.tumblr.com/d97a9e5993bcb6693546b0e07eb9a9bd/tumblr_inline_mk5knrrHCr1qz4rgp.jpg)

…he was right.

Finally, I learned that people care enough about education to give of themselves. In the eleventh hour, I found myself short two box cutters, a table clamp and $70 worth of servo motors (darn you Hobbyking!!!). I was shocked by how quickly people lent out these items for nothing more in return than a knock on the door, a distressed smile, and a request to help me inspire some youngsters.

On this note, thank you to the two generous MIT employees who lent us your box openers, thank you to the MIT Hobby Shop for the table clamp, thank you Roger and Netia for TAing and helping to guide and supervise the students and thank you Danny Braunstein for $70 worth of servo motors. You all made SPARK happen for five excited kids, and an excited undergraduate. And of course, thank you to Dr. Kolitz of Draper Labs and our Kickstarter backers for funding us and making SPARK possible in the first place.
