# Day 40:🌵✈️😁⛰️🌦️🌈

Fowler, CO to Colorado Springs, CO — 87.4 mi, 3174 ft

💤 The night before a century is a tense one. Even if we have 95 mile days, nothing quite gets you as excited or as anticipatory as the big scary triple digit number. Thus, we agreed to enforce our strict wake-up routine that worked extremely well for our first century back in Missouri — wake up at 6 and leave by 7 to conquer the day.

🐐 However, you might have noticed that the first line says 87 miles, not 100! That’s because we woke up to find out that Aarushi, our route queen, was able to shave off several miles with some magic and work into the night. The news worked slowly from person to person, as it usually does, and as each person was informed of our shortened route, the urgency in which we got ready slowly dwindled. Usually I am all for full urgency, but we were able to enjoy our morning a bit more and savor the breakfast the church had generously provided us. Notably, the new Arizona Iced Teas that are my new favorite drink. Thanks Aarushi for sacrificing some sleep to make the rest of our lives a bit easier 🙂

🌵 We set off from the church at around 7:30 am, and hit a pretty desolate stretch of highway surrounded by arid prairies and shortgrass growth, which is also where we started seeing more and more cacti. I had the feeling of being home once again. While I’m not necessarily from an area that looks like this in California, seeing such wide and desolate landscapes really remind me that we’re finally fully entering the west and leaving the midwest. Having spent most of my summers traipsing around the western states with my family, from this point on, it’s much more familiar territory. We also saw the Rockies on the horizon! After so much talk of crossing them, it was a bit surreal seeing them appear, knowing that we would take on the challenge soon. 

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-30.png?w=1024)“Blogger behavior,” aka trying to take photos on the road + the Rockies in the distance! ⛰️

🚂 This stretch was mostly parallel to a set of train tracks, so we were able to see a couple of trains go by with long chains of cargo in tow. While I wasn’t paying super close attention to them, one was apparently carrying the deconstructed parts of a windmill much like the hundreds we had seen in the days prior. A little of the “behind the scenes” action! They were super loud though, and the sound carried around us all the way to our first rest stop 24 miles in. This was a longer stretch than usual since there was literally nothing else inhabited on the way there, but at least this was a produce place we could finally stop at!

🥐…Except this wasn’t even an actual or open store, testifying to how sparse services are out here. Thus, our rest stop was of the picnic-blanket-on-the-ground-next-to-the-car flavor, which is also a vibe as well. Another thing to make it better was that Grandpa Tom and Grandma Brenda bought us HUMONGOUS cinnamon rolls from a local bakery that I happily chowed down on.

🏛️During this stop, Aarushi mentioned the Pueblo Weisbrod Aircraft Museum about 10 miles away, which she had found when scouring for stops the night before. She didn’t end up picking it because of the awkward mileage and the fact it was slightly off-course, but Drew’s ears pricked up at the mention of aircraft. As our resident Course 16 (Aerospace Engineering), he was immediately determined to go and the rest of us were also interested! Thus, to the aircraft museum we went.

🛫As we sped into the museum area, we were simultaneously following signs to the Pueblo airport since they were right next to each other. I had the fleeting thought that in another completely non-realistic universe, we were a group of bikers racing to catch our flight. I don’t know what the context of that would be, but it was a fun scenario to ponder about — checking in our bikes, decked out in our gear… 

🛬The museum appeared before us, and we all headed into the first hangar absolutely PACKED with planes. One of the workers there led us in and gave us a quick overview of their most famous plane, the B-29. We also learned more about it from Drew, who is a never-ending encyclopedia on planes. 

**B-29:** The B-29 is most famous for being the first mass-produced bomber with a pressurized cabin, which allowed it to operate at much higher altitudes than rival planes (namely the Japanese air forces). They were the model of planes that dropped the atomic bombs in Hiroshima and Nagasaki, proving to be instrumental in the conclusion of WWII.

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-31.png?w=1024)

🇻🇳 As we wandered around the hangar, Drew acted as our trustworthy tour guide and told us all about each plane that caught our eye. I learned how the Soviet Union and the US developed competing planes based on different philosophies in the Vietnam War: the US prioritized speed, the USSR prioritized maneuverability. Apparently the US’s bet didn’t pay off, and the USSR emerged victorious in this development battle. It was really cool to learn more about the technology that dictated the outcomes of different wars, since as someone with a very surface-level understanding of these historical events, these aspects of conflict are often glossed over and understated. 

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-139.jpeg?w=1024)The hangar 

We then all chose a plane that we felt most connected to — up to you to judge if you feel that they are accurate 😉

It was also the first jet fighter manufactured by Grumman, known now to many as part of major defense company Northrop-Grumman. ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-141.jpeg?w=768)**Carmen, Lockheed F-104 Starfighter:** Deployed mostly as a fighter-bomber in the Cold War and is widely produced by other nations outside of the US. ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-142.jpeg?w=1024)**Nunu, Mikoyan-Gurevich MiG-17:** Saw most of its success in the Vietnam War, nearly a decade after development by the USSR. It was initially meant to combat slow-moving US bombers, but it was surprisingly successful against the US’s faster fighters that were designed for speed and long-range combat. ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-143.jpeg?w=1024)**Tatiana, Lockheed P-2 Neptune:** Developed as anti-submarine warfare in WWII and was famous for its incredible range + mixed-engine design. ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-144.jpeg?w=1024)**Drew, Fairchild Republic A-10 Thunderbolt II (Warthog):** The only aircraft in the Air Force developed primarily for close air support (attacking enemy ground forces)! ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-145.jpeg?w=768)**Aarushi, F-86 Sabre:** An iconic, transonic jet-powered fighter that defined early Cold War aviation. Originally designed as a Mach 2 nuclear bomber, it was later converted into a premier reconnaissance aircraft that played a major role in the Vietnam War. I just liked the shark face on the side!

Although almost certainly unintentional, they gave the planes so much personality that Nunu recreated them beautifully (+ a cameo from Drew and Aarushi, which is also why Aarushi is making a face in her plane photo for context).

🧑‍🚀 Grandpa Tom also gave us a quick history lesson about the development of space suits! There are so many details that go into its creation that I never thought about, including being able to adjust the temperature of the suit and how the gloves are the area that need to be heated the most. The labeling on the suits must also be mirrored since the astronaut is using a mirror to see the front of their own suit, so the actual text must be mirrored.   


![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-152.jpeg?w=768)

📖 We left the aircraft museum enlightened and with a lot more knowledge of planes + Pueblo, or at least I did. This was honestly my dream stop and what I had initially imagined more of what this summer would be like — going to museums/visitor centers and learning more about the local history through these places. Don’t get me wrong, we’ve learned a lot from all of the people we’ve met and the other places we stopped, but nothing compares to a relatively unknown museum run by a small team. They often have the coolest bits of history and information that you’d never know otherwise, except for your decision to cross paths at this moment. 

⚠️ On the next stretch, we hit some gravel and a desert-y area with some unique houses (a watermelon-shaped abode) and quirky decor (a giant 8-foot tall skeleton and a sign that said CAUTION: Corgi territory). Very uniquely western. We followed the gravel until we had to merge onto I-25 for a little while to reach our next rest stop, which was an official freeway rest stop. Unfortunately, the stop was closed and boarded up, so we spent a while debating what to do: to move or not to move, that is the question. We also made extra sure Tatiana (the driver) knew about the situation.

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-153.jpeg?w=843) ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-32.png?w=800)I was the first to sound the alarm + how it felt

☕️We ended up deciding to just have the rest stop in the unblocked exit of the rest stop, so Tatiana just backed the car up into the driveway and we had our stop on the side of the freeway. The picnic blanket is very clutch in elevating the vibes of the stops and allowing us to pretend we’re having a picnic somewhere not by hundreds of cars whizzing by! Brenda also brought some of us coffee, which was also very sweet.  


![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-154.jpeg?w=1024)

😷 During this time, Aarushi finally pulled out the face mask her mom had got her for face protection, wanting to at least give it some action before she reunited with them later today. She put it on with some hesitancy after seeing Nunu rock it, but it ended up not fitting due to the limited real estate around her ear (where the mask hangs). For context, we have a LOT of gear around our face. Most of us have our sunglasses and shockz (sport headphones that go over your ear), so there’s already 2 things hanging off our ears… yeah the mask had to go. 

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-155.jpeg?w=768)No pictures 📸  


🚵 We also had to decide if we wanted to stay on I-25 as well or not. During our brief stint on the road, several of us felt unsafe with so many cars going 80 mph past us. We decided to go on this small trail that ran parallel to the road for a couple of miles, and it was actually really fun! I’ve found the off-road stuff to be more and more fun after being initially scared of it, but after the long highways of Kansas, it’s much more mentally stimulating to be just a bittt worried if your bike will slip in the dirt or not. However, the fun was short-lasted, and soon we were back on our highways and streets. I biked with Aarushi and Carmen, helping Aarushi summarize the plot of the first Heroes of Olympus book (an ongoing conversation topic) whenever we could. However, the cars don’t seem to like it when we go 3-wide and take up the whole lane… it’s kinda rude, especially when we’re trying to have an important conversation. 

🍧 Our last rest stop was Colorado Sno-Balls, a snow cone shop! I split a medium blue raspberry + cherry combo with Drew (which was good, since a medium turned out to be a good 5 stories tall) and it was AMAZING. Normally snow cones seem to have an issue with the top part being desaturated in syrup relative to the bottom, but this one was _perfectly_ saturated at the top and had an amazing texture. $2.00 well spent.

🥪 Aarushi’s family, who had flown into Denver in the morning, met up with us and brought us a lot of delicious fruit. We had lunch outside on a bench before the skies opened up and brought a downpour, forcing us to evacuate under the narrow overhang. Aarushi’s parents were discussing Tatiana’s bad luck with mayo (see Day 17 blog) and Tatiana promptly drops her mayo-covered slice of bread into the cooler facedown — the universe works in funny ways.

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-34.png?w=1024)

🌦️ Once it stopped raining, we packed up for the last segment into Colorado Springs, and experienced my favorite weather. I love when you’re somewhere that was freshly rained on, with the rain still continuing on nearby and patches of clouds still lingering in the sky. It made for some great views that my phone camera can’t capture, but trust me when I say it was gorgeous, especially as the Rockies grew larger in the background. We also saw our first rainbow!

🍔 We biked on a nicely paved trail that followed a creek for the next couple of miles with a crazyyy headwind. The city also decided to become the hilliest it's been the whole day, so suddenly the 10 miles to the end felt much longer than face value. We chugged through, and were soon rewarded by a sudden gift of… In N Out. A true oasis after many Wendy’s and McDonald’s. Right along our route, Nate and Drew, who were ahead of us, had also stopped and were planning to head inside. Aarushi kept going to meet up with her family, but the rest of us got a small thing to eat just for the joy that In N Out brings, rather than an actual meal since we wanted to leave room for the real dinner coming up. It was also Drew’s first time at one as well, so we needed to be there to witness history. I reunited with my beloved animal fries and got extra sauce on the side.

🍉 After leaving In N Out, we powered through the last climb of the day and reached the airbnb that we were sharing with Aarushi’s family (thank you!!). It was a beautiful house that we had all to ourselves for the next two days. They cooked up a delicious dinner of Indian food, and it was so nice to have some flavors in my mouth that weren’t just tomato and cheese. The watermelon was also very yummy, and _slightly_ resembled the Rocky Mountains. 

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/image-168.jpeg?w=1024)⛰️🍉

⬆️ ⬇️ To round out the night, Aarushi’s dad asked us to share our highs and lows of the trip so far. We all went around, and it was an amazing conversation about our experiences. It was cool when we shared highs and when highs + lows were on the same day, showing just how much happens in a day. I shared a lot about my dog-bite experience (suprise surprise, my personal low), which was nice to get out. We moved the conversation to the couch after eating. It was a great reflective end to the day, and we went to bed excited about our rest day tomorrow! So many things to do, but so little time…
