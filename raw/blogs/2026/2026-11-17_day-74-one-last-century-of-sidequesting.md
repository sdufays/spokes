# Day 74: One Last Century of Sidequesting

Folsom, CA to Benecia, CA — 108.1 mi, 2207 ft  
  
Hello my lovely reader. Today I have the privilege of writing my last spokes blog about one of my favorite days of spokes, the century before we biked into SF. This is an extremely controversial take as these 100 miles took some of us 18 hours. In my very first blog, I wrote about how intimately we were about to get to know one another, because we would be by each other’s side during some of our lowest lows and our highest highs. These 18 hours had both—but the lowest lows only makes the highs higher, right?

  
As the route planners, Carmen and I fell asleep last night giddy with excitement to make the most of our last full day of biking, by doing as much non-biking as possible: writing in 10 planned side quests on the route. In hindsight it is incredibly obvious how this day would get completely derailed…

7-7:30 AM, Mile 0: Wake Up

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06256.jpg?w=1024)

  
We started off the day with a wake-up that was way too leisurely for a 100 mile day with 10 rest stops and a delicious breakfast. We were staying with a college batchmate of my dad’s in Folsom, a friend he hadn’t seen in 33 years since they graduated. Somehow my dad’s graduating class of ~250 has a network so strong that even with just a week’s notice, Rajeev Uncle very generously agreed to host and feed our whole team at their place—thank you!!! 

9:00 AM, Mile 0: Departure  
We finally set off towards Sacramento, riding fast to make time for our side quests. We were on a beautiful windy trail for twenty miles, zipping left and right through pedestrians, flying by slower bikers, soaking up our last full bike day, looking extremely swaggy in our official-looking matching bike gear. Spirits were high.

10:20 AM, Mile 14: Asian snack haul

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06257.jpg?w=1024)

Spirits were even higher when Nunu came back with one of this summer’s most legendary snack hauls from an asian supermarket: crispy green grapes, apple juice, famine, honey butter chips, rice crackers, yakult, can man, rambutan, pocky, and jelly sticks!!!! What a fresh surprise after weeks of every driver literally bringing back the exact same snacks.

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06259.jpg?w=768) ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06260.jpg?w=768) ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06261.jpg?w=768)

11:30 AM, Mile 26: SUSHI!!!!!!

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06264.jpg?w=1024)

Unarguably the best meal of the trip. A huge huge huge shoutout to [Makisu Sushi](https://www.google.com/maps/place/MAKISU+SUSHI+RESTAURANT+-+Contemporary+Japanese+Fusion/@38.5738754,-121.4107605,17z/data=!3m1!4b1!4m6!3m5!1s0x809ada57b51fad71:0x865296cb721f2eb3!8m2!3d38.5738754!4d-121.4081802!16s%2Fg%2F125_xdf7b?entry=ttu&g_ep=EgoyMDI2MTExNS4wIKXMDSoASAFQAw%3D%3D), a sushi restaurant run by Caroline’s friend’s uncle that very kindly offered to donate our team a meal as we passed through Sacramento! We had all been looking forward to this for the last week. Some of us were initially feeling skeptical about eating raw fish (me, as a novice level-1 meat eater; Drew, concerned about the state of his stomach for the 70+ miles we still needed to bike), but our concerns were quickly forgotten once this beautiful boat came out. Each piece literally tasted like a flavor bomb exploded in my mouth. I have no idea what was in each roll but everything complimented each other perfectly.

Bellies full and hearts content, we set off again. This would be the last time we would see Nate and Drew today, who wanted to speed to Davis to hang with Nate’s sister (shoutout Miranda).

2:10 PM, Mile 37: IKEA

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06268.jpg?w=768)  
Asking them to pose as if we were a family cooking dinner together ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06282.jpg?w=768)Two cute ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06287.jpg?w=768)3 free meatballs ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06266.jpg?w=1024)Looking very out of place ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06271.jpg?w=1024)Shoes off the bed 🙅‍♀️ ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06290.jpg?w=1024)Super hidden hiding place for our bikes

Our most unserious side quest yet, a side quest just for the sake of a side quest, and in hindsight we definitely should have stopped at Sacramento’s historic village downtown area instead. Caroline put a 25 minute timer for us to roam around IKEA, and so we went around touching many things, reading out loud everything’s swedish name with very offensive pronunciation, and posing in various model homes as if we were living there together as a family. At the end of our loop, Nunu wanted to try the Swedish meatballs but didn’t want to buy a full plate of 11, so she asked the cafeteria lady if she could purchase just 1 meatball. She gave one to Nunu for free, which promptly prompted Tatiana and I to ask for a free meatball too.

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06291.jpg?w=1024)

  
After leaving the IKEA blackhole, we biked out of the city and onto a trail right alongside the main freeway out of Sacramento and into Davis. This is where we first felt the full force of the wind rolling straight east from the Pacific Ocean, and we were biking headfirst into it. Good thing I had Carmen to draft the whole time.

4:00 PM, Mile 49: UC Davis

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06295.jpg?w=1024)Going ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06292.jpg?w=1024)going ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06294.jpg?w=1024)gone ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06293.jpg?w=1024)

Nate and Drew were gone by the time the four of us arrived in Davis. They went to some mid boba spot with Nate’s sister Miranda and Nunu, but we met up with them at this fire bingsu (Korean shaved ice) place we found from last year’s blog. These were so delicious and perfectly refreshing that we ordered a fourth mango sticky rice bingsu. Last day of Spokes = last day to fully give into our greed. I also left a review to get a free strawberry iced tea which I attempted to drink while starting off on the next leg. 

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06297.jpg?w=768)

There were initially ambitious plans to go to the Davis art museum which would’ve been soooo cool but they were closing by the time we finished eating. We stopped by Davis’ redwood grove instead—the largest collection of redwood trees west of the coastline forests! I imagined myself as a UC Davis student walking home from failing an exam or something and coming here for a serene spot to contemplate the state of my life.

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06302.jpg?w=1024) ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06309.jpg?w=1024)

Leaving Davis, we were greeted by true agricultural California. Miles and miles of neatly squared plots of crops, and unlike the corn-ful midwest, each plot was different: cows, tomatoes, walnuts, and many more we couldn’t identify. We weaved through the square fields making right angle turns heading west and south: either biking into straight headwind or a cross-wind strong enough to blow us onto the main road. 

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06301-1.jpg?w=1024) ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06301.jpg?w=1024) ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06305.jpg?w=768) ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06306.jpg?w=1024)

7:00 PM, Mile 62: Dixon Fruit Market

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/fruit-market.jpg?w=1024) ![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06314.jpg?w=1024)

We bought our usual (peaches) at the fruit stand and stuffed our bike bags with snacks so Nunu could drive off and support Drew and Nate until their finish. We were going unsupported for the next couple hours. The sun was setting, we were barely halfway, and it was finally starting to settling in just how screwed we were.

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06317.jpg?w=1024)

At this point, the logistics were getting tricky. Carmen and I were together, and perhaps the only people genuinely enjoying ourselves, still committed to this foolish sense of “adventure” trying to make our last spokes day last as long as possible. Drew and Nate were a couple hours ahead, Caroline was ~40 min ahead, and Tatiana was ~20 min behind. Nunu had to speed ahead with the van to catch Drew and Nate before they reached the host. All three of them advised the four of us to stick together as it started getting dark, warning that the drivers along the route were aggressive. The four of us were extremely spread out, however, and the remainder of our rest stops (including a bakery, Costco, and thrift store…. the potential was there) were closed. A (rightfully) annoyed Caroline said she would stop to wait for us at a grocery store 3 miles away from her, and Carmen and I waited for Tatiana, whose spirits were at a severe low. If Tatiana has one fatal flaw, it’s that she loses the ability to do anything when she gets tired, and it was well past the normal time-of-day when her tiredness kicks in. This makes her an incredible morning person (in undergrad she would rather wake up at 3 to study for an exam than stay up even past midnight), and she started floating the idea of her finishing the last leg of the day early morning tomorrow, even though we already were pushing for a 7 AM start to get to SF on time.

10:30 PM, Mile 77?: Lucky's

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06319.jpg?w=768)Customer-in-training!

When we arrived at Lucky’s, we found poor Caroline waiting on the floor near the entrance where the grocery carts are kept, and Tatiana started calling the others to figure out how she could quit the ride early and finish it tomorrow. The situation was not looking good. I felt really bad actually, since my route and rest stops were responsible for how inefficiently our day had gone. Although Carmen and I were still in a good mood (we were having a great existential conversation on this last leg), it wasn’t fair that we dragged Caroline and Tatiana into this mess of a day. Instead of apologizing however, I encouraged both Caroline and Tatiana to drink as many bottled Starbucks Frappuccino’s in an attempt to raise their spirits via sugar and caffeine. We sat on the floor next to the shopping carts and ate and drank our snack haul, which was bought mostly just out of greed since we had been eating good all day. 

Grudgingly getting back on our bikes, we realized we had a major problem: it was freezing cold (and still windy) now that it was nighttime, probably the coldest conditions we had biked in. Only Caroline had been smart enough to grab her jacket from the van before Nunu left, so we needed Nunu to come back from the host's place to supply one last rest stop. 

11:30 PM, Mile ????: Side of the road

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06321.jpg?w=1024)

Nunu and Nate supplied us a much-needed quick rest stop on the side of the road. They asked Tatiana if she still wanted to SAG, but she was miraculously revived. She said she doesn’t know what happened, but all of a sudden was feeling insanely wired and locked in. It turns out she had never drank caffeine at night before. Things started making a lot of sense.

Caroline and Tatiana split off ahead of us, while Carmen and I dragged behind. I don’t know what was up with us either, but we both feeling wired too, not from caffeine but maybe just the adrenaline of this being our last day of spokes ever. This sounds insane after the day we already had, but we were looking for more side quests. It was something like a “what the hell, sure”  feeling—we were already screwed in terms of our sleep, so let’s just keep the adventure going as long as possible. If we could screw off as a duo, that would be ideal, but we didn’t want the team waiting or worrying for us—so we sadly decided against stopping at the Dave and Buster’s enticingly perfectly along our route. We opted for a 7-11 with RedBulls instead.

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/dsc06329.jpg?w=1024)

It was 1:30 AM and I remember feeling on top of the world, seeing the view of the lights of the bay, thinking oh my gosh what are we about to accomplish tomorrow, and yeah we are goated and the toughest people in the game. It is hard to encapsulate in words, but I genuinely felt euphoric. Like probably what smoking a meth pipe feels like. High on life, or whatever they say.

![](https://mitspokes2026.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/ses.jpg?w=1024)

In a famous scene in _The Notebook_ , the lead characters lie down in the middle of a street’s intersection on their first date. It’s meant to encapsulate the rashness of youth, how fleeting and thrilling it is to be alive in a moment that reminds you how precariousness life is. It was 2:30 AM, no car in sight, car headlight visibility almost a minute in each direction. Carmen and I lay down on the concrete. We listened to Green Light by Lorde.

3:20 AM, Mile 108: We arrive

At some point we took a wrong turn and went downhill to a dead-end with a house whose gate had animal skulls decorating it, but regardless we made it to our host (huge huge thank you to Gretchen and Anthony!), less than 3 hours before we had to wake up again. We brushed our teeth together, and when I came back from my shower, Carmen had already laid out my sleeping bag. It was 4:10 AM.

I miss you Carmie. I miss the whole team, Caroline’s quick witty comments, Nunu’s boundless enthusiasm, Tatiana’s weird stories, Drew’s funny one-liners, Nate’s dry humor. I meant to write each of you one of the Spokes thank-you notes I gave to so many people along our way, but it seems too late now. I hope you know how much you mean to me and that we will forever be a team in my heart. I miss all the people who gave to us expecting nothing in return, I miss the free-ness of biking through wide open land, I miss following random signs on the road or posters on the wall for random adventures, I miss having not a care in the world except for how to finish day’s mileage, I miss having this majestic goal to work towards everyday, I miss it all.

May we all keep finding this euphoria in our lives, and to the next generation of Spokes that gets to experience it for the first time. 

Signing off,

Aarushi ❤
