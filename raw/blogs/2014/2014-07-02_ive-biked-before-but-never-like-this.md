# I've biked before, but never like this.

[I’ve biked before, but never like this. ](http://spokesamerica.tumblr.com/post/90523886065/ive-biked-before-but-never-like-this "I’ve biked before, but never like this. ")

When I was growing up in Great Neck, my full size 26’’ bike was a blue and silver Schwinn.The Gregg Brothers recommended the model from their extensive use and so I had to have one too. We used to go around town and local neighborhoods stealing and sometimes exchanging valve caps with cars- Perhaps you weren’t aware, but cars have all kinds of valve caps: sometimes a chiseled BMW or Porsche insignia on top; other times the cap would be shaped like a bullet casing; or when we were lucky, the car owner would go so far as to purchase caps from speciality shops in the form of smileys, skulls, dice and eight balls. In the top drawer of my bureau at home, I would stash all my loot caps. I never did get caught. But anyhow, I finally settled on these golden skulls with red eyes to replace my ‘basics,’ a jarring image no doubt, but I loved seeing the gold glisten in the sun as my spokes turned. Constantly checking my back wheel to see the glow. My klepto days quickly faded, however, when the Greggs moved to another school. It is remarkable, though, that up until the gold caps, I never did replace my basics with the spares in my bureau drawer.  
  
The most strenuous route I would ride started close to home at the intersection between Pembroke and Arcadia. Turn right and an easy downward slope awaited you, but turn left and you encountered the base of the Hill. A long stretch of uphill that serpentined past the Ambrosio Street, around Gladys’s house, under the magnolia trees, and eventually into Lake Success. I can’t remember when we first tackled it, although I know the first time I climbed it was with Dad. I still hold a deep sense of satisfaction to those first rides, sweating my way up, calves on the verge of cramping, my back stiff all the way until that moment when we would reach the top, immediately swing around, and bomb the same hill down towards Jose’s house or the Park. As the summers passed and my rides stretched their radius from home up towards Danny’s house in the Hills or to Great Neck South, the Hill became easier- but never a trifle. But now, my sacred Hill could be just that. A trifle.

Just the other day, we covered a 20 mile uphill, seventy times the length of the hill, ascending about 4000 feet. In painful heat, we all made it to the top. And these sorts of climbs are becoming almost customary. Treacherous, sure, but customary nonetheless. One pedal after the other, again and again. And eventually, you get there. You just have to do the math.  
  
On these nine hour rides, that feeling of pulling into the day’s destination is elating. Your legs have been moving for hours, your back is shot, your nerves are still on edge after looking out for that occasional RV that pulls too close, the sunblock has melted into your mouth or trickled down onto your salt-striped shirt, and your skin is scaly from the Western dryness. But you have reached that new campsite, Subway, Main Street, that new end, and the sense of achievement washes over you like a cold shower. You smile with the numbness and almost immediately you feel ready for the day ahead, because you will have this feeling again.

Just some musings-

EG

P.S. Interestingly, my tires are now missing their valve caps.
