# What is Your Battle Cry?

[What is Your Battle Cry?](http://spokesamerica.tumblr.com/post/89512926385/what-is-your-battle-cry "What is Your Battle Cry?")

Hello from the Prospector Hotel and Casino! Emma here, reporting on various adventures and encounters we’ve had over the last couple of days, in no particular order. 

We rode into Austin a couple days ago and camped out in the local park. We met two retired gentlemen (Dick and Don) who were part of Emigrant Trails Inc, a group that puts up signs marking the old Pony Express and other historic trails in the west. Each sign has an engraved quote from the diary of someone who traveled over that trail. Don was a civil engineer and Dick was a chemical engineer; both had worked for oil companies in San Francisco. They were eating cheese and fancy meats for lunch, and Don was enjoying a Sierra Nevada pale ale. I asked them what advice they would give to a young engineer starting her first job, and they said: “well, just remember that you don’t know anything. The important thing that you learned in college was how to communicate - so use your ability to communicate using the written and spoken word, and the rest will follow.” Thanks for the advice!

:: This is the sunset view from our campsite in Austin. ::

![](http://33.media.tumblr.com/a8a384972862846e419644156edc88ad/tumblr_inline_n7jt90xxpv1s3e7lq.jpg)

The ride into Austin might have been my favorite day so far. It was a beautiful ride with great scenery, and I felt completely fresh after my driving day. I was by myself most of the day, and to keep myself entertained on the lonely straight stretches of road, I stretched out my arms and pedaled fast, soaring like a bird underneath the hawks overhead. I felt so alive and free, absolutely content with where I was and what I was doing. Those are the moments I cherish on this trip. 

:: The view from Sweet Caroline’s handlebars onto the mountains of Nevada.::

![](http://33.media.tumblr.com/767882b6ec330c4a0d08ba260a32382c/tumblr_inline_n7jtdfAllX1s3e7lq.jpg)

This week we also had a great evening at Middlegate, Nevada. Officially Middlegate has a population of 17, and its mostly a bar and restaurant; the only one for about 50 miles in either direction on Highway 50. Middlegate is definitely one of the hidden gems of the US; the people we met there were so welcoming and hilarious to talk to - we had a great time. Caleb and Sam took on the Middlegate Monster burger-eating challenge while the rest of us chowed down on amazing mac-and-cheese fritters and bacon burgers. The over-21s among us (Jamin, Maddie and I) proceeded to close down the bar, shooting some pool, putting a Spokes dollar-bill on the ceiling, and singing along with the local guitar player, Paul Smith. Paul told us all about Virginia City - where Janis Joplin used to play - and about how Middlegate is struggling to stay open. Middlegate runs entirely on generators, so their bill for diesel along is several thousands of dollars a month, and they have to replace the generators several times a year. Maddie was super-interested in their cause, so I have no doubt their luck will change soon! 

::Yes, the Middlegate Monster rivals Maddie’s head in size. ::

![](http://33.media.tumblr.com/b45f03db574212fd324f29e186410f0f/tumblr_inline_n7jturv4xV1s3e7lq.jpg)

More recently, we met a family that is driving/biking from Florida to San Francisco today. I spoke to the wife, Betsy, and something she said really sparked a train of thought in my brain. She said “everyone’s doing this for a reason; everyone is fighting for a cause.” I linked this to a passage from one of my favorite writers, Cheryl Strayed, who wrote “You’re generally less humble in [your twenties] than you’ll ever be…You will learn a lot about yourself if you stretch in the direction of goodness, of bigness, of kindness, of forgiveness, of emotional bravery. Be a warrior for love.” I know everyone on Spokes has their own reasons for being on the trip, but one of mine is to work on becoming the person I want to be. I feel like my four years at college went faster than a downhill ride on Carroll Summit, and this ride is a pause, a time to collect my thoughts and learn to fight for the cause of good, for the cause of helping others. I want to learn to be selfless and caring and a pillar of strength for others, and I am starting on that journey with Spokes.
