# We're in the Sister City to Dushanbe, Tajikistan

[We’re in the Sister City to Dushanbe, Tajikistan ](http://spokesamerica.tumblr.com/post/91304571265/were-in-the-sister-city-to-dushanbe-tajikistan "We’re in the Sister City to Dushanbe, Tajikistan ")

Have you figured it out yet? No? Well I’ll just let you enjoy the suspense until the end of this post, then. :) Don’t say you never learned anything while reading our blog.

Oh, and this is Emma, by the way. My teammates keep reminding me that I never mention my identity in my blog posts. 

While you’re trying to figure out where we are, let me describe where we’ve been. Since our last post, we entered Colorado and have ridden through the Rockies. Colorado has some of the most beautiful riding scenery we’ve encountered so far. I would have to agree with Mick, the Irish ex-army officer who was also doing a cross country journey, who described Colorado as “the Ireland of America…with more mountains.” Check out this photo for proof: 

![](http://38.media.tumblr.com/0aa1ffac6d0547822d2bfcf32d0d1fde/tumblr_inline_n8h06ltl8e1s3e7lq.jpg)

As further proof of Mick’s claim, on July 3rd we encountered our first mountain thunderstorm. I enjoyed it (even the hail was kind of cool) but poor Jamin and Cali were out in it way too long and were pretty frozen when they joined us in Telluride. Thanks for the warm clothes, Sam. :)

![](http://33.media.tumblr.com/a0142a6a1a3f41e093fd6efb2c3bcb7f/tumblr_inline_n8h09j7wUM1s3e7lq.jpg)

Telluride was a fantastic town for us to visit. We stayed with Sarah, the Executive Director of the Pinhead Institute of Telluride, a STEM education center for the grade school students of the area. Unfortunately we didn’t have the time to do a set of workshops with the Institute, but we did build a bicycle-carried solar system model that appeared in Telluride’s 4th of July parade. We also rode with the Institute in the parade, which was a lot of fun - at least, Maddie and Jamin thought so.

![](http://38.media.tumblr.com/05c6503923227e6ae6fad9d80fe0d5af/tumblr_inline_n8h0zf6Rgu1s3e7lq.jpg)

Talking to Sarah was a blast because she is a high energy, dynamic person. As a leader of the Pinhead Institute, she has enlisted the help of local engineers, mathematicians, scientists and computer coders to offer week-long summer camps. She has also convinced major tech companies to offer internships to the high school students in the program. Last night I had a conversation with our hosts in…hahaha - you thought I’d tell you where we are, didn’t you? …in our current location, and we were talking about how little students know about many real-life occupations before they go to college. Take engineering, for instance; very few high school students know what an engineer does, and often engineering classes are a poor or boring representation of what engineering can be. Places like the Pinhead Institute have the potential to change a high school student’s perspective for the better by giving them a very early opportunity to see what engineers or scientists or coders do, before they commit to studying a particular field in college.

After Telluride, we pushed on through the Rockies for the next couple of days. We have been particularly lucky in getting great views from the tops of the mountains, and gorgeous sunsets. This sunset in Parlin made up for the fact that it rained all afternoon and that there were mosquitoes everywhere:![](http://38.media.tumblr.com/fd993164b97abecc32b381868fa572fb/tumblr_inline_n8h131rABM1s3e7lq.jpg)

At Monarch Pass we crossed the Continental Divide for the first time: 

![](http://38.media.tumblr.com/22b88260aac89a2aac3a94770f1955e8/tumblr_inline_n8h14sJ0EJ1s3e7lq.jpg)

And outside of Frisco, I saw the most beautiful lakeside sunset of my life: 

![](http://38.media.tumblr.com/856fb8d8775f138e2a6c99f3605807bf/tumblr_inline_n8h16aUyJb1s3e7lq.jpg)

And now, for the big reveal, I will tell you where we currently are: 

After pulling Team Spokes 2014’s first century (i.e. 100+ mile ride)…

…braving the highest peak of our journey, at Loveland Pass, at 11,990 feet…

…and displaying formidable navigational skills in negotiating the final 30 miles of the ride from of Golden, CO…

…we have arrived in Boulder! Woohoo!!!
