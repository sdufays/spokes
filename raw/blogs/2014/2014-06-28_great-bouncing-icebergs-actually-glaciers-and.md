# Great bouncing icebergs! (actually glaciers and mountains and canyons)

[Great bouncing icebergs! (actually glaciers and mountains and canyons)](http://spokesamerica.tumblr.com/post/90176651990/great-bouncing-icebergs-actually-glaciers-and "Great bouncing icebergs! \(actually glaciers and mountains and canyons\)")

Greetings from Escalante, Utah. We exited the basin-and-range geography of Nevada into the canyon geography of Utah. We rode through Red Canyon yesterday, and then some of Bryce Canyon National Park and Escalante Grand Staircase National Monument today. Yesterday morning started with a twenty-something mile climb up to 10,350 feet passing through Cedar Breaks National Monument, which was worth the $4.

Cedar Breaks overlook

![image](http://31.media.tumblr.com/b7182516b72641ca4f5278f2cbc60fce/tumblr_inline_n7w73akabF1s3e7lq.jpg)

Red Canyons![image](http://38.media.tumblr.com/f1bb8caee710b1073826c8580d1ac019/tumblr_inline_n7w72502Y91s3e7lq.jpg)

Cedar City (two days ago now), we stopped and taught to two dozen girl scouts, age 5-10ish. We split into pairs and taught three workshops in the park in the late afternoon.

Maddie and I took the bicycle/tinkering side of things and sort of winged it (shhhh.) In the afternoon before, Maddie, with a little assistance from me, prepped most of a bicycle-powered blender in case we needed it. We sat down with the kids, asked them about their experience with bicycles, and then talked to them about sprockets, free-wheels, hubs, tires and tubes, fixing flats, motors, magnets, electricity, and more. Whatever they seemed interested it, we took it and ran with it. We let a couple of them completely hack apart a blender motor, which they were totally into. We taught them about Phillips-head versus flathead versus allen (hex) keys, and “righty-tighty lefty-loosey”.

Things that I learned:

  1. Acceptance: Kids are way less skeptical than teenagers. They just take what you say as if you were telling them they would grow up to be wealthy, or grow up to be blue. It’s easy to tell if you’ve got their interest or not.
  2. Outside the box: Maddie and I asked “What sticks to magnets?” and one of the answers we got was “pictures”. This is classically “wrong”, but we didn’t tell her that because my goal as part of Spokes is to keep people open-minded about non-traditional ways to think about things. This answer was the highlight of my day because she was thinking about things that she had seen, rather than trying to remember things she had memorized in school.
  3. Parents: It kind of surprised me, but this means a lot to the parents. Especially with the girl scouts, one of the parents came up to me and said she appreciated seeing girls teaching girls how to get their hands dirty (literally, and figuratively) with engineering.



With two teaching stops down, I’m a little less terrified. I’m excited to teach and excited to get to run my own workshop with kids. It’s been sort fun to make my workshop into the whole trip from estimating how much our cart of groceries will cost to “Hey Maddie, how tall do you think those windmills are?”
