# Day 3: Hitting the Hills

[Day 3: Hitting the Hills](http://spokesamerica.tumblr.com/post/88488180760/day-3-hitting-the-hills "Day 3: Hitting the Hills")

We’re on day three now and we’re starting to hit the hills.

The first two days were brutally hot. After a long ride on the Caltrain and a long but gorgeous ferry ride, we were all wilted by the time we got in during the late afternoon heat. 

![](http://38.media.tumblr.com/09bbeda0fff2899b2c6165c51124b61d/tumblr_inline_n70lyfjqTs1s3e7lq.jpg)

At the entrance of the Lake Soleno campsite, we found a marvelously cool sprinkler to frolic in. I think we all need to practice hydrating and getting up earlier. Needless to say, we broke out the ibuprofen. Enough to please my left IT band. There were peacocks all over the campsite, which turned out to sound like shrieking children. All night long. Pretty, but not to the ears.

![](http://38.media.tumblr.com/27efdfe65fad7a1b54125b5e67944684/tumblr_inline_n70js4Qtnq1s3e7lq.jpg)

Day two we started earlier, heading out of Winters around 6:45 am. The morning was filled with agriculture: fields and fields of agriculture. Grains, fruits, vegetable fields. Rows and rows of fields of trees grafted about a foot of the ground. This perplexed me for at least ten miles, until I gave up, grabbed one, and cut it in half. Walnut? Your guess is probably better than mine.

![](http://33.media.tumblr.com/dd3c759f1fdc8c31bb41742cb035f995/tumblr_inline_n70lrwVIXQ1s3e7lq.jpg)

We rode through Davis, Sacramento, and Yolo county, which I took great enjoyment out of.

![](http://38.media.tumblr.com/4252803d7348152337cc399dca2d6fc3/tumblr_inline_n70lorq6ZI1s3e7lq.jpg)

The afternoon was mostly on the American River Trail Bike Path, which was quite nice and filled with serious cyclists.

I am fascinated by these little ground squirrels that scamper around and dig holes everywhere. Also by all the dead grasses everywhere (except the golf courses, or course) and the buzz of the high voltage power lines. All mixed in with palm trees. California is weird, man.

By noon, we wilted again, to the point that Maddie, Caleb, Emma, and I killed a couple hours by the river in the hottest part of the day by napping, swimming, and sitting in the shade. Pavement temperature was 103-108 degrees; Yahoo weather gave me 100. We realized it wasn’t going to get much cooler and trekked onward. 

![](http://38.media.tumblr.com/5c2c6a4649c8628ef1239ebcfc60c859/tumblr_inline_n70jptj0mE1s3e7lq.jpg)

Over dinner we chatted with our campsite neighbor Kirsty, a Londoner who was also cycling across the country of the Adventure cycle trail. She was alone, carrying all her gear herself, with max water capacity of 16 liters. We were all quite impressed. 

This morning the riders left at 5:30am. We woke up with the sun to beat the heat. Today’s my driving day and all I have to say is that I have a lot of sympathy for long-haul truckers. I feel like this minivan with a bike rack is a tank; I can’t even imagine driving anything larger. Caleb is riding with me and proving to be a master navigator, as well as a compatible music-listener.

From Placerville, California,

Cali
