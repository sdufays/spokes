# Self-driven learning and helping students find their answers

[Self-driven learning and helping students find their answers](http://spokesamerica.tumblr.com/post/84611566300/self-driven-learning-and-helping-students-find "Self-driven learning and helping students find their answers")

Mark Twain: “I have never let my schooling interfere with my education”.

Most people understand that people learn differently. But what is less understood, is what people should do about this. How can a single teacher address the learning needs of all the individual learning styles of the students before them?

Unfortunately, there isn’t a right answer to this question.

But humor me, and allow me break out my soapbox and talk about this anyway. Specifically, let’s talk about self-driven learning. Rather than trying to lecture to each student’s individual learning styles and interests, why can’t the teacher inspire the students to explore their own curiosities in however they best learn?

I’m excited to be able to light a fire under the curiosity of students across the summer this summer. Spokes isn’t about _us_ teaching _them_ ; it is about _them_ teaching _themselves_. I am excited to simple provide the activation energy to show students they already have the skills to learn and explore the world.

For my past two years at MIT, I have taught classes in immunology to middle and high school students through the student-run program, Educational Studies Program (ESP), where MIT students teach what they love to younger students. As a new convert into the Mechanical Engineering department, kissing a bittersweet farewell to my previous home in the math department, my affinity for immunology often surprises my peers and my pupils. However, it was introduced to me in a way that struck a chord with my learning style and thus it has a special place in my heart about exposing students to immunology as well.

I try not to teach students in the standard lecture. I don’t know anyone who wants to sit through an hour or two of “…and then the CD4 protein binds to facilitate the activation of the helper T-cell receptor…” Memorizing names of cells and surface proteins is the focus of most immunology texts, yet the vast majority of students will find that profoundly boring.

I refer to my classes as shaggy dog stories, because the students often lead us on a winding path. Yet the important part is that the students are figuring it out themselves and learning that they can go through this sort of thinking for other problems. I am not teaching them, I am merely asking the questions to keep them headed in the right direction. I want to teach students how to ask themselves the right questions.

I always start out my classes by telling them I’m just an engineering student with perhaps a few more years of geeking out about immunology than them. They can ask me questions as they please, and chances are, I won’t know the answer. But that’s okay, because I can help them find _an_ answer.

I’m excited this summer with Spokes to help kids find answers to their questions.

Cali
