# Ne tirez pas, je suis un crayon!*

[Ne tirez pas, je suis un crayon!*](http://spokesamerica.tumblr.com/post/89991398070/ne-tirez-pas-je-suis-un-crayon "Ne tirez pas, je suis un crayon!*")

*Don’t shoot, I’m a pencil!

Hello from Cedar City, UT! In this update I will write in no particular order about the laughs we’ve had this week, the mountains we’ve climbed and the things we’ve seen on the road. 

Things we’ve seen on the road: 

  * Roadkill
  * Gizzards
  * Lots of beer cans
  * Shotgun shells
  * Signs with holes in them from the aforementioned shotgun shells
  * Waving grasses
  * Lizards
  * An amazing multicolored pair of pants that is our new bike-cleaning rag
  * Strips of tire



Its always interesting to look around at the junk on the road, because it tells fascinating stories. For instance, we figure that a lot of people are using the signs for target practice - who knew that was such a common activity?

The title of this post comes from Maddie, who recently taught us that the best phrase to learn in a foreign language is “Don’t shoot, I’m a pencil.” because a) they won’t shoot you and b) its clear you don’t know the language. I can now speak this phrase in French, Russian and Spanish. I feel much safer and more confident for my next overseas adventure. I also burst out laughing every time I think about this. 

![](http://33.media.tumblr.com/9ea1528f3595d72246fc6b6041011307/tumblr_inline_n7smbtnMDS1s3e7lq.jpg)

The most recent time I giggled about our favorite phrase was as we were climbing a 13,000 foot mountain in Great Basin National Park, where we had our most recent rest stop. I know what you’re thinking (my mom laughed for a solid minute when I told her) - why would you climb a mountain on your day off??? Well, folks, here’s the answer: IT WAS THERE. And because Team Spokes eats the impossible for breakfast. We started at 10,000 feet and followed the 3.1 mile trail up 3000 feet to the top of Mt. Wheeler for some fantastic views. We could see the route we’d ridden for the past couple of days, as well as the entire stretch of the mountain range that Mt. Wheeler dominated. We finished the hike with a refreshing (read: freezing) swim in one of the lakes below.
