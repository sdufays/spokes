# If students designed their own schools...

[If students designed their own schools…](http://spokesamerica.tumblr.com/post/45392567583/if-students-designed-their-own-schools "If students designed their own schools…")

The other day, Sanjay Sarma, the director of digital learning at MIT, came into a class that I’m taking in the department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science called [the Founder’s Journey](http://web.mit.edu/founders/www/details.html) to discuss the revolution that is occurring in the world of education. His presentation spurred a lot of discussion among my classmates about how education should be, and what we all think an optimal educational system would look like.

![image](http://31.media.tumblr.com/80d62571cd9937dce676a9396a604077/tumblr_inline_mjokug1qDh1qz4rgp.jpg)

After class, a friend of mine and I were discussing this, and in doing so came across something called [the Independent Project](http://www.theindependentproject.org/); in this project, students in a high school had a very similar discussion to what we talked about with Sanjay in class, but they went one giant step further: they implemented their idea. The following video demonstrates what the students do in the program:

The idea for The Independent Project came about from one student’s own experience of high school, and his observations of the experiences of his peers. The two main things he felt were missing from many high school classrooms were **engagement** and **mastery.** He also felt that even students who were engaged were often learning material that was not very intellectually valuable. They were learning lots of information, but very little about how to obtain information on their own, or even **create new information**. His intent was to design a school in which students would be fully **engaged in and passionate** about what they were learning, would have the experience of truly mastering something, or developing expertise in something, and would be learning how to learn. He felt that the most important ingredient to a school like that would be that it was student-driven.
