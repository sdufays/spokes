# A misplaced border

[A misplaced border](http://spokesamerica.tumblr.com/post/55431314436/a-misplaced-border "A misplaced border")

Though our trip up to the northern parts of Colorado added a couple hundred miles to our route, it was totally worth it. We really got to appreciate the incredible CO scenery, and we’ll need to rely on memories of those mountains for quite some time. As we headed south toward CO springs, we dropped thousands of feet of elevation. Some people feel like riding down here feels easier (yayyyy more oxygen!), but easier breathing comes at a price. All of a sudden, mountains turned into “turdlets” (- affectionately named by Nathan ;)), and now, even those are gone. Everyone said the flats would come in Kansas, but they’ve already arrived. Here are some of the guys yesterday, gathering the energy to face 22 miles on a gravel/dirt road. Titiaan’s “ferrari” (his supafancy Santos bike) did great on the different terrain, but the Jamis bikes struggled a bit. 

![](http://33.media.tumblr.com/267b1286792511f272c61116fb29f54a/tumblr_inline_mpxnqsm3x81qz4rgp.jpg)

That road led us into Pueblo, where the Western Express trail terminated and we joined up with the Transamerica trail! Jeff and I accidentally passed through the city and missed the downtown. A big mistake, as I think missing the city meant missing the last real coffee shop for a long, long while. Right now we’re hanging out in a gas station (yup, that’s a thing now :P) that has wifi, taking advantage of having any sort of connection to the rest of the world while we still can. Last night, Jeff, Phillip and I arrived early in the town of Boone, and though there wasn’t anywhere to go there, we had an awesome talk about Jeff’s work in physics, imaging live brains, the universe possibly being in a constant state of expansion and compression - not quite what you would expect in a playground on the side of a highway! 

Before we arrived in Pueblo, we stayed in CO Springs with an incredible family. I felt like I had two new little sisters :) Jade and Eva were wonderful hosts, sharing their coloring books, stone collection, stuffed elephant, pillow pets, kitten, guinea pig…:) And we discovered that there just might be a bigger fan of lime flavored tortilla chips in the world than Turner: Jade and Eva’s little brother, Dillon. He managed to climb up onto my lap, reach across the counter, and take fistfuls of chips. We also got to celebrate Jade’s 10th birthday with her! We were all very jealous of her birthday present - she got to go ride a horse through the Garden of the Gods with her parents :) 

![](http://38.media.tumblr.com/7041a0024722c4e979b7cd65cc7c31d1/tumblr_inline_mpxnqxcPB41qz4rgp.jpg)

And this girl doesn’t just ride horses - she rode the Oregon Coast Trail on a tandem bike with her mom, and has also gone with her parents on a tour across Southeast Asia. An athlete in the making if I ever saw one!

I’m going to miss these smiles, but they gave me some rocks and a piece of engraved wood for good luck on the road :) 

![](http://38.media.tumblr.com/94135bc44516655571a424bea9718e4b/tumblr_inline_mpxnqea88a1qz4rgp.jpg)

One last big piece of news from the road: our team has grown to 9 people! Noor Razzak flew in from Vancouver to travel with us and help Nathan with his video documentation of the trip. Welcome to the team, Noor! 

Righto. Going to head back out onto the road. We’re going 95 miles today, but with the new flat terrain, we cruised the first 31 at 22 mph, so we just might be at our destination in no time (knock on wood - you never know where a giant headwind might be hiding…)
