# Mindset - You can learn anything

[Mindset - You can learn anything](http://spokesamerica.tumblr.com/post/51388031766/mindset-you-can-learn-anything "Mindset - You can learn anything")

Carol Dweck, a researcher at Stanford, has spent her life studying motivation. In her book Mindset, ms. Dweck introduces the idea that the most important factor for success is the way you think about learning. 

Ms. Dweck separates mindsets into two types: fixed and growth mindsets. People with a fixed mindset think that their potential is determined at birth. They think their success is based on their innate talents. People with a fixed mindset are afraid to make mistakes and being laughed at. Why? Because poor performance or failure is an indicator of limited talent, when you believe the quality of your performance is fixed.  


People with a growth mindset think very differently. They believe that their intelligence is something they can develop. By learning and practicing, you can become better at an activity: whether it is music, maths or public speaking.

**How can you nurture the growth mindset among children?**

By explaining that the brain works like a muscle: it gets stronger as you train it. Every time you learn something new, your brain forms new connections between neurons. Think about it: nobody laughs at babies, despite the fact they do not know anything - babies just have not learned yet.

The growth mindset is fundamental to our workshops this summer. Through hands-on projects, we want to show children that background knowledge is not the most important factor on your ability to learn something.

What matters is following your curiosity and believing that you can learn by doing. Through hands-on classes — from growing vegetables to building solar panels — we show children how much fun learning can be when you believe you can learn anything. 

Let’s keep learning!
