# Wilderness First Aid and The Algorithmic Beauty of Plants

[Wilderness First Aid and The Algorithmic Beauty of Plants](http://spokesamerica.tumblr.com/post/41934910135/wilderness-first-aid-and-the-algorithmic-beauty-of "Wilderness First Aid and The Algorithmic Beauty of Plants")

My name is [Ethan Sherbondy](http://www.tooepic.com). I’m a junior studying Computer Science at MIT. I love plants, honeybees, and writing software. When Turner approached me about the possibility of doing this bike trip, I immediately said yes. Over the next few weeks, I will be weaving together a proper homepage for this site to promote and document our travels.

This past weekend, I took a Wilderness First Aid course at Boston College to prepare myself for any mishaps that might befall us on the road. The class was a two-day, 9am-5pm affair. On Sunday evening, I emerged triumphant, clenching a paper card:

![image](http://38.media.tumblr.com/f23f3a4235721c3b0ca1b04b741ae5ed/tumblr_inline_mhh59i3nmd1qz4rgp.jpg)

I also took away some valuable knowledge. Here’s a smattering:

  * Do not risk giving Aspirin to a person suffering a stroke, since strokes can be caused by both blood clots and hemorrhages. In the latter case, Aspirin would only make the situation worse.
  * In an emergency situation with multiple responders on hand, always designate one person as the leader! Going through practice scenarios, I learned that indecision can lead to precious minutes lost, and in a disaster, a few minutes makes a huge difference.
  * When dealing with a potential heat stroke case, respond immediately by applying cool water to decrease the patient’s body temperature. **But** be careful not to induce shivering, as shivering will  _increase_ body temperature.



Now, all of these are serious considerations, and the last is especially pertinent, since we’ll be biking in the hot summer sun each day. Despite the grave subject matter, the author of the course guidebook (_SOLO’s Field Guide to Wilderness First Aid_) saw it fit to include colorful, whimsical illustrations to lighten the mood. Here’s a gem of a diagram from the page on Heat Stroke:

![image](http://33.media.tumblr.com/8fd1a9733418d210c91bb881730b9627/tumblr_inline_mhhc08dcO21qz4rgp.jpg)

So besides getting practice preparing splints and receiving advice on how to respond to the most common medical emergencies, I also left the class knowing the Spanish words for man and water.

Overall, the course was a positive experience, and I managed to befriend some nice students from Boston College. Plus, on the T rides (Massachusetts Bay public transit) to and from BC, I had the chance to dive into a wonderful little book called  _[The Algorithmic Beauty of Plants](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/#abop), _which describes powerful ideas and techniques for mathematically modeling and simulating the physiology of plants using a special language called [L-systems](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-system).

![image](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/74/Dragon_trees.jpg)

I was so inspired by the sheer beauty of the generated images that I signed up to teach a hands-on intro course on some of the ideas covered in the book to middle/high-school students as part of [MIT’s Spark program](http://esp.mit.edu/learn/Spark/index.html). If you live in the Boston area and are interested in this sort of thing, feel free to register for my course starting February 18th via [the ESP website](http://esp.mit.edu/learn/Spark/index.html).

I’m sure I’ll have much more to say on this subject as we get closer to our departure date, but for now, I encourage you to [read the (freely available) book](http://algorithmicbotany.org/papers/#abop) and [start playing with L-systems](http://www.kevs3d.co.uk/dev/lsystems/).

Until next time,

Ethan
