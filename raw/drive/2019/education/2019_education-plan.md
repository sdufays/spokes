## Purpose of LFs
Show engineers/scientists can be athletic; show STEM is exciting; give kids resources/next steps.

## Locations and dates
Reuse past years' LF spreadsheets (2017, 2018) and choose which places to return to; brainstorm new places; route and LFs are interdependent (told route team top 3 from 2018). Once route is picked, contact places with a vague date, ask about space, resources, number of kids. Ask someone for last year's publicity email correspondence.

## Content
Collect workshop ideas via a shared sheet (people add names next to topics). Advice for finding topics: topics you loved learning; past years' ideas in Drive; MIT Splash/Spark catalogs (esp.mit.edu/learn/Splash/2018/catalog); Edgerton Center K-12 activities (edgerton.mit.edu/k-12/about). Choosing: think about takeaways, prior knowledge, number of students, whether students know each other, resources available/bringable.

## Structure / publicity
Last year (2018): one day, three 2-hr sessions 9-4 with an hour lunch. Hosts may be responsible for publicity; give them flyers. Collect feedback from students/schools to help future funding. Ideas: practice with nearby kids; night astronomy workshop; LF on a reservation. Past team (Kate and Delia) said kids were 6-18, avg 11-12; purpose should be fun, not super technical.

## Tasks
Ask for workshop ideas (done); choose workshops together; create titles/descriptions; develop curriculum; buy supplies; test and iterate; pick LF places; specify dates with route team.
