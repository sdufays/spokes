(Written Nov 2018 by the Spokes 2018 team for Spokes 2019.)

## To do
1. Get to know each other
2. Figure out what needs to be done
   - Money: grant applications (the earlier the better); crowdfunding (must be through MIT, not GoFundMe etc.); talk to Sandra J Lipnoski (Edgerton Center, "Sandi"/"Sandy")
   - Bikes/gear: used gravel bikes in 2018, but team can choose anything
   - Route: stops; places to stay (Warmshowers)
   - Curriculum: contact schools to set LF dates; design workshops in pairs; get materials, refine
3. Figure out who does what: Fundraising (start EARLY, "go crazy"; more than one person), Route/Hosts (see "epic spreadsheet", Warmshowers), LF setup, Bikes/Gear, Curriculum (all)
4. Set up regular meetings and track action items
5. Familiarize with the Spokes Google Drive; tons of email templates
6. Ask past teams for help anytime
7. Create a timeline (IMPORTANT)
8. Use MIT Edgerton Center contacts (Sandy etc.)

## Fundraising lessons (from 2018)
- Need more than one person on fundraising. Fundraise as early as possible: by spring, excess corporate money (Texas Instruments, Microsoft, Dell etc.) is already gone.
- Most of 2018's money came from MIT: everyone emailed their own department for donations; applied to several MIT scholarships/fellowships; got the TBP Fellowship for Summer 2018. Crowdfunding also brought in a lot; the person who coordinated social-media posts for it was very helpful.
- Other ideas: email local news stations with draft stories (little result; Boston Globe did not respond); reach out to past major donors (Sandi has a list); ask outdoors/cycling/food companies for in-kind (2018 got LARABARs, sunscreen, bike kit; "the bike kit sucked, you probably want to invest").
- Have a good sense of the trip before reaching out; rough curriculum drafts help. Companies wanted actual examples of what the team did, so website/social presence matters.

## Teaching / Learning Festival lessons
- Be careful about the timing of securing hosts vs LFs; in 2018 they had to rearrange after ordering them wrongly. Sunday LFs are normally a no-go, especially in religious areas. Confirm the school/learning center is OK with the date before arranging anything else; plan sequentially.
- Taught in pairs, each designed own curriculum covering varied topics with lots of hands-on activity. Activities must adapt on the fly to different ages. Revise workshops after teaching once or twice.
- Motto: "Always a little more adventure than we expect."
