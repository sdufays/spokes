Common elements: intro should come from you and be specific to your relationship; link donation page (2019: crowdfund.mit.edu/project/15470; giving.mit.edu) and website mitspokes.com; "if you can't donate, spread the word / sign up for updates".

**MIT department (professor):** "My name is Leah Yost and I am a current sophomore at MIT in Course 18... part of Spokes. In collaboration with Teach for America, the team this summer will bike from Washington D.C. to San Francisco... Spokes has been running every summer for seven years... last year's team received some funding from various MIT departments. I spoke to [name] and she said you would be best to talk to about funding from the [X] Department... would love to meet." Attach the longer info doc. Variant: relate your workshop to the department (e.g. Biology).

**Companies:** intro, "partnering with [company]"; attach longer document and formal request letter.

**General contacts:** "This summer I will be biking across America, DC to San Francisco, with the MIT Spokes team to teach STEM workshops in rural communities... Your support would help us buy bike gear, camping fees, food, gas, and educational equipment... carry us over 3,500 miles... Every little bit helps." Option to spread the word.

**Alumni groups (ESG example):** mention your tie to the group (ESG freshman year, TA for 8.01/18.03), the coordinator's role (e.g. San Francisco Bay Area ESG Alumni Branch Coordinator), ask to spread the word to other alumni. Variant for state alumni group (PA).

**Teachers:** personalized thank-you to former teachers; mention gratitude and challenging stereotypes about athleticism and STEM.

**Local businesses on route:** "Hello from the 2019 MIT Spokes America team! ... we will be stopping in [town] on [date]... We want to provide our workshops absolutely for free... partner with [business]... If unable to donate, share our mission with your customers." **News orgs:** same, ask them to share with readers/viewers.

**Panorama Education (Boston):** asks them to sponsor, noting 2019 is 7th year; since 2013, 8-10 MIT/Harvard students per year to towns like Hazard KY and Newton KS; sponsors help young people, connect teachers to resources, challenge stereotypes.

**Girls Who Code:** not asking for money but a social media shout-out to help overcome low participation in rural areas; mentions networking dinners about college applications.

**Church hosting request:** "I wasn't sure who to send this email to, so I thought as the administrative assistant you'd be able to direct me... MIT Spokes is a student group of eight MIT students biking from Washington DC to San Francisco... We will be passing through [town] on [date] and can't find any hosts. Would [church] be willing to host our team that night? If not in the building, we have camping equipment so a yard would be appreciated, or any member of the congregation... I understand I am a complete stranger; I can provide reference letters." Sent to Grace Heartland (Elizabethtown, Jun 15), New Hope Bible, Church on the Rock (Berea, Jun 13), Front Royal Church of Christ, Dynamic Life, First Assembly of God (Front Royal, May 29; "extremely last minute, sorry").
