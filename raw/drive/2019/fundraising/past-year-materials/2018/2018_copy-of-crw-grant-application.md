Please describe nature and primary focus of your organization: Spokes America is a six-year-old program that has MIT students cycle across the country, teaching students in middle America to engage with the scientific world around them. Along the way, the Spokes team hosts Learning Festivals at schools, libraries, museums, and summer camps, encouraging children from across the country to engage with the scientific world around them. Our trip will defy stereotypes about what it means to be a scientist, teaching kids that scientists and engineers can be athletic and engaged with their communities. Ultimately, we hope to inspire the next generation of scientists and engineers. 

Total organizational budget for the current fiscal year: Current Funds: $6,000 
 Project Cost: $25,000

Proposed Grant Amount: $4,000

Program Description: 8 students. 8 bikes. 3,500 miles. 75 days. An effort to inspire the next generation of STEM students and professionals. The Spokes team will be biking from DC to San Francisco, teaching at 9 schools and community centers along the way, listed below.

Louisa County Library, VA | June 1st
Challenger Learning Center, KY | June 13-14th
Lab: Revolution, MO | June 23rd
St. Louis Homeschool Connection, MO | June 26th
aSTEAM Village, MO | July 2nd
Newton Public Library, KS | July 5th
Loveland Public Library, CO | July 14th
Christa McAuliffe Space Center, UT | July 21st
Carson City Public Library, NV | July 31st

At each location, we will be teaching four workshops. These include “3… 2...1… Build!”, “Circuits Galore!”, “Playing with Python”, and “Picture This: The Science of Photography.” These workshops cover topics ranging from engineering design to physics to computer science, and will be adaptable to fit the needs of elementary through high school aged students. 

This is the 6th year of Spokes, so we have received guidance from members of past years’ teams. Additionally, we have an experienced cyclist on the team who has been a part of the MIT Cycling Club for years. From actresses to athletes and future Air Force members, our team members have a variety of interests and activities. Studying physics, aerospace engineering, computer science, electrical engineering, biological engineering, and mechanical engineering, we bring a strong STEM skillset. More importantly, we are passionate about teaching and sharing our enthusiasm with kids across the country. Many of us have experience leading STEM outreach programs in the past, whether it be through the Society of Women Engineers or our own initiatives. We have planned the entire trip ourselves, while remaining in contact with MIT staff. 

The estimated cost for the program is $25,000. This breaks down to about $10,000 for bikes, associated gear, and camping gear. Then, $6,000 will go toward the van rental and gas which we will switch off driving to carry our teaching materials and camping gear. Additionally, $8,000 goes toward food and miscellaneous supplies. The last $1,000 is for teaching materials and emergency purposes. So far, we have raised $5,000. $3,000 of that came from MIT departments and the remaining $2,000 has been generated through individual donors. 

Program Benefits: The goals are two-fold. On one hand, we are traveling to rural areas of the US to spread innovative, project-based STEM education to get students interested in science and engineering. On the other hand, the biking component of the trip also serves to break stereotypes about engineers. We aim to show that engineers like ourselves can be active, adventurous, and community-oriented. Plus, our project might also get students interested in biking as well! Our educational initiative also promotes cycling as a form of recreation and transportation.

Club Recognition: We will proudly recognize the club as a sponsor on our website. Additionally, the club will be featured on our social media (Instagram, Facebook). 

Measuring success: Success will be based on us making it across the country by bike and teaching at 9 schools. More specifically, seeing students interested in our activities and inquiring more about STEM will be a big victory. We will be surveying schools after our visits to inquire about their feedback from our Learning Festivals so that future teams can improve their educational outreach even more. Also, positive feedback from kids and parents will be a means to demonstrate success. Being asked back for future years will also indicate that things went well, which has happened throughout the past 5 years of Spokes. Finally, the publicity for our project across the US will generate a meaningful spread of our message and crusade for STEM. Past years have all successfully completed their journeys across the country, and this year’s team is just as ambitious as the last: along the way, we hope to continue a tradition of documenting our experience in small towns and big cities alike, sharing the experience of what it means to be an athletic and active engineer.
