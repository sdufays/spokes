Cover Page
Spokes America 2018
Delia Stephens, Kate Pearce, An Jimenez, Bethany Cates, Lisa Peng, Annie Phan, Matthew Luerman, and Alex Tran
Summer 2018

Project abstract
Provide a clear and concise description of your project and objectives. Avoid jargon and include information such as the project location, community partner and team members, if applicable. This can be copied from the Google Form portion of the application.
Spokes America is a six-year-old program that brings MIT students cycling across the country, teaching students in middle America to engage with the scientific world around them. Along the way, the Spokes team hosts learning festivals at schools, libraries, museums, and summer camps, encouraging children from across the country to engage with the scientific world around them. Our trip will defy stereotypes about what it means to be a scientist, teaching kids that scientists and engineers can be athletic and engaged with their communities. Ultimately, we hope to inspire the next generation of scientists and engineers. 

Background and Motivation
Introduce the community that your project intends to help, existing issues and needs, and why they are important to the community. In a broad sense, explain what impact your project will have on the community and address its sustainability, i.e. what are the lasting benefits of the project on the community after you leave?
	An early exposure to science, technology, engineering, and math is essential to any child’s education. Unfortunately, many children in middle America lack both access to STEM resources and role models to guide them to pursue their passions. STEM education in rural communities like those the Spokes America team will reach is particularly lacking; through our cycling program, we hope inspire students to chase their dreams. Our program will challenge stereotypes about what it means to be an engineer, teaching kids that scientists can be athletic, engaged, and active within their communities. 
Because Spokes America has visited many of the same communities in past years, we can see the lasting effects of our efforts: educators across the country are incredibly excited to have the program return. We have been contacted by parents and teachers across the country who are genuinely excited about getting their kids interested in STEM, and many have reported that they’ve seen engagement in their STEM-related curriculum increase after Spokes programs have passed through the community. We believe that the benefits of the Spokes America program are long-lasting and almost immeasurable; we are truly passionate about inspiring the next generation of scientists and engineers in traditionally underrepresented communities.

Project Plan
Outline the technical details of your project, with a focus on the goals you wish to achieve, the specific roles that you will play (experimentation, implementation, etc.), and how success will be evaluated. Include any figures and diagrams that may help us visualize your project. Also elaborate on additional safety precautions that will be taken in addition to those mentioned in your safety statement part of the Google Form, if necessary (especially if working in a region under travel warning).
	Beginning in Washington, DC, and ending in San Francisco, California, the Spokes team will bike across the country, hosting workshops in science, technology, engineering, and math. Our offered curriculum--a building workshop, an introduction to electronics, a short Python course, and a lesson in the physics of photography--will work together to introduce students to many different elements of STEM. The team will form four pairs of instructors responsible for hosting two 2-hour workshops over the course of a day so every student has a chance to encounter every concept. We will reuse much of the material from each workshop in order to save money; many of the projects are easy to set up and break down, ensuring a quick turnover between
	Between workshops, we will cycle across the country, staying in the homes of our generous hosts we have contacted through either personal connections or the cycling site https://www.warmshowers.org/. Along the way, we forge close connections with the hosts and their communities, sharing our interest in and excitement for STEM along the way. We hope to inspire the next generation of scientists and engineers to pursue their passions with pride, and we take immense joy in the fact that we are continuing the work of past years. Because future teams will go through the same regions, we are able to see firsthand the impact of our work on the communities we visit.
	We have been interfacing closely with the MIT Student Activities Office to ensure that safety criteria are met: each team member will undergo a background check, we have an emergency contact at MIT should something go awry, and we have filled out local and international travel forms. In addition to paperwork, we will purchase all of the necessary safety equipment: helmets, lights, and bright jerseys are necessary for the completion of this journey.
