Spokes America 2018
Delia Stephens, Kate Pearce, An Jimenez, 
Bethany Cates, Lisa Peng, Annie Phan, 
Matthew Luerman, and Alex Tran
Summer 2018

Abstract
Spokes America is a six-year-old student initiative that takes MIT students on a cross-country bike ride as an effort to rethink STEM education. Along the way, the Spokes team hosts 8-hour Learning Festivals at schools, libraries, museums, and summer camps, encouraging children from all across the country to engage with the scientific world around them. More specifically, Spokes targets rural communities that may not have a strong STEM curriculum in place. Moreover, our trip will defy stereotypes about what it means to be a scientist, teaching kids that scientists and engineers can be athletic and engaged with their communities too. Ultimately, we hope to inspire the next generation of scientists and engineers. 

Background and Motivation
An early exposure to science, technology, engineering, and math is essential to any child’s education. Unfortunately, many children in rural towns across America lack both the access to STEM resources and the role models to guide them to pursue their passions. STEM education in rural communities like these is imperative, and through Spokes, we will reach out directly to these communities. 
Because Spokes has visited many of the same communities in past years, we can see the lasting effects of our efforts: educators across the country are incredibly excited to have the program return, most of the times promising large numbers of attendees. Parents, teachers, and host families across the U.S. have reached out directly to Spokes and have shown a genuine interest in getting their kids excited about STEM, More importantly, many have reported that they’ve seen engagement in their STEM-related curriculum increase after Spokes programs have passed through the community during previous years. We believe that the benefits of Spokes program are long-lasting and almost immeasurable; we are truly passionate about inspiring the next generation of scientists and engineers in traditionally underrepresented communities.

Project Plan
Beginning in Washington, D.C., and ending in San Francisco, California, the Spokes team will bike across the country (all 3,500 miles in 75 days) and host over nine Learning Festivals along the way. Our offered curriculum - Design Challenges, Circuit Building, Introduction to Python, and The Physics of Photography - are designed to complement the school’s curriculum and highlight MIT’s hands-on approach to education. The team will form four pairs of instructors responsible for hosting two 2-hour workshops over the course of a day so every student has a chance to encounter every concept. We will reuse much of the material from each workshop in order to save money; in fact, many of the projects are easy to set up and break down, ensuring a quick turnover between workshops and Learning Festivals. 
	For most of our trip, we’ll be on the road cycling. We’ll be staying in the homes of our generous hosts whom we have contacted through either personal connections or the cycling site https://www.warmshowers.org/. Along the way, we will forge close connections with the hosts and their communities, sharing our interest in and excitement for STEM along the way. We hope to inspire the next generation of scientists and engineers to pursue their passions with pride, and we take immense joy in the fact that we are continuing the work of past years. Because future teams will go through the same regions, we are able to see firsthand the impact of our work on the communities we visit.
	We have been interfacing closely with the MIT Student Activities Office to ensure that safety criteria are met. In addition to the safety paperwork, we will purchase all of the necessary safety equipment: helmets, lights, bright jerseys, first-aid kits, etc. are necessary for the completion of this journey. 

Timeline
Team members have been working on this project since February of 2018. To date, we have fundraised through corporate sponsorships and our MIT Crowdfund page. We’ve reached out to hosts on Warmshowers, receiving confirmation from nearly all of them, and contacted all 9 schools. Seven of those schools have confirmed that we can host workshops; as of right now, only two haven’t confirmed the specific dates we requested. We hope to have finished our planning stage by May 17th, nearly two weeks before the trip; this extra time allows us to make any last-minute adjustments and ensure that the logistics are fully confirmed before we leave from Washington, DC.

Personal Statements
Bethany Cates
“I love pretty much all things outdoors, and have gotten really into biking since college; I've been contemplating a cross-country bike trip for a while now. When I heard about Spokes, I got really excited: I could complete the cross-country trip and extend STEM to students in rural communities? Coming from a low-income background myself, I'm acutely aware of the stark disparities in exposure and resources between schools that regularly produce students that go to MIT, and those that, well, don't; it's really important to me to try and erase those disparities where I can, so Spokes was kind of a perfect fit.”

Kate Pearce

Once I learned more about the mission to teach and spread STEM to kids across the US, I knew I needed to be a part of the team.
