Project Purpose
	The Spokes 2014 team seeks to inspire collaboration and understanding between different racial groups in the US by fostering a passion for science and engineering among children of all cultural identities.

Background
The current US K-12 education system is struggling to adequately prepare certain groups of students to enter technical fields such as the physical sciences, computer programming, and engineering. This achievement gap is due to a variety of factors, ranging from socioeconomic differences to how well the conventional education system incorporates the norms and values of each culture. If this gap is not addressed, it could perpetuate certain stereotypes, while fostering a sense of insecurity and create walls between different cultural groups, hindering mutual understanding and collaboration.
Furthermore, many communities of the communities that lack adequate preparation for entering STEM fields have also been affected by a number of setbacks. In addition to being less wealthy, these communities are often also poverty stricken and plagued by gang activity. If a nation can keep young people in school to develop technical competence, it will lower the chances of children becoming involved in dangerous crimes, increase their access to well-paid, value-creating jobs that help individuals enter the middle class and help the country strengthen its economic base.
Though there are already many resources available to help K-12 students master technical skills, teachers and students are often unaware of them and rarely use these programs to their full potential. Spokes’ plan is to take these resources into the classroom and to collaborate with teachers to make them an integral part of their teaching plans and assure that the information and lessons are reaching our specific and intended audience.

Program Details
Our goal is to teach at over ten different schools over the course of the summer. We expect to reach between 30 and 80 students at each school. During this time, team members will also be documenting their experiences on social media and on film, so that we can inspire a wider audience to explore STEM subjects with the same online resources that we will demonstrate in classrooms. We will also offer our lesson plans to any interested educational professionals. We are focusing on reaching under-resourced areas which will have the most to gain from using free online resources to teach STEM subjects, so many of our teaching stops will be in rural areas.
Our project will consist of a cross-country bicycle journey, with frequent stops at schools along the way where we will organize “learning festivals” focused on teaching subjects in science and engineering and encouraging students to build teamwork skills and understanding of each. We decided to travel by bicycle as it allows us to travel to rural areas that are not often visited by larger “traveling circus” STEM shows and demonstrate our excitement to engage with the students and their surroundings.

Partners and Collaborators
Spokes America will be collaborating heavily with Teach For America in planning our hands-on lessons and connecting with partner schools. Our financial and project sponsors will include Raspberry Pi and Khan Academy. Additionally, we will publicize our work through blog posts, video posts, compilations of lesson plans, and, potentially, by presenting our findings to Department of Education staff in Washington DC at the culmination of our trip.

Primary Goals and Feasibility
We want to empower our students to teach themselves mathematics, computer programming or engineering. We want to show them that building, tinkering, creating and exploring is a way of life, and that there are resources available for them to create anything they want, even if they do not have access to laboratories or machine shops. We want to encourage teamwork and communication in children as young engineers in hopes that a common interest will give way to future mutual understanding. We want to change the way STEM is taught in the classroom, so that the students are not just inspired for a weekend, but become passionate for a lifetime of learning.
Once on the road we will measure our progress by several metrics. The most obvious will be the miles we cover, but we will also collect feedback at every school we visit to learn how we were received and what we could improve on for the next learning festival. We will also be posting online regular updates of our teaching experiences, to allow us to receive online feedback from readers and viewers. The bulk of the funding we receive will be used over the course of the summer, but we will use our online presence and our post-trip reports to prove that Spokes is a valuable program that should be supported for the next several years; in essence, the funding that we receive now will be used to prove that Spokes should receive larger investments in the future.
Finally, we are confident this trip is feasible, because the first iteration of Spokes America (2013) successfully completed with its given resources.

Community Impact
	The desired magnitude and effect of our project is feasible for a team of our size. We seek to create an example of how online resources can be integrated into the classroom. We hope students with continue with informal STEM projects outside the classroom.  To extend the impact of our work, our sponsors have offered to donate electronics to each school we visit.
	Spokes will also be influential to the schools we don’t visit – by sharing our experiences, we chart a path that other educators can follow. We hope to inspire teachers and students alike to explore how education can be improved, how anyone can be a maker, and how we can find creative solutions to societal barriers.
	We hope Spokes will also have secondary effects, inspiring similar projects by other students, and showing the value of such projects to sponsors. We believe the human connection to our students is a crucial part of this project -- this will encourage others to form connections with cultures other than their own, and show the power of investing in struggling communities.  Those connections can produce reconciliation, and that investment might provide alternatives to poverty and crime.
