General: For the summer of 2017, a team of 8 MIT and Harvard students will bike from Washington D.C. to San Francisco to increase interest in science, technology, engineering, and math (STEM). Along the way, we will stop at schools, libraries, museums, and camps to hold learning festivals that encourage hands-on, project-based learning to students who do not often see a lot of STEM in their daily curriculum. Our goal is to encourage students to ask questions and use creative problem-solving skills to satisfy their own curiosity, and by doing this, inspiring them to become the next generation of scientists and engineers.

Alternate:
Since its founding in 2013, Spokes America has sent 8-10 MIT and Harvard students to bike cross country over the summer and teach STEM to middle and high school students. Along the way, Spokes will stop at schools, libraries, museums, and camps to hold learning festivals that encourage hands-on, project-based learning to students who do not often see a lot of STEM in their daily curriculum. Our goal is to encourage students to ask questions and use creative problem-solving skills to satisfy their own curiosity, and by doing this, inspiring them to become the next generation of scientists and engineers. Since 2013, we have taught at small rural towns such as Hazard, KY and Newton, KS, along with larger cities, including St. Louis, MO and  Denver, CO.

History: This is the fifth year that Spokes America will be biking across the country. Since 2013, we have taught at small rural towns, including Hazard, KY and Newton, KS, to well-known, large cities, including St. Louis, MO and  Denver, CO. Each year, 8-10 students have successfully biked across the country, spreading the love for STEM wherever they go. We currently have a rough outline of our curriculum planned out, confirmation for the nine learning festivals we will be hosting this summer, and many of the places that we are planning to stay along our route.
The most important thing now is to raise the money to actually make our trip possible.
