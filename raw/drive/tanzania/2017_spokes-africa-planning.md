Thinking about overall mission of Spokes in Tanzania:

I think it’s important to be conscious of making Spokes in Tanzania a partnership, not something where the help/assistance is going one way, but where foreigners and Tanzanians are mutually learning/helping each other. I think we can do this by:
Being specific about the fact that we want to travel in Tanzania (as opposed to East Africa, or Africa in general)
Like we talked about, this is a partnership that will benefit the foreign (i.e. american) participants as much as the Tanzanian participants
Getting educated on Tanzania’s many cultures, languages, regions, etc
Thinking about how this can make Spokes’ work better by refining our curricula, finding ways to simplify the equipment needed without losing any of the educational benefits
This might be a good opportunity to make our teaching workshops more “lean”, with less equipment, more focus on ingenuity, creativity and teaching basic concepts
Maybe we can teach more about computer theory through demonstrations with people - like teach sorting algorithms by sorting students by height, or solving logic puzzles, etc?
Building things with whatever we find in the area
Taking apart a cell phone to learn about injection molding, or electronics
Building simple turbines from cans
Learning about acoustics and waves by building musical instruments
Botany relating to local plant life, medicinal uses, etc
How a bicycle works - we could take apart one of our bicycles as a demo
We could invite students to ride with us for a day around their neighborhood
We could carry an extra bike on our support vehicle (if we had one) and let anyone who wanted to join us for a day ride it
We might want to train up much more on bicycle repair before doing this trip
Replacing components
Replacing cables
Fixing derailleurs
Tuning
Welding frames
Flats
Balancing wheels
Focus on using the bicycle as a teaching tool
Power source for blenders, electricity, etc
Physics - forces, rotation
Loads, cantilever beams, engineering
Bearings
Gears
Pulleys
If we are communicating what we are doing back to the States, we need to think carefully about how we represent Tanzania
We don’t want to show it as just a poor, crowded country full of uneducated people
We want to show that it has industry, and innovative people, and educated people even if their lifestyles look very different from ours
We want to make life in Tanzania relatable
We want to show that tanzania has rural and urban regions just like the US, that it is not a homogenous country
How could Spokes Tanzania help Spokes America?
Start an exchange program between Tanzania and the US
Of students
Of ideas - ways of refining our teaching programs to be more robust and universally comprehensible, and more applicable to all kinds of walks of life
Invite Tanzanians to teach us about their innovative skills and take that back to the US
Maybe this expands so that we could do something similar in another foreign country
Cycling is a great way to explore a new place because you are completely immersed in the experience
You’re not enclosed in a vehicle
You go slow enough to see things
You talk to people
You live in different communities for a time, not just hotels in major cities
You experience other ways of life
Spokes gives you a way to engage with all kinds of people
Maybe this becomes a way for American students to travel the US and foreign countries and learn more about environments different from their homes
Like Outward Bound in the sense that it educates young people by exposing them to new and different experiences
Promote understanding of international communities and empathic connections with other peoples
Within the US we might want to recruit participants who have not already experienced other cultures so that they could try it for the first time
