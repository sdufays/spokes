**Campaign:** Kickstarter (some docs say Indiegogo) launched ~May 8 then pushed to June; target $8,500-10,000 ("$1.5K per class"). Purpose reframed: fund 25-50 student projects and supplies; stretch goal over $40,000 = build an online platform for students/mentors.
**Story structure used:** Who we are -> big vision (every student deserves a mentor + project) -> what we're doing (8 students, 4,000 miles, 10 towns, 80 days, Learning Festivals) -> how (TFA, Freeflow Bikes, North Face, mentors) -> what your money supports -> class descriptions (curricula shared under Creative Commons) -> mentoring plan (CORI-checked mentors, weekly Skype, TFA teacher facilitating) -> stretch goals.
**Video notes:** ~3 minutes; great music and audio; one narrator plus partner testimonials; state clear end goal; "kicker: biking"; B-roll of biking, MIT, class demos, whiteboarding, spinning wheel; 10-second smiling headshots from every member.
**Reward tiers (5/14 meeting):** $5 website thank-you + note; $25 video thank-you + laptop stickers; t-shirt; $100 + 25x36" plant poster signed by students; $150 + personalized video; $300 Spokes jersey; $600 invitation to DC VIP dinner; $1,500 workshop in your name anywhere in New England; $5,000 workshop anywhere in US. (Also discussed: 3 $500 tickets to DC send-off dinner.)
**Advice from Kevin Rustagi (MIT alum, 5/1):** video is crucial; email a huge number of people right at the start; pick 7-10 groups you belong to and contact a few people in each; ask friends to each contact 40 people and track; PR on launch minute; set goal low and blow it out, or high and push; day-one momentum ("HUGE first day"); referral tracking shows which blogs sent backers.
**End-of-summer video (V2) goals:** show MIT president (Rafael Reif) the method to win support for future teams; headers for blog posts (30-45 s teaser); invite students to start their own Spokes team ("Do you want to start your own team? www.spokesamerica.org"). Claims: ~400 students in 10 locations; footage ideas: trebuchet in Millstadt, mousetrap cars in Oakland, Arduinos in Nashville/Lexington. Difficulty: cannot quantify impact on kids.
