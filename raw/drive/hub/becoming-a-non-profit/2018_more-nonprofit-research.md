## Sheet: Board and Structure

Usually only 1-5 directors on the board; they set the overall strategy/mission of the company.
Officers (president, treasurer, secretary) and employees are separate from board members.
The board members can also be employees/officers (and often are in small nonprofits) but you have to keep in mind that they have to avoid conflicts of interest.
Board members cannot profit excessively from the corporation.
If the board is voting on employee salaries and one of the board members is also an employee, they should recuse themselves from the vote since that would be a conflict of interest.
Officers are CEO, COO, CFO. They have more responsibility, less rights than typical employees. They are chosen by the board and can be fired by the board.
https://www.501c3.org/nonprofits-board-directors-board/
https://www.501c3.org/avoiding-conflicts-of-interest/
## Sheet: Apply for EIN

The person applying for the EIN must be a "responsible and duly authorized member or officer of the exempt organization." Your authorized agent or incorporator can also file for the EIN on your behalf.
That person will use his or her personal social security number for the application. However, once the EIN is established, no personal social security number will be needed.
The person applying for the EIN will need to have, besides his or her social security number, your organization's legal name and both the mailing address and the physical address of your organization.
This person may be our registered agent

## Sheet: Glossary

| IRS 501(c)(3) Determination Letter |   | This document that proves that a charity has been granted tax-exemption |
|---|---|---|
| Employer Identification Number |   | The EIN works just like individual Social Security numbers and all businesses (including nonprofits) need one |
| C Corporation |   | C corporations offer more flexibility than S corporations when it comes to the number of owners (shareholders) they can have as well as who can be an owner. Plus, the owners can hold different types of stock interests (such as preferred versus common stock.) This is one reason why venture capitalists choose C corporations when they offer funding to a business. Investors are drawn with the prospect of dividends if the corporation makes a profit. A C corporation can also retain and accumulate earnings (within reasonable limits) from year to year. |
| S Corporation |   | An S corporation allows the owners to offset non-business income with losses from the business, unlike a C corporation which is a completely separate tax entity. When compared to an LLC, the S corporation enables an owner to characterize a portion of income as “dividend,” rather than “earnings” that are subject to employment taxes. |
| LLC |   | Consider if you are fearful of personal risks to lawsuits arising from your business |
| Ethics Code |   | Shareholders' expectations of the corporation and officers |
| Code of Conduct |   | The corporation's expectations for the employees |
