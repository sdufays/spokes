We believe in the power of students to direct their own learning. A good teacher serves as a mentor that guides the students in their quest for knowledge, holding them as if they were learning to bike for the first time. A great teacher knows when to let go. The student is the one learning, and needs to be given ownership over that learning.

Imagine an environment where students worked alongside their mentors, learning from one another and empowering each other. Imagine students had the confidence to make their own decisions of what to learn. Imagine students not learning through lectures or in classrooms, but through hands-on projects in the beautiful outdoors where they are immersed in the material.

Enough imagining; let’s make this a reality. This summer, a team of 7 college undergraduates—students ourselves—will bike across the country to introduce such an environment in towns across the United States to foster student’s love of science and engineering. At our Learning Festivals, we will work alongside middle and high school students as we build everything from model rockets to robots. We will be role models, mentors, and peers—all at once. Just as much as we want to share our love for the sciences and engineering, we want the students to discover their unbounded imagination and creativity.

We recognize that self-directed learning requires practice. Not only are we encouraging all the students we meet on our journey to continue tinkering, building, and inventing, we're inviting students anywhere in the United States to apply to our new grant program to grow their projects, their communities, and themselves. With funding and mentorship, students will be able to invent and engineer innovative solutions to problems facing their local communities. They'll have guidance from college students who will double as role models. Not only do we hope the students undergo a transformative learning experience, we hope they find ways to give back to their towns and cities through their inventions.

This is more than just some summer plans. This is a vision for a more self-directed way of learning—a new model for education. If you are as excited about our vision as we are, we want to hear from you! You can follow the project by joining our email list and receiving an executive summary of the trip and our progress at the end of the summer.

Help us empower students everywhere. Join the Spokes movement.

Operating Procedures
We are our each others’ keepers; we will always have our teammates’ health and safety as our #1 priority.
We will always strive to make a unanimous decision when the decision is critical to the project. If we cannot reach full agreement, we will decide in favor of the majority vote, a majority being 4 or more team members.
We will always be direct in our communication with each other; if something is wrong or missing or problematic, we will bring it to the attention of the group.
We will always stay positive and remember that this is the greatest adventure of our lives. If we’re struggling to deal with the difficulty of the ride or something else, we will fall back on the support of our teammates for strength.
We will always treat every person we encounter on the trip with respect and good humor.
We will keep an open mind and a spirit of exploration so that we can get the most out of this adventure.
We will be sensitive to our teammates and know when they’re having a bad day and when they need some support or cheering up, and act on that intuition.
We will stay in close contact with our support team at MIT and across the US via phone, e-mail and social media.
