[Chemistry] Flames and Explosions
Would consist of many demos that could interact some (but not all) students and would culminate in every student making their own bottle rocket!
Click the links below to see the demo videos!
Advantages: I’m really excited about this, would help students build an intuition for what seems like a simple concept (combustion and flames) but actually isn’t, they could make their own bottle rockets, many parts would be hands on, based on a similar program my grandfather (a chemist) did at schools for years with much success
Disadvantages: nothing to really take home at the end, part of it would be demos and not hands-on, may only take a few hours (not a full day) → would be better as a filler

Learning goals
Have students understand the reactants and products of combustion and build an intuition for what they look like
Have students understand the anatomy of a flame
Have students understand the conversation of matter in reactions
Have students understand the difference between a diffusion flame and premixed flame
Have students understand how combustion leads to propulsion and explosions
Exercises
Understand anatomy of a flame
Put wire mesh / metal gauze over candle to show candle is hollow (anatomy of flame)
Collect soot (matter is conserved)
Self-trimming wicks
Apply concepts of hollow flame
Soak towel or dollar bills in alcohol and light with tongs
Fish tank of CO2
Baking soda and vinegar to produce CO2
Blow bubbles that float on invisible gas
Flow CO2 down staircase and repeat
Propane down staircase
Like CO2, heavier than air, but a reactant of combustion
Pour down staircase and ignite
Propane bubbles
Light them in your hands
Hydrogen balloons vs. oxygen balloon explosions
Pre-mixed vs. diffusion
Bottle rockets
Yeast and hydrogen peroxide (oxygen rich)
Isopropyl alcohol
Heat with blowtorch

Final Curriculum Proposal:

2 Classes: CS and MechE
have the two classes combine for final project (battlebots type thing or another intersection of building and programming [cool lego robot])
get kids excited about the possibilities with programming and engineering
(watch this video and then this one)
best to have the two groups colaborate on a final project, so all the kids are proud of the same outcome
- [CS]
	- Day 1: Start with Scratch or something similar to get basics of programming
		- maybe see if we can partner with CS50 and receive some funding from them
	- Day 2: have skeleton code written, for final project, just have kids add specific parts (i.e. make arm move when …)
		- create code for a working robot!
	- Basic Goals:
expose kids to programming basics (how to structure a conditional expressions)
show them online resources and places to code
expose them to a more advanced language with our own code
go into the basics of HTML if they’re interested in how websites are formed
- [MechE]
	- Day 1: Basics of circuits, electricity, and how to problem solve → give them examples of past problems and solutions from engineering
		- maybe a short project with something like these small solar powered cars
	- Day 2: Focus on building the final robot (if thats what we choose)
		- demonstrate and have fun with the working robot!
	- Basic Goals:
expose kids to critical thinking / problem solving
engage with lots of activities and projects
show kids that engineering is fun → some fun videos or examples online
