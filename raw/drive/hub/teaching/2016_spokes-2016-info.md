What were the formats?
Our workshops became collections of fun hands on activities that conveyed some of the key ideas in the field.

We all realized that we should shift emphasis from information retention to hyping kids up and getting them interested and getting them engaged and getting them to believe in themselves. Geared workshops to make it more and more hands on and interactive.

What important changes/adjustments did you make during the trip?
Some people didn’t have enough material. Scrambled to fill in more material

The workshops got better and better but it took some tweaking.

Chloe - Ended up removing a lot of teaching material and giving a lot more time to the interactive bits. More time for creative ideas from students. EG had a noise-making device that connected to your arm and picked up muscle nervous systems. Kids wanted to put it on their face or arm wrestle with it on.

Reading the room. Don’t let them control everything, but workshops can be free flowing depending on how the kids are engaged.

Having feedback forms was invaluable!

What were the most valuable lessons that the students got from your workshop?
Having kids become more confident to do things that they think are hard, or that they have experienced to be too hard for them to do. A lot of the times they would start off the workshop by inviting everyone to write anything they wanted to know about the brain. At the beginning they would mostly not come up for fear of sharing because they didn’t feel they knew anything.

Important to have kids do something that has a clear end result that they can reach.

Student Reaction
What was the optimal classroom size that you taught?
Really depends on the workshop. For neuroscience did around 15 kids. Smaller ones would be tough to do group activities in. And hard for kid moral.

Rocketry: had trouble with more kids. Had a lot of classroom management work they had to do.

15 is general the sweet spot.
What age group of students (K-5, 6-8, 9-12) worked best with your workshop? (Were the most attentive, learned the most, had the most fun, whatever you felt like “clicked”)
5th to 12th grade. Sometimes they had this span in the same room. Esp. hard for the math problem solving group.

What was the format at each teaching location?
2 days. One full day one half day.
Three teaching blocks:
9:30 - 12:30
1:30 - 4:30
9:30 - 12:30 second day
Had students self select into the different workshops. If the numbers were off, they would ask some people switch. People generally shifted over.
Kids ended up doing 3 out of 4 workshops
Kids chose their workshop right before the next workshop

Most places offered them lunch. Most schools had the kids all together eating lunch. In some of the larger cities the students would drift off more, but generally everyone together.

Sign in and pick up were organized by the school

Showed up an hour early to get set up

All logistics arranged by hosts.
