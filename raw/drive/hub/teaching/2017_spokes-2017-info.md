What were the formats?
4 Different Workshops that were co-taught by two instructors (except for one workshop because a member dropped out before trip started)

Aimed to be 3 hour long workshops, with an emphasis on hands-on activities and less on “lecturing”
	Some workshops ended up being shorter/longer due to nature of activities (breadboarding was longer, cow eye dissection was shorter)

Aimed towards high school level kids

Class sizes were determined by interest of children and time alotted at school (i.e, were pretty flexible)

What important changes/adjustments did you make during the trip?
Some workshops needed to be “toned down” (no calculus optimization) because of lower than expected age groups

Some workshops were changed for variety sake (different dissections)

Some parts were added at request of children (physics workshop had cannonball wars which were not originally planned but lots of fun for kids)

What were the most valuable lessons that the students got from your workshop?
	There is always somewhere to learn about science or anything online - if you want to learn something, you can learn it!

Math and Science can be fun! It’s not a boring thing you learn at school, it can have applications!

Student Reaction
What was the optimal classroom size that you taught?
	Physics: 25ish (could accommodate more because open build workshop)
	Biology:25ish (could accommodate more because lecture and dissections)
	Math: 10ish (Had to be lower because of one teacher)
	EECS: 12 (had to be lower because of limited supplies)

What age group of students (K-5, 6-8, 9-12) worked best with your workshop? (Were the most attentive, learned the most, had the most fun, whatever you felt like “clicked”)
	6-8: Best age because kids were into it while still learning new things, and they seemed to have fun because lots of the experiences were new.

K-5: Good age group, but our workshops were sometimes a little too complicated for certain kids. This age group had lots of fun during the hands-on activity, but could also get out of hand if too many kids. Could tone workshops down by adding even more hands on activities, and less lecturing.

9-12: Always a very well-behaved group, but many students were bored or went through our workshops incredibly fast. Could tone workshops up by increasing complexity of activities (EECS workshop went very well with 9-12)
