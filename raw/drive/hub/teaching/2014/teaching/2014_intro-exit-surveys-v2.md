Here are my initial thoughts on intro / exit surveys (the exit survey ideas are on the next page).  Please share comments and concerns, add suggestions, let me know if you think anything is missing or unnecessary, etc.

Intro Survey

The current draft is set up to show what I think some of our main goals in the teaching workshops are, and what sorts of questions we could ask both before and after to try and assess if we’ve met any of those goals or made any visible changes on the students’ thinking.  (This is a rough outline, so please let me know if anything is unclear!)

Goal: Help introduce kids to engineering/design, and what that means.
Questions:
What do you think engineering is?
Design?

Goal: Get kids excited about engineering and design in everyday life.
Questions:
What does an engineer do?
What do you think of when you think of an engineer?
Have you ever engineered or designed something in your own life?
Are there any scientists or engineers whom you admire?
Do you like to build things? (Scale 1-5)
Do you like to play with Legos? (Scale 1-5)
Could also ask them to rate how true some of these statements are for them on a scale of 1-5

Goal:  Build kids’ confidence to tinker and explore, and to learn on their own.
Questions:
(Quantitative?)  How comfortable are you building something?  Could you design something?
(Creative design question?  Draw us a cool design for ____!) I like this one!

Goal:  Show kids that engineering and learning are fun!  Possibly encourage kids to pursue them in the future.
Questions:
What is your favorite subject in school?
How much do you like STEM?  (Include quantitative questions for how much students enjoy STEM classes and work, how excited they are about STEM in school and in the world, etc.)
How often do you watch the Discovery Channel? (Other TV shows/media?)
Do you have any ideas about what you want to do after high school?  and/or after college?

Goal:  Help raise kids’ awareness and knowledge of a few subject areas / fields, or fun technologies etc.
Questions:
Depending on classes, ask about knowledge in certain areas - e.g. comfort level with math 1-5, do you enjoy your math class?
What do you know about programming?
Have you ever made anything before?

Exit Survey

The theory of the exit survey would be both to (1) figure out if our classes helped in any of the goals we set (are kids more excited about STEM now?  Do they have a better idea of what that means?  A lot of these questions will probably be similar to the intro survey questions), and (2) get feedback on our classes and ways to improve for future iterations.

Goal: Help show what engineering/design is.
Questions:
What is engineering?
What is design?
Is this different than what you thought before Spokes?

Goal: Get kids excited about engineering and design in everyday life.
Questions:
What does an engineer do?
What do you think of when you think of an engineer?  Did this change?
Have you ever engineered or designed something in your own life?

Goal:  Build kids’ confidence to tinker and explore, and to learn on their own.
Questions:
(Quantitative?)  How comfortable are you building something?  Did this change?
Could you design something?

Goal:  Show kids that engineering and learning are fun!  Possibly encourage kids to pursue them in the future.
Questions:
What is your favorite subject in school?
How much do you like STEM?
Do you have any ideas about what you want to be after high school? and/or after college?

Goal:  Help raise kids’ awareness and knowledge of a few subject areas / fields, or fun technologies etc.
Questions:
Ask the same class-specific questions to get feedback on learning progress
This might sound too much like a test

Feedback:
What is something you learned in your Spokes class?
Did you enjoy the experience? What did you learn?
What was your favorite part of the class(es)?
What was your least favorite?  Do you have any suggestions for improvements, or any other classes you’d like to see?
Would you like to stay in touch throughout the year, and continue this sort of learning?  Would you continue to learn/tinker on your own?  After-school?  If so, how could we best help you?  (Emails with cool projects, website to talk to other people designing things, etc.?)
Overall experience?
Any other comments?  :)

Emma’s random comments:
Relative length of surveys? I think shorter is better because kids have short attention spans
Asking quantitative questions or questions that use number ratings would be useful because we could analyze that data nicely to compare our effectiveness at different schools
Also asking kids questions related to their daily lives (TV shows they watch, activities they do, games they play) probably gives us a good insight into their interests
I like the idea of asking the kids to design something quickly on paper because that is open-ended so they can show us who they are
Like “how many ways could you improve a manhole cover?” or something (NO JOKE I actually had that question in an interview once)
