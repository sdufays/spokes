Educational Outcomes
Major Themes
Empowerment
Confidence that they can affect the world / their community with their work
Pushed to learn skills/ways of thinking students may not have otherwise pursued (due to lack of confidence, resources, etc)
Students gain comfort that they can build things on their own (physical)
Students feel like they have control over their own learning
Students produce something individually or in teams
HAVE FUN!
Tangible skills
Come away with some tangible achievement that can be built on/expanded
Getting the students to get something out of the teaching festivals was the main goal, whether that something be to know and understand binary, love the fact that they know binary and go on to learn more about circuits and computers, or just to love the fact that they could learn things in general
New experiences
Learn about our perspective on the world/learn from our experiences
Students do something creative
Students participate in hands-on activities
Students tackle and completely fail at something they’ve always wanted to do
Students have experience trying to do something they think they can’t do (“Flailing”)
Change perception of learning
Restructure their notions about learning ( X subject is boring, etc) by making things hands-on and hacky so more motivated to learn when we are gone
Learning as exploration - flexibility to direct their own learning
Students can take a starting activity and do their own thing
Learning how to learn
Students have taken a step towards managing their own learning process.
Can state what it is they are trying to do/learn
Can find a resource that might help
Can try doing something in the real world that might help
Can break down their big goal into a few starting point steps
Students have communicated their idea to someone else, and can have multi-step conversations to clarify someone elses understanding
Students can recognize a skill they developed during Spokes
Students change their opinion positively about subject/subjects
Build long-lasting enthusiasm for learning in communities
Students interact with university students or alumni and get excited by their personal interests or current work
Students feel like their learning is connected to things that are relevant to their lives (interests, community, etc.)
Life-long learning attitude (excitement about self-directed learning)
Students feel connected to careers or long-term self-directed projects
Comfort learning new topics - including STEM?
Better performance in classes
Better engagement…
See relevance of classroom learning?
After the workshop is over students are still learning or building new things
Communities are more connected than before - new friends in neighboring towns, etc.
Students start their own project after we leave

Alternative words to “STEM”
Hands-on learning, lifelong learning, self-directed learning, creative work, theory v. practice, building things, flailing, own learning process, open-ended learning, hacky learning
