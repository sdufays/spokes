# Highlights: Spokes Hub (cross-year, 2013-2018; some roster data to 2019)

- The Spokes Hub (2017-2018) was an alumni group trying to centralize past resources for new teams: routes, equipment, budgets, host contacts, curricula, waivers, mentors, a wiki. This folder is the surviving archive; most useful for curriculum, school/host contacts and the legal/liability story (2017-2018, source: About Spokes Hub, Spokes Hub Timeline).
- Recommended 2018 team roles (from the 2017 team): Fundraising (2 people, contacts companies), Budget (1, controls spending), Route (2, Warmshowers hosts and campgrounds), Equipment (1, bikes/supplies/car rental), Education (1, learning festivals), Social media (1). Each paired with a Hub mentor (2017, source: Mentorship Ideas). Compare to the 2027 role split (fundraising, finance, food, social media).
- Spending priority order proposed for equipment budgets: 1 bikes, 2 plane tickets, 3 first-aid training, 4 car, 5 AAA membership, 6 bike accessories, 7 bike training, 8 health and safety, 9 camping/travel equipment, 10 warm clothes, 11 bike clothes, 12 jerseys (2017, source: Required Equipment).
- Learning-festival format that worked: 1-2 day stops, 15-60 students, 3-8 workshops, max 8 students per instructor, students choose workshops, 15 students is the sweet spot, arrive an hour early, hosts handle sign-in and lunch (2016/2017, source: Workshop Summary Sheet; Spokes 2016 Info).
- 2017 workshop costs: biology (cow-eye dissection) about 150-200 per festival (about 2,000 total); physics about 200 total; circuits about 300 total; math about 10 total. All fit in car boxes (2017, source: Spokes 2017 Info).
- Age-group lessons: grades 6-8 were the best fit; K-5 needs more hands-on and less lecture; 9-12 get bored quickly so raise complexity. Tone down content when ages are lower than expected (2017, source: Spokes 2017 Info).
- Shift emphasis from information retention to excitement and confidence: cut lecture, give more time to interactive/creative bits; feedback forms were "invaluable" (2016, source: Spokes 2016 Info).
- Best past teaching hosts per 2017 ratings: Challenger Learning Center of Kentucky (Hazard KY), Lab: Revolution (Farmington MO), University Academy and Alta Vista Charter (Kansas City) rated 5; Exploration Place, Carson City library, The Leonardo rated lower. A Loveland CO host family was "the best" (2017, source: School Feedback, Teaching Location Info).
- Loveland CO library (2017) had no students show up because it was not advertised: always confirm advertising and attendance with the host (2017, source: Teaching Location Info).
- 2013 trip contacted schools through Teach for America contacts (Oakland, Sacramento, Colorado Springs) and found Telluride hosts on Warmshowers; confirmed-venue sheet shows how far ahead stops were locked (2013, source: Confirmed Schools; Spokes Teaching Locations 2013).
- Cold-email templates for schools and a logistics questionnaire for hosts (numbers, time, space, supplies) are ready to reuse (2014/2016, source: Email to schools; Logistics Questionnaire v2; Cold Email to School).
- Bike & Build safety model to copy: online safety courses with quizzes before riders get a bike, 500 training miles, at least one 65-mile ride, a ride in the rain, group ride, orientation with hand signals, a 30-mile shakedown ride (2018, source: Bike and Build Meeting).
- Liability advice from Bike & Build (Ron): you cannot prevent people suing officers; carry D and O insurance, name directors/officers explicitly in waivers, have a lawyer review (2018, source: Bike and Build - Meeting with Ron April 25, 2018).
- Fiscal sponsor typically takes about 5% of donations and handles accounting and insurance; MIT can play this role (2018, source: Peter Crosby - 29 Mar 2018). In 2013 donations were routed via StartLabs, a 501(c)(3) (2013, source: DonationRequest_Final).
- Non-profit advice: turnover of students is the core problem; build a small board with 3+ year terms for continuity; find a non-MIT parent org; BC law clinic declined the case; Delaware registered-agent service about 180 first year (2017-2018, source: Notes from Joanne Mtg; Rolling Notes; Registered Agents List).
- Draft waiver and MIT Domestic Travel risk-acknowledgement form exist; plan was to have all riders sign an indemnification waiver naming Hub members (2018, source: Spokes Waiver 5_29_2018; Waiver to Indemnify Spokes Hub).
- Recruiting target for Hub funding: 10-20K total from donors giving about 1,000 each, to hire a grant writer; each member lists 4 and contacts 2 advisors and 2 funders (2018, source: Recruiting Advisors & Funders).
- Hub timeline for supporting teams: mentors/roles by Nov, fundraising packet and legal entity by Jan, standard route and school forms by Feb, insurance/car-rental advice by Mar, publicity and waivers by Apr, first-aid and bike training by May (2017, source: Spokes Hub Timeline).
- 2017 Stanford team started with 7 riders and only 4 reached DC; two left early including a concussion. Plan for attrition and injury contingencies (2017, source: Spokes 2017 Stanford; Injury Contingency Plans).
- A 2018 rider left because of a bad experience with another team member, and selection by the same committee every year was flagged as a risk: vet team dynamics at selection (2018, source: Meeting Notes 1_23_2018).
- Publicity: keep ONE official name/mailing list for all channels and log all accounts in one sheet; 2017 used SpokesAmerica.com, WordPress, Instagram @spokesamerica, Warmshowers team account, MIT Crowdfund (2017, source: Spokes Hub Publicity Outline; Social Media Passwords, credentials redacted).
- Roster of ~75 alumni across 2013-2019 teams with emails, many willing to mentor (2019, source: All Spokes Team Members).
