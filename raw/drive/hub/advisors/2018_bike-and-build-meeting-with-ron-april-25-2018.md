What, if any, is the legal liability to officers/members associated with non-profit? (Considering that Spokes involves cross-country bicycle trips with a non-zero risk of accidents etc)
The board wanted to make sure that they as individuals can’t be sued
It’s impossible to prevent someone from suing the board members or officers
People can twist the situation in some way that will end up pushing liability on the officers
Want to carry D&O - Directors and Officers Insurance. That blanked of coverage should cover the costs if an individual is named
In your waivers, make sure that you are naming any directors and officers explicitly. Have a legal eye look at all of this to make sure that you are
Make sure to pay attention to standard fiduciary responsibilities
If someone is operating within those responsibilities, then you are protecting yourself from being accused of behaving badly
A gray area - To what level of expertise is a board member offering expertise? For example, they have a lawyer on their board. That lawyer wanted to make sure they were not taking the role of legal council, so they required that a separate council was hired
Board members can end up taking on liability if they give explicit approval about legal or liability concerns. Essentially they can then be blamed for being wrong
“Scariest part of all this” - They have alums that help them plan their trips. These are “experts” that are on the board. They have to be very careful not to accidentally take on liability here.
When writing minutes from a board meeting you have to stay someone ambiguous to make sure you are not pigeonholing someone into a specific comment that could end up resulting in liability.
“Provided there is not legal repercussion on my personal assets, I would like to share this opinion”
How to deal with liability of supporting bicycle trips as an organization?
Make sure to repeat that people can not come back to the organization or the officers.
Liability coverage/insurance
More importantly - your commitment to safety has got to be second to none
Implementing policies becomes critical here.
Paint the picture here by saying “Biking is inherently dangerous. Worst case scenario you could die on this trip” - This kind of information is everywhere that they can put it.
Potential risk for officers - if there is a comment that says “hey we think we need two vans for support” and then you only have one, you have to have some sort of logical explanation as to why you made that decision.
Should we be purchasing liability insurance for individuals/the nonprofit organization?
Answered above - Yes.
Can you help us draft waivers for participants (students and cyclists)?
They have a waiver and will share it with us
There are a whole lot of pieces of information that we would like provide to our riders - what they might want to eat, routes they should take, safety, etc. Is it bad for us to make these recommendations
The concern is if you make a standard and then you don’t end up implementing it, that’s an issue
If it is recorded in the minutes, then you need to explain an action if you do or do not take it
Another sad thing we are running into - We require our riders to train for 500 miles. We have a higher percentage of people who show up in good shape. Their insurance agent is saying that they can’t require that because that training time isn’t covered by liability insurance
Can you talk a bit more about the insurance component
Sad, but this is primarily there to protect the organization and not the riders
Anything that comes up on the trip, you need to be covered
We require that everyone has health insurance benefits that are active until the end of the trip.
All insurance Policies:
We have an accident medical policy. Currently is $50K per occurrence of out of pocket expenses.
They recommend that each rider gets their own trip insurance to cover other costs
Have an add on to auto insurance that covers being hit by a car even if you are not in your car. This is critical if you are hit by an uninsured driver
Uninsured/underinsured
Organization liability insurance
They charge based on person-days on the road.
Property insurance that covers things like office
Auto insurance for vans, etc
Workers comp
Should we setup a corporation before we have fully determined all of these issues? What are the risks that we run if we do not setup a corporation?
One concern - if you don’t set up a corporate who are people donating to?
You all are in a dangerous position - there is going to be an accident eventually. At some point someone will respond by accusing all of us as wrong doing.
There is really no way to avoid that. Even if we suggest the idea to someone, that is a concern.
If we provide any advice at all we have to have an entity to protect us.
D&O insurance is critical here.
How have you minimize personal risk (reputation, liability, etc) to employees, board members, and officers of your org given that there is a risk of physical harm to participants?
Answered in question 1

Wrap up…
Would there be an opportunity to join with another trip? Join with Bike and Build?
They could be willing to integrate us into a trip or create a trip for us.
If we are doing this, then we are playing by their rules. That would include training, fundraising, etc
They grant out dollars to a lot of affordable housing agencies.
Let’s say they did one big trip from all our schools. Maybe there are educational components for the project as well. To maintain 501(c)3 status they have to line up with their goals of affordable housing or healthy community lifestyles.
Then we could be part of the grant pool and dictate where some of our money goes from a grant standpoint.
“There’s got to be a way where we can set it up where, yes, it has to fit with our stuff, but what are you trying to accomplish”
They would be looking for this partnership to drive their mission forward. Impacting lives, empowering young adults, developing communities
They avoid having too many close friends on one team because that dynamic can influence the organic growth of the group.
