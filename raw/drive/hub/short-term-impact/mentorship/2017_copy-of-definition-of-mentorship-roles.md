General mentorship role
What is a Spokes mentor? What is a mentor’s responsibilities?

One mentor per expertise (categories of expertise described below) will help and guide all mentees of existing Spokes groups (including MIT/Harvard, Stanford, etc.)
Mentees’ tasks and responsibilities in their respective teams fall under the mentor’s field of expertise
The mentor’s introduction to their group of mentees should involve an orientation, preferably via video chat if possible, of the general tasks to be completed, how/where to start, and any pieces of information that are necessary to know.
Mentors should check in (via email, text, etc.) weekly during the beginning of the planning process. Check-ins include making sure mentees are on track to complete necessary tasks in a reasonable time frame.
A mentor’s purpose is to provide information their mentees need to effectively complete their tasks.
Mentors will likely need to act in a guiding role more heavily at the very beginning of the process, but once mentees are comfortable in their responsibilities, the mentor is a resource for advice and information, not a supervisor.
Mentors will also help coordinate activity between teams (ex. Ensure different teams do not contact the same companies for funding)

Areas of mentor expertise
What information should mentors be familiar with in their field of expertise?

Financial logistics and fundraising
Financial logistics
How to obtain procurement card and travel card
How to gain access to Spokes MIT Account
How to budget money and decide how much to fundraise and spend
Restrictions on what teams can or cannot buy (i.e personal items like sunscreen)
How to track spending and send receipts to MIT
Fundraising
How to begin contacting corporate sponsors and personal donors
Where to begin contacting departments, alumni, companies in STEM, biking, etc. for funding
How to create an MIT Crowdfund page
Suggest potential perks for sponsors
This mentor should help coordinate finances and contacted companies between Spokes groups

Education and curriculum
How to reach out to and keep up communications with schools/educators
How to build up a solid curriculum
What works, what doesn’t work in a workshop
Resources for ways to practice teaching and receive feedback on curriculums
What to expect at a Learning Festival
How to run Learning Festivals
How to prepare for learning festivals on the road (preparation on the day of, etc.)
Keep in mind that the team may want to try different strategies with respect to Learning Festival format, preparation, etc. This mentor should let mentees know what worked best for them but should not frame their experience as the one “correct” approach.

Route and housing
Assist in making and using a Warmshowers account to contact hosts
Suggest other possible contacts such as churches and community centers
Forewarn mentees about general problems that may arise throughout the route and how to avoid them
Ex. Explain how to use Google Maps, Google Earth, and other resources to find where dirt roads and paths that should not be used are
Come up with a plan to deal with snafus faced during the ride (what to do if you hit a closed road, dead end, etc)
Advise mentees on how to navigate situations in which there is no housing plan for the night of (see point two)

Materials and bikes
Materials
Give a basic outline of the materials next year’s team will need and NOT need
Where to buy materials cheapest
Which materials to buy that will be most effective on the trip
Which materials are should be prioritized
Bikes
Teach the next team what bikes would be best for the trip and how to maintain
Where to buy bikes
Which tools to buy, how to use them, and which ones work with your bikes
How to maintain bikes daily
Where to learn how to fix a flat
Where to get bikes sized correctly

Social media and webmaster
Social Media
Inform the next team what to post on social media and when
Explaining “what to post” involves describing what content was most popular with followers (what most people like to hear about on social media pages)
Know which social media platforms people pay the most attention to
Offer advice on how to organize photos for ease of use for the rest of the team
Webmaster
Know how to maintain the website and upload pictures/blogs to the website for the rest of the team
Know how to upload biographies, route information, fundraising sponsors, etc. to website
