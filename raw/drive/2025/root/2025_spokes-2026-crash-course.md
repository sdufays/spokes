## Slide 6
STEP 2: ROUTE PLANNING
Look @ previous years routes: they have definitely narrowed down and made less changes over the years as safer routes have been discovered and dangerous parts have been avoided
Some years go SF -> DC (no headwinds) but that’s lame :( 
Try to have a general route done by end of January/IAP so everything else can be planned (housing, LFs, etc.)
Try to follow Bike Route 76, as well as the Katy Trail in Missouri (see pic for the abuse I took on the Katy trail 💔)
Avoid a certain trail going into Cedar City it was SCARY (Joseph can tell you more)
In general descending in dirt/gravel for long periods of time is not a good idea
Make sure your not accidentally being routed onto major highways
Plan in National Park stops! We hit the Big 5 in Utah (Zion, Bryce Canyon, Capitol Reef, Canyonlands, and Arches)
Things to take into account: SAFETY, types of terrain (for example gravel vs. busy highways), elevation, rest stops (we usually had them every 1-2 hours, or every 10-20 miles)
## Slide 7
STEP 3: LEARNING FESTIVALS 
As you are route planning, reach out to schools, libraries, churches, educational amps, etc. ESPECIALLY ones we have previous connections with
Start reaching out end of January after the general route is made
A solid number of learning festivals is around 10
Do the learning festival at the correctional facility in D.C. as well as at the Fallon Youth Center in Nevada
We had a few reach out from Missouri and Indiana asking for learning festivals, look into that and decide if it’s worth it (in the Spokes 2026 advice document)
Some kids are gonna be crazy you just gotta go with the flow sometimes 😭😭
We split into 5 groups, ran 4 lesson plans each festival so the 5th group could act as “floaters”
## Slide 8
STEP 4: HOUSING
Housing Options…
Warmshowers: the BEST ppl ever, they are bike-tourists who open up their homes to people on tour when they are home, usually also give you food and have the best stories, maximize these
Churches: nice people, def a good option especially in more deserted/less-populated areas
Learning Festivals: they will sometimes give you a place to stay!
Camping: chill, fun, especially in forests and other nice areas
Spokes 2024 did a bit of this on the way, but we had 99% of our housing planned before we started and I think it made our lives 1000x easier, the only challenge was Northern NV…
## Slide 9
STEP 5: BIKES!!! 
IDK if this was ever clarified but Spokes will buy your bike, and you get to KEEP it after 😍😍
Spokes 2024 had a few different bikes, but 2025 all got the same and it made repairs a lot easier plus we all looked dope AF
We got our bikes from Trek, who gave a group discount + a free Level 1 repair within a year of purchase if you want to tune it up and use it back at school the next year
GET GRAVEL BIKES bc there WILL be terrains other than road. TRUST US ON THIS PLZ.
We also got tubeless tires and got a LOT less flats than 2024, but they started being weird in Colorado (this may have been slightly our fault tho (if you get tubeless, check for micro leaks once you get hands on the bikes and complain to whoever you got them from))
Try and buy your bikes around Spring Break so you can get used to riding them, get them fitted (if you want), etc.
Play around with different seat heights, saddle and handlebar positions, etc. otherwise you might be in pain :(
Also practice w/ clip-on shoes!
Also if you can learn bike repair it saves time AND money

## Slide 15
OTHER GENERAL SPOKES TIPS:
We naturally split into groups of 2-4 people, sometimes we rode all together, sometimes we rode alone, depends on the mood, weather conditions, etc.
Ways to stay entertained: music, podcasts, audiobooks, shows, games, calling people if it’s quiet enough and you have service, the lovely scenery 😍
Highly recommend getting “bone-conducting” earbuds: allow you to listen to stuff but still be aware of what’s going on around you
There will be areas lacking cell service: download your routes beforehand (sometimes for multiple days)(bike computers are your friend!), set your meeting points with other bikers/the driver in advance, make a plan of what to do if someone doesn’t show up by a certain time
Make a plan of what to do in dangerous weather (CHECK THIS AHEAD OF TIME AND AT EACH REST STOP): some general good tips are to get down (and get cover if you can), get away from the road if you can, and stick together with someone
We had a rule that if the van deemed a situation dangerous and came back to get you, you HAD to get in
There will be nice cars 😄 and there will be not nice cars 😔
