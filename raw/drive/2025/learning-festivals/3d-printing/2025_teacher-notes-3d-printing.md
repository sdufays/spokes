**Intro (~7 min):** introduce Spokes (8 MIT students biking DC to SF to inspire STEM interest); show the printer printing; icebreakers (coolest thing you made? what would you 3D print?); motivation: cheap printers ($200 FDM; Bambu A1 mini ~$250), uses from hobby (parts, tools, figurines, toys, costumes) to research (art, prosthetics, houses, food).

**How printers work (~8 min):** idea -> modeling (CAD = computer-aided design) -> slicing (printer-specific software, thin layers; look at a "benchy" for layer smoothness) -> printing (FDM = fused deposition modeling: extrude thermoplastic through ~0.5 mm nozzle; Bambu A1 mini with AMS Lite for multicolor; PLA filament; ~50 micron precision) -> finishing (remove supports, sand). Other types: SLA (resin, light-cured), SLS (laser-sintered powder, no supports, stronger, rougher).

**Additive manufacturing (~5 min):** additive vs subtractive; design constraints: overhangs, angles, bridges, wall thickness, print volume, surface finish, infill, supports.

**Activity (~35 min):** split students who want to try 3D printing pens (filament pens) vs design something to be printed (TinkerCAD).

**Conclusion (~5 min).**
Lesson learned (from surveys): kids loved this most but some did not get to print anything; print designs while the next workshop runs.
