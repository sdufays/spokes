The Spokes Master Guide

How to Use the Master Guides
We created some master guides to help with planning your trip! The master guide includes knowledge compiled from multiple years of Spokes. We hope that it will be useful for planning your trip. Once your trip is over, please update the Master Guides to include knowledge from your year and delete outdated information so you can pass it down to the next generation of Spokes. This Doc includes tips for different topics and links to other documents for topics that require more details. See table of contents for different topics

Food and Nutrition

Eating the right food for fueling yourself and for recovery is important. Eat carbs in the morning and while you are biking. Make sure to eat enough protein for dinner / later in the day to help with recovery. Drink electrolytes while biking to prevent cramping and replenish electrolytes. Below is a list of snacks, breakfast ideas, lunch ideas, dinner ideas, electrolytes, dinner ideas, and a list of protein sources we recommend.
Breakfast
Usually our hosts would provide breakfast for us. When we’re camping, we have to make stuff for ourselves. Here are some of our good things to do for breakfast that we “mostly” recommend.
Bagels and cream cheese: Fast and easy! Get some smoked salmon if you’re feeling fancy
Also PB and nutella for protein, fat, and carbs
Yogurt + Granola: Yum
Overnight oats are good. Once we made overnight oats in ziplock bags and ate them in the car on the way to a learning festival. Yum. Don’t even need a fridge
Apples and PB: Yum
Protein pancakes: Personally not a huge fan because it requires setting up the stove, cooking each pancake one at a time, and cleaning a lot more stuff. Takes too long. But also I’m not a big pancake fan.
Spokes 24 classic: powdered milk + instant coffee + cereal + powdered pb (optional)
Protein powder/powdered PB + powdered milk
Cottage cheese if you get sick of sugary breakfast items
Lunch
Spokes 22: For lunch we tried to include a mix of protein and carbs. We try to make these meals easy and fast. Often times we just use paper towels as plates
Sardines and tortillas
Rotisserie chicken with Hawaiian bread
Deli meat sandwiches
Hummus with veggies
Apples and PB
Spokes 24: We had bagel sandwiches for approximately 74 days in a row. Towards the end of the trip, we also discovered the majesty of French bread, which is super cheap from Walmart and tastes better. Our sandwich supplies were usually:
Deli meat (+ tofurkey meat)
bagels/bread
Hummus (very important)
lettuce/tomato (the water from tomato feels divine on a hot day)
Cream cheese
Mayo
Sliced cheeses
pickles/jalapeños
Potato chips (for crunch)
Spokes 26: We found the Venn diagram of snack that most people on the team liked and spammed those throughout the whole summer. In parts of the summer it meant sandwiches like past teams, sometimes we did a liquid diet, sometimes a bunch of fruit and snacks
Snacks
Always bring snacks with you while biking! Good to prevent bonking (running out of energy while biking).

Dried dates: Costco sells huge bags of dates for really cheap! Dates carried the 2022 team. Cheaper than buying a ton of Larabars
Dried fruits: I know dried dates are also dried fruits but they are so good they require their own section. Dried mango, figs, and apricots are all good too.
Bars: Our favorites are Clif bars and Lara bars. Some chocolate ones melt so might want to stay away from them
The best snacks for Spokes 24 were the nature’s bakery chocolate brownie bar. Holy crap. heavenly
Nuts: Go nuts
Trail mix really hits the spot but will melt super quickly and you have to eat it from the bag like a grazing cow
Electrolytes! They are real and important! We like Nunn, Liquid IV, Propel, and Gatorade.
Buying a tub of Gatorade powder is by far the cheapest, would recommend. Still good to have packets that people can take with them on the bike
Random shit from gas stations…… MEHHH. Sometimes we get snacks from gas stations when we stop there for water stops. Personally not a huge fan of their selection but others like it.
Dinner
Spokes 2026 did not cook a single time. We called places and asked for free meals if our host could not feed us. If you do want to cook, here are some meals that you can make using just 2 pots that are easy to make and packed with protein.
Jambalaya: One pot rice dish with tomatoes, red beans, sausage, and meat. Good for camping days
Feijoada: Easy black bean stew with pork. Good for camping days
Chicken Salad: Made with canned chicken, or canned tuna. Good for hotel stays where we don’t have access to stove
Tacos: Easy dinner, can use beans, chicken, and other meats as a filling.
Chicken and Rice: Easy. Pan fry some chicken and finish it off in the rice. Throw whatever seasonings you want. Replace chicken with any meat you’d like. Here are some ideas.
Spam, pineapple “fried rice”
Bell peppers
Onions
GARLIC
Craisons and nuts
Lemon
Spring Rolls: Can buy shrimp and tofu and just season them, and then make a sauce and cut up some veggies. No stove! Woop!
Pasta: Find a good one pot pasta recipe and then make some meat/tofu on the side. Easy money
Protein Sources
Here are the protein sources we (2022) used the most on our trip
Sardines (Canned) Great for lunch! The canned variety from Costco was very good. We did eventually get tired of it after eating sardines everyday for lunch
Textured Vegetable Protein (Shelf Stable): This can be found at Whole Foods. Pure protein granules that can be added to dinner foods for extra protein. Kinda like sofritas or ground beef aesthetic. Takes 5 mins to cook and can be bought at whole foods.
Tofu (Can Be Shelf Stable) The shelf stable variety is great!
Canned Chicken (Canned): Good to add to so many meals
Unflavored protein powder (Shelf Stable): Good protein source you can find in whole foods. Good to add to savory foods as a thickener. Can also be used to make a protein shake or in sweet dishes
24 had chocolate powder which was great!
Fresh Meats: Fresh meat is just so much better than the canned stuff, but requires constant refrigeration. Good for dinner
Rotisserie Chicken: Great for a yummy and fast lunch. Goes well with Hawaiian rolls. Good for dinner too.
Deli Meats: Good for a lazy lunch
Jerky (Shelf Stable Until Opened): Costco has them! They are great. Expensive though
Soymilk (Shelf Stable)
Peanut Butter (Shelf Stable)
Tricks of the Trade
Use Minute Rice! It makes cooking rice dishes so much faster and easier
Costco is your friend
Walmart is also your friend. You will miss it when you get to more remote areas
Baby wipes are REALLY useful for cleaning your hands. Also, we prewipe our dishes with them and clean them with soap later.
Paper towels as plates for fast cleanup
A pocket stove is good if you need to cook something small but don't have to pull out the behemoth of a camping stove we have. Spokes 2022 used it for boiled eggs! We could cook up to 9 eggs in them at a time!
The driver picking up some cold soft drinks/iced tea can be life saving on a hot day. Ice becomes a luxury
Packing Guide
For Spokes, it's important to pack light because there will not be a lot of room in the car. We were able to do laundry pretty much everyday or every other day. That means that you don’t really need that many pairs of clothes. I would recommend having 2 or 3 sets of biking outfits (No more than 3), and 2 sets of non-biking outfits (including the Spokes T-shirt). Also, bring some clothes for cold weather (Temperature can get down to the 50s in the Rockies) Get easy to dry clothes like synthetic or wool. Faster drying means less mildew. People often brought backpacking bags, but I’ve found that having a small carry-on suitcase works better because things will be easier to access and the bag will be easier to stack inside the car.

Cold weather clothes
1x thermal leggings
1x long pants

Crocs + Shoes (Prefer to keep these inside/on my bag so they are not loose and all over the place)

Portable bike pump + Bike tools

Air horn and mace (dog protection)
