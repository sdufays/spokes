Spokes Route Planning and Housing Master Guide

Planning the Route
For many years, MIT Spokes has started in Washington, DC and headed west to San Francisco. We would recommend taking a similar route because there is already a lot of documentation and planning done for this route. Much easier to stick with this than come up with a new route from scratch. We have also built strong connections with some of the learning festivals and hosts on this route and it would be good to continue building on those relations. The route that we made follows US Bike Route 76, aka the Transamerican Bike Route From DC all the way to Colorado. Adventure Cycling website contains information about the US Bike Routes! Link below
USBRS Maps and Route Resources | Adventure Cycling Association

We chose to bike through a lot of national parks and visit some cities that were slightly off route- this made our mileage higher than previous years. It’s up to you as a team to decide which reroutes/detours you want to take to see the sights you want to see.

Important note:

When planning the route, we recommend having the Warm Showers map of hosts, Google maps, and USBRS maps all open at the same time. This will save a lot of time. We made the mistake of planning our route before checking whether there were Warm Showers hosts in the towns that we were staying in, and had to make several changes due to lack of hosts in some areas.

Also, we found that having at least 2 people (we had 3) to plan the route was a good number (at least one person to contact warm showers and one person to book campsites).
Day to Day Navigation
The route usually includes any rest stops, water stops, meet ups with the car, lunch stops, and etc. We found that 1.5 hrs to 2 hrs (around 15 mi) between stops so we can refill our water works well. Water refills can usually be done at gas stations. Gas stations have soda fountains that we can use to fill up our bottles with nice cold water, buy any snacks, and use the bathroom. We would meet with the car if there were no gas stations on the route.

In 2026, we had all of the route generally preplanned before summer, but the route team would adjust the night before and see if anything needed to be changed or if they could find better rest stops. It’s very useful to look at previous years blogs for any notes on the next day’s route, and for anything they’d recommend to be changed.

One useful tool we have used for planning the day to day route is called Ride With GPS. Ride With GPS is a free app / website that is designed for route planning for cyclists. Another option is to have one person get Strava premium because it helps you see the types of road (paved, unpaved) that you might be biking through. Google Maps street view is also helpful for examining road types and the size of the shoulder (for highways). Once we plan a route using Ride With GPS, we create a GPX file that you can then upload to your bike computer (which you should try to charge every few days). In a pinch, Google Maps is good to navigate over unforeseen reroutes. Make sure you download offline maps (especially because sometimes you will be biking in areas without service).

For Spokes 2024 route, take a look at Hank, Varsha, and Jess’ strava pages for the daily routes.

Finding Stays
For Spokes 2022, our stays were found through Warm Showers, Campsites, Learning Festivals, friends, MIT connections, churches, and motels/hotels (ranked in order of frequency). Definitely use warmshowers to find hosts. Most hosts will let you use the showers + laundry, cook dinner + breakfast, and give you a place to sleep! A lot of times learning festivals are more than happy to help find places to stay so definitely ask them! In 2022, we stayed with many of Sophia’s church members. Reaching out through the MIT alumni association is also helpful. We got a learning festival and many stays through one of the alumni connections (Sam Ford in Kentucky).

Warm Showers: Make a new account. Include that you will have a support car in the account profile. Start contacting people in February. Since we are a large group to host, try to show flexibility and appreciation when messaging people (i.e. we can sleep on the floor). Only contact hosts who indicate that they can take 2 or more people (unless you are really desperate and there is no one else who can host you, it’s worth a shot just to see). Once you’ve confirmed a host, ask for their phone number and address since the app can be buggy, and you want to make sure that the driver has a guaranteed way to contact the host by the day you arrive. Try to get the WS address as early as possible so that the person in charge of route planning has it the night before.

For 26, we reached out to those who said they could host 4 or more people, this usually means we stayed in surprisingly nice houses as they didn’t have to squish us onto their living room floor to accommodate, they instead had guest rooms in their basement for example.

Make sure to reach out to confirm housing a week or so before arriving, and then the driver will the day of to coordinate specifics.

Campsites: Start booking in February since national parks can fill up fast. Make sure there are showers if possible.

Learning Festivals: Coordinate with the person who is contacting learning festivals to ask if a learning festival can provide a place (especially if there are no warm showers in that area)

MIT connections: We emailed all of the MIT alumni clubs of the states we were passing through, but only 3 responded. Still totally worth it since one donated and we also met Sam Ford of Kentucky this way.

Churches: Generally great place to stay because there’s a large kitchen space, lots of bathrooms, and sometimes will also give you snacks/feed you. We would look for churches to contact if there were no confirmed warm showers hosts in the area. Call and email every church since no response most of the time.

Kentucky
Overview
We found that Kentucky had a lot of great hosts and an amazing sense of hospitality! A lot of our stays were coordinated with Sam Ford who we connected with through the MIT Alumni network. Sam Ford also helped organize a learning festival for us in Bowling Green, KY. Kentuky has a history of coal mining and a lot of the towns are relatively poor. A lot of coal mines have shut down so many people in Kentucky are struggling to find work. One thing to watch out for in Kentucky is the dogs. The dogs here are notorious for chasing cyclists. The first day in Kentucky, one of the bikers from Spokes 2022 got into an accident while being chased by a dog. We would recommend having air horns on you when biking to scare off any dogs.

Utah
Overview
Utah has so many national parks! Although I wouldn’t recommend biking down into Arches National Park like Spokes 2022 did because the road that leads into Arches from Thompson Springs has very little shoulder and high traffic. We had to shuttle that day because it was too dangerous. Definitely visit the national parks! The bike from Bryce Canyon to Cedar City was one of the most beautiful rides. Mostly downhill through some gorgeous gorges and rivers.
Nevada
Overview
A large part of Nevada is desert and there are parts of Nevada that don’t have many services for hundreds of miles. The route from Baker, NV to Carson City, NV is named the Loneliest Road in America because of this reason. Be prepared to stock up on food, snacks, and water. Ely, NV is a good place to stock up on supplies. The section from Eureka to Austin to Fallon is especially void of any services. On the loneliest road, you can collect stamps in a booklet that can be later traded in for a pin! You can get this stamp at most gas stations. Middlegate, NV has a famous burger stay that is worth stopping by. See if you can spot messages left by previous years of Spokes!

California
Overview
Definitely recommend staying at Lake Tahoe for a day - the water was lovely and the camping was nice. The only thing is that we couldn’t find a welcome to California sign when traveling to our campsite in South Lake Tahoe. When biking from Lake Tahoe to Sacramento, highway 50 quickly becomes a freeway with angry cars and signs that say no cyclists. Plan to get off the highway and take side roads instead. Also note that a lot of gas stations in the area north of San Francisco (before getting to Sausalito) didn’t have water or bathrooms, so go to a grocery store like Whole Foods instead of hopping from one gas station to the next.
