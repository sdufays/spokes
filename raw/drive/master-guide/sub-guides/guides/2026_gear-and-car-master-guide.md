# Spokes Gear and Car Master Guide

## Choosing and Buying Bikes
Trek Cambridge gives 20% on all gear bought through them: use for big purchases. Cambridge Bicycle sells at wholesale but has a smaller selection: use for small purchases.

Recommendations:
- Go **tubeless**: fewer flats, simpler repairs than inner tubes.
- Everyone gets the **same type of bike** (swap parts for temporary fixes).
- Must be **gravel bikes**; disc brakes; drop handlebars; tires **35 mm or wider**.
- Shop around for better prices.
- Bike shops: recommend Cambridge Trek in Alewife (negotiate 20% discount); recommend Landry's (gave a discount on gear, not bikes); "Do not go to Cambridge Bicycle. Overpriced." (note: the guide also says to use Cambridge Bicycle for small wholesale purchases; 2026 expense log shows both used.)

**Note from Spokes 26:** bought Trek Checkpoint ALR3 gen3 bikes for everyone, then bike fits with Brendan Connor at CycleLifePT. Reasons: 1x10 gearing removes the troublesome front derailleur and has a massive rear gear for steep climbs (huge help in Appalachia); wide tire clearance is crucial (a significant minority of time is off-road); Trek was good to work with and the team squeezed several hundred dollars of warranty work out of them during the trip.

## Bike Gear
Previous team left bike gear, camping supplies and LF supplies stored in **Milkdrop N52**. Previous team can show you where; coordinate with Edgerton about storage.

Things to consider buying with the bike (keep on you while biking):
- **Water bottles and cages (must).** Get **metal** bottles: 2026 discovered (super el nino) that plastic doesn't keep water cool; ice savings offset the cost of at least one metal bottle per person. 2026 got the Camelbak Podium metal (tall). Plastic ones break. See how many cages fit; one past rider ran 3 cages through the Utah/Nevada deserts. Smaller frames: some bought Camelbaks (~60 oz).
- Frame bags and saddle bags (snacks = constant carbs to avoid bonking).
- Small first aid kit; small Swiss army knife (optional); spare tubes matching tire size; bike multitool with all Allen keys; small frame-mounted pump; portable charger (strongly recommended; useful when camping without electricity).

### Must Have Gear While Biking
- Rear and front lights
- Clip-in shoes & pedals (more efficient)
- Helmet
- First aid kit (at least one rider carries one)
- Allen keys (portable set)
- Water bottles and cages (Spokes 24 started with 48 oz; upgraded to 72 oz by Utah due to heat/fewer towns)
- Bike pump: floor pump with PSI gauge + portable frame-mounted pump
- Snacks
- Upgraded seat: 2022 and 2019 got the Terry Liberator; 2024 and 2026 did not upgrade and were fine
- Sunglasses; biking gloves; padded bike shorts
- Tire repair kit: spare tubes, tire levers, patch kit if using tubes; **tire plugs** if tubeless

### Nice to Have
- Sun legs and sun sleeves (alternative to sunscreen all day, better protection)
- Chamois butter (used less than expected)
- Fenders: 2026 did not use them; one rider got dried mud in his bibs after a muddy gravel day; judged not worth the weight (personal preference)
- Handlebar bag for snacks (2022 converted fanny packs into handlebar bags; better than a backpack)
- Battery pack
- Open-ear headphones (e.g. Shokz bone conduction); useful in Kansas/eastern Colorado for audiobooks/language practice (2026, Drew)

### Gear to Keep in the Car
Chain cleaning tools (rag, degreaser, lubricant, chain brush); Allen keys; floor pump; extra sealant (tubeless); extra tires and inner tubes; bike rack (make sure car and rack are compatible); bike locks.

## Bike Repair and Maintenance
Clean and lube bikes about once a week. Before leaving Boston, as many members as possible should know at minimum: fix flats, tune derailleur, adjust brakes, clean and lube chain.

## Car Rental
### Type of Car + Rental Company
- MIT has a discount with **Budget and Avis**; rent through them. Talk to Sandi (slipnosk@mit.edu) and Chris (mayerc@mit.edu); in 2024 Chris was main contact. Reserve as soon as you know the Boston departure date. Recommend a **minivan**. Preferred Budget location: Central Square, 350 Mass Ave.
- 2022 could not get a minivan, used a Ford Explorer SUV (only 6 people; liked it). 2022 bike rack: Saris Bones EX. 2023 and 2024 used a 2022 Chrysler Pacifica; **2024 does not recommend its bike rack (bent and fell near end of trip).**
- **Avis/Budget is described as a bad rental company** but it is the only MIT-agreement company that covers drivers under 21 (check whether this changed). If problems: talk to Edgerton and Jessica Tam (Office of the VP for Finance; https://vpf.mit.edu/jessica-tam), who helped get a discount after the 2024 troubles. Details of the 2024 Avis saga: "Spokes 2024 Car Troubles" (saved in linked/).
- 2023, 2024 and 2026 also rented a **truck/cargo van for the first few days** to carry bikes, supplies and people to DC (booked through Edgerton). 2024: 2024 Toyota Tacoma (3 bikes in the bed; very large); can't guarantee make/model. Returned at Washington Dulles (~50 min from DC, close to Oakton where 2024 started). 2026: cargo van.

### Car Size
With luggage, LF supplies, gear and food, only driver and passenger seats were usable. A minivan has 7 seats vs 8 people. SUV: 8 seats and more packing space but costlier; driver comfort matters; some drivers are inexperienced, so SUV raises incident risk (minivan team had one small accident, no injuries, and hit a pole in an underground lot). Sandi recommended against an SUV. Don't go smaller than a minivan. Stanford Spokes 2024 used an 11-seater van and said it wasn't bad to drive (https://stanfordspokes.squarespace.com/).

### One-way vs Two-way
2023 and 2024 had one-way rentals dropped at SFO: a few thousand dollars more. They then had to ship gear and bikes back to Boston: a 2-day hassle and really expensive; "might not have been the best choice"; travel for East-Coast-bound members was covered anyway. Past teams also did 2-way, with a few East Coast members driving back with bikes/gear (a rest/adventure after arriving in SF). Members not going east still need to get bikes home (possibly check bikes on planes).

### Background Checks and Acquiring the Car
- Contact Edgerton early to start **background checks** for every student driver (emailed forms, quick; nudge people). Cannot drive the MIT rental without one.
- Edgerton books the car and pays from the account (not the travel card). Travel card may be used to book a car in an emergency. MIT discount/insurance info: https://insurance.mit.edu/services/insurance-coverage-claims/automobile/rentals/
- **Roadside assistance trap:** have **4 drivers go to an Avis/Budget service desk** to be added to the rental (renter + 4 = 5 max) so they can request roadside assistance. Non-added drivers may still drive with a background check. Once Avis refused roadside help because the named renter was biking. Check with Edgerton if the policy changed; do this in Boston or at Dulles before the trip.
- At pickup: ask when it was last serviced, do a full walk-around, photo all scratches, check engine and tires, and look up recalls for that make/model.

## Car Troubles
Spokes 19, 23 and 24 all had car trouble (23 and 24 concentrated in week 1).
- Rental cars come without tools such as a tire-change kit.
- 2026 notes: don't speed in Vail, Colorado; had to rip off a dragging oil-pan cover in Kansas.
- Call the 24-hour roadside number on the keys.
- If none of the 5 eligible people is with the car, call Edgerton; 2024 eventually got a tow authorized with their help (Avis asks for ID). Talk to Edgerton before departure about easing this.
- When repairs are needed Avis just swaps the whole car (annoying with a loaded car and far-off locations).

### Accidents or Damage
Call Edgerton (Chris in 2026). MIT has no physical insurance card: don't give personal insurance; show the rental agreement and explain it is a company car insured through MIT (Sandi explained to the other driver once). Get written proof of insurance from MIT for the glove box if possible. Fill out the Avis/Budget accident form and send to MIT ASAP. "You are insured."

## Day2Day Car
The car will get gross: buy a small hand vacuum and air fresheners (2026: it isn't that bad if proactive). Buy **hard-sided plastic boxes** (Walmart, cheap) instead of "Tetris on impossible mode". One tire often deflated: everyone should know how to add air and basic car maintenance. The overloaded car rides low and can get stuck on bumpy unmaintained roads (2024 did: https://www.mitspokes.com/post/day-52-ah-shit-here-we-go-again); fixed by unloading until it lifted. Be a minimalist and keep food, water, first aid, pump and spare tubes accessible. (The original has a photo of how the car was packed; not captured.)

2026 note: Sandi "will be on your ass all the time"; take what she says with a grain of salt; a little respectful pushback is sometimes necessary.
