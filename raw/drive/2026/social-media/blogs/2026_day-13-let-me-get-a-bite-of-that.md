Breaks Interstate Park, VA to Hazard, KY: bike 1.4 mi (planned 85.1 mi, 4,749 ft). Author: Carmen (first driver x blogger day). The longer rider/driver drafts are merged here.

Morning: bare-bones campground (no electricity or drinking water) so devices were dead; Garmins would not connect. The day before the team had biked through a sketchy neighborhood and dragged bikes under potentially live electric fencing.

The bite (~8:45am): first group (Drew leading, Nunu, Caroline, Nate at rear) descended out of Breaks Park onto a back road. A big white fluffy dog ("Max") in a driveway charged Nunu then turned on Caroline, who had just unclipped to try to kick it; Nate yelled "NO" and scared it off. Caroline kept biking to a house a block away. No cell service; they walked back up the hill. A car bumped the dog and allowed them to escape. Owner ("Scotty") blamed them for being stopped in the road; Drew confronted him, then rode up a 400 ft climb into Breaks Park to get service and call police.

Response timeline:
- Nunu gave first aid; walkie talkies (Nunu had been charging them) used because of no reliable service.
- Police took about 1 hour; Haysi volunteer rescue team arrived ~40 min after call (older volunteers, slow paperwork; spelled H-U-A-N-G; rural healthcare observations). Sheriff arrived with two more cars; jokingly said "Let me get a bite of that" when photographing the wound. County confusion: road in Dickenson County, house in Buchanan County.
- Rescue advised she might need tetanus (last shot over 5 years ago) and said the dog needs to be sampled for rabies. Caroline went by free volunteer ambulance with Aarushi to Pikeville Medical Center (35 min drive turned into 55 min after waiting for release); she received six rabies shots around the wound. Hospital staff recommended a fried chicken place.
- The sheriff said this dog had bitten a cyclist before, no charges. Drew's photo of the owner's license plate let the police identify him. Animal control showed up.
- Aarushi called Charles, coordinator at the Challenger Learning Center in Hazard KY; he brought a cargo van and drove about 2 hours to pick up the team and bikes. Shuttling 5 people plus 7 bikes with the minivan took an hour for a 2 mile trip. Team waited at a diner. Slept at a bed and breakfast in Hazard.
- Evening team meeting on the "Kentucky problem": those who want to bike bike; those who want to drive drive.

Lessons: the team had pre-bought 7 cans of Halt dog spray but never agreed on a plan (half thought it impractical; spray needs both hands). Decided to clip Halt to jerseys' front and spray any large dog chasing. Dogs on rural KY roads can be seriously aggressive; agree a dog protocol before Appalachia; no service means walkie talkies matter; know what you would do in a medical emergency (hospital vs ambulance, free volunteer ambulance); photograph plates; keep tetanus shots current.
