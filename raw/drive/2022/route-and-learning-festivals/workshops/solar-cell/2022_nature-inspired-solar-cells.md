## Slide 42
3. Use a fork to crush 3-4 defrosted berries in a petri dish.  

4. Add about a tablespoon (15mL) of distilled water to the crushed berries and stir.

5. Place the titanium dioxide slide face down into berry juice.
## Slide 43

6. Let the slide soak for 10 minutes in the berry juice. The film should be stained bright purple/pink. 

7. Lift the slide out of the juice using fork. Rinse the slide first with distilled water, then one small squirt of isopropyl alcohol (IPA) over a paper towel. Gently blot the slide dry with a tissue.

## Slide 44

8. Identify the clean 3mm strip on each slide. These strips will be connected to alligator clips to test your cell. Orient the strips so that they are on opposite sides as shown.

9. Place the carbon-coated slide on the table face up. Place the berry-stained slide on top of it so that the coated areas match and are touching with a 3 mm strip on each side.

10. Pick up the slides in this orientation, careful not to let them move. Clip the edges with binder clips to hold the cell together.

## Slide 45

11. Now with an eyedropper, add 1-­2 drops of Iodide/Iodine electrolyte solution to the crease between the two slides. The solution will be drawn into the cell by capillary action and will stain the entire inside of the slides. Gently open and close one of the binder clips to distribute.
16. Make sure that all of the berry-stained area is wetted.
12. Wipe off excess electrolyte solution with a small amount of IPA on a tissue. Make sure there is no electrolyte solution on the 3mm strips where the alligator clips will attach. *Instructors will super-glue the creases to prevent leaking.

## Slide 46
13. Attach the alligator clips to the two overhanging edges of the slide and attach the clip leads to your multimeter.

14. Turn the multimeter to measure voltage (V) and note this value with and without the light. The maximum voltage in direct sunlight should be about 0.1 – 0.5 volts. 

If the numbers changed, you successfully made a working solar cell!

Cost: lower than Si

Other: flexible, light-weight, thin, works in diffuse light and cooler conditions, electrolyte can evaporate
Commercial Silicon Solar Cell

Cost: higher than DSSC 
Efficiency: 21%
Other: lots of Si (sand!), stable over time
