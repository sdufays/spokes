Bike Safety Information
Compile Important facts and info about biking in general and specifically in Cambridge, MA
Finding the Right Bike
Types of Bikes:
Commuter Bike: upright bike, made for comfort, not for speed or long distance
Road Bikes: Body is more parallel to round, lighter weight, drop bar, more efficient for longer distance, thinner tires
Mountain Bikes: Thicker tires, has shocks, On road and off road, 
Folding BIkes: Easy to move around on the T, can put on green line. 
E-Bikes: any of the above bikes can be modified for electric assist
Cargo Bikes and trailers: useful for hauling groceries and kitty litter.
Blue Bikes: Shared bikes, Income eligible memberships, MIT also has discounted memberships. 

Equipment
Essentials:
Helmet: There is a right way to wear a helmet. Helmet is where the brian is. Eyes should see helmet ring. Ears are inch above buckle. Buckled should be tight enough such that 2 fingers fit below the strap (pretend to eat an apple and should feel the helmet) Every helmet brand has different shape and size. Helmet has a shelf life of 5 years and is one time use only. Under 16 is legally required.
Light: Front light is white, red rear light. Lights are important for others to see you. Legally required for biking at night. 
Massachusetts law requires bicyclists to use a white front light from 30 minutes after sunset to 30 minutes before sunrise.
The law also requires a red rear light or reflector and reflectors on the pedals or reflective material on the rider’s ankles. Lights

Traffic Laws:
Bike = vehicle
Stop at stop lights and stop signs
Yield to pedestrians on roads and paths
Ride on right side of the rode, dont go wrong way down one-ways
Hold your line (ride straight)
Stay 3 to 4 feet from parked cars to avoid doored
Avoid right hook: watch for cars turning right, don’t be parallel to the turning vehicle, 
Use hand signals
Multi-modal
Most MBTA buses have bike racks (but only 2 spaces)
Bikes are allows on subways and commuter rail except the green line. Bikes not allowed during commuter hours (7-10 am and 4-7 pm)
Long Distance Biking
Get an adventure bike - front racks, rear racks to hold camping bags, especially rack for water bottle OR get a little trailer
Make sure your bike wheels have enough spokes if you’re carrying weight on the bike
Want to avoid weight on back
All gear for camping/hiking (tent, sleeping pad) translates to bike camping trips but you want mostly lightweight gear to start off
You can feel vulnerable after 6-8 hrs of biking so it’s important to make a little home base for yourself
Bugs - lighting fire (smoke keeps them away, pack full of leaves to create more smoke), TIGER BALM is gold, end your day 2-3 hrs before sundown, mental game (don’t scratch bug bite - it becomes a mantra), bug nets can help
