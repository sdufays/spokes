**Sustainable Approaches and Materials to Manufacturing** - 2 days x ~2.5 hours. Authors: Sophia Wang and JD Hagood.

**Description:** additive manufacturing as route to sustainable manufacturing; environmental impact of plastic vs biologically derived options (recycled wood, oyster shells); hands-on 3D printing with biodegradable filaments; research of open-source biocomposite repositories.
**Learning goals:** environmental impact of conventional vs sustainable materials; infill, supports, slicing; CAD and importing to printer; analyzing sustainable material properties.

## Schedule
**Day 1 - Module 1 Intro to Additive Manufacturing:** 9:30 intros (15); 9:45 discussion what is 3D printing / additive vs subtractive (10); 9:55 lesson slicing and supports (15); 10:10 3D-pen tallest-structure challenge (15); 10:25 infill research group work (15); 10:40 break. **Module 2 Sustainable Materials** (Patritsia Stathatou open-source slides, fab.cba.mit.edu/classes/863.23): 10:55 why care (15); 11:10 biodegradable materials, molding/casting, CNC (15); 11:25 Wood PLA research (15); 11:40 DIY materials (15); 11:55 clean-up.
**Day 2 - Module 3 CAD:** 9:30 recap; 9:45 Onshape navigation (5); 9:50 intro to sketching (15); 10:05 additive design challenge (25); 10:25 importing to printer (15); break. **Module 4 Frontier of New Materials:** large-format printers (15); material applications (15); Materiom overview (15); Materiom research/presentation (15); clean-up.

## Lesson guide
**Day 1 materials:** 3D printing pens, infill samples, Wood PLA samples, laptops.
- *Discussion:* ask what opposite of additive is; show Bambu printing and Hurco 5-axis timelapses. Additive: slow, saves material; subtractive: fast at scale, wastes material. 3D printing's power is rapid prototyping, not mass production.
- *Slicing/supports:* 4 steps - CAD (show Thingiverse), STL, slicing (horizontal slices -> machine code), printing. Supports needed for >45 deg overhangs; show support-free printing (in-liquid printing, instant ceramic polymerization).
- *3D pen challenge:* tallest free-standing structure with a >45 deg overhang (3Doodler guide learn.the3doodler.com). Warn: nozzle tip very hot. Tip: 2D->3D works best (draw rectangular frames then glue together). Measure heights, discuss supports used.
- *Infill:* 5 infill patterns and 3 densities as samples; even 10-20% infill is strong; honeycomb/bees/wasp nests analogy. Groups pick a pattern, research why, 1-minute share.
- *Why sustainable materials:* list most impactful materials; plastic age; show ocean pollution and CO2 figures.
- *Biodegradable materials:* PLA (corn starch/sugarcane, biodegradable), wood PLA (PLA + wood fibers), PHA (biopolymer from plant oils/sugar, fully biodegradable), PETG (not biodegradable but recyclable).
- *Wood PLA research:* samples in walnut, oak, cherry, teak; groups research on laptops and make a tradeoff table (e.g., oak strong/durable but warps; walnut dark/smooth but costly; cherry smooth but brittle; teak durable/moisture-resistant but costly/needs temperature control).
- *DIY materials:* sustainable molds (glycerol/gelatin, natural latex, alginate from seaweed); sustainable plywood (bamboo), SuperWood (delignified wood), bacterial cellulose (kombucha SCOBY).

**Day 2 materials:** laptops with free Onshape accounts; Bambu printer and Bambu Studio slicer.
- *Onshape:* built-in tutorials Navigating Onshape and Introduction to Sketching (lines, circles, arcs, rectangles, constraints, dimensions).
- *Additive design challenge:* design a piece that can't be made subtractively (e.g., ball in a cube, lattice) within 5x5x5 cm; class votes; top 3 printed.
- *Importing to printer:* export a student piece (flash drive or email) to instructor laptop with Bambu Studio; choose supports/infill; orientation affects supports; view slicing preview; send to printer (calibration, preheating, sample extrusion). Bambu manual: wiki.bambulab.com.
- *Large-format printers:* concrete/clay house printing (limit = machine size; robotic assembly as future), Relativity Space metal rockets; ask what other industries use it.
- *Material applications:* glass and chocolate; TPU (crocs); anything meltable/extrudable.
- *Materiom* (commons.materiom.org): open platform for regenerative biomaterials; groups pick 2-3 materials producible locally (e.g., eggshell + gelatin biocomposite, printable) and present.
