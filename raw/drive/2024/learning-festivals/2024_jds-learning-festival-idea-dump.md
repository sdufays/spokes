**Arduino** - Pros: good skill for kids to continue after workshop. Cons: abstract, slow, few eye-catching demos besides blinking an LED; could be expensive; may not excite kids about STEM.

**What's that sound? Physics of speakers and waves** - Pros: highly conceptual high-level touch of sound, chemistry, engineering, E&M with fast-paced demos and a cheap hands-on project. Cons: not enough time for all demos.
- Main project: build a paper speaker that plays music.
- Learning objective: how a speaker works; sound as longitudinal waves; electromagnets, permanent magnets, speaker construction, membrane vibration; ear as inverse of speaker.
- Demo ideas: speaker in a vacuum chamber (sound needs a medium; marshmallows expand); plasma speaker/musical Tesla coil; speaker in helium and sulfur hexafluoride (pitch shifts; don't let kids breathe it); Newton's cradle (or three Spokes people lined up pushing); pouring-sound demo; bluetooth speaker.
- Speaker materials: 2 paper plates, cardstock, magnet wire, strong magnet (maybe expensive), audio amplifier. To save cost, recover magnets and amp from each built speaker.
- Cost notes: sulfur hexafluoride ~$195 for 250 g (~38 L); helium expensive (hydrogen an alternative if not breathed); vacuum chamber can be built from big glass jar + plexiglass with a vacuum pump.
