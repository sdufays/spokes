Protocol: NHGRI "How to extract DNA from a strawberry" (genome.gov).

**Background to share:** DNA determines traits; if stretched out, DNA in one human cell is ~6 ft long; organized into chromosomes in the nucleus; humans have 23 pairs (diploid). Many plants are polyploid (more than 2 copies of the genome) - commercial strawberries have 8 copies of 7 chromosomes (56), bananas 3 copies, sweet potatoes 6. Polyploidy in plants gives larger seeds/fruit and more variation; in humans it is associated with cancer/stress. Polyploidy is why DNA is visible by eye.

**Experiment - ask students what each component does, then explain:**
- Smashing strawberries: gets individual cells.
- Dish soap (detergent): water-loving and water-hating ends lodge in the membrane and break it apart, opening cell and nucleus.
- Salt (NaCl -> Na+, Cl-): DNA is negatively charged and repels itself; Na+ neutralizes the charge so DNA clumps.
- Rubbing alcohol: DNA is insoluble in it so it precipitates as a visible clump.

**Conclusion:** same components (detergent, salt, alcohol) are used in labs to extract DNA from any cell; relevance from human genome sequencing to ancestry tests.
