**Feb 27 meeting**
- June 3 chosen: DC youth facility (high school) + jail (19-20 year olds). Start in the morning, lunch break, end like a regular school day.
- Give them a clear list of what we need; they approve/deny. Lots of rules on what can be brought in; dress code; ID required.
- Experiments: less lecture, more interactive. 2023 feedback: yogurt experiment too slow; pressure one good; mixing liquids good; circuits bad (student tried to steal something); chromatography good.
- Some youth can't leave rooms but want to join; they look out windows to the courtyard; no Zoom, but paper/pen could be used; make things visual.
- Send Mariam a list of last year's experiments for feedback.
- Setup in past: 3-4 simultaneous experiments, each going to 3 classrooms; 9-12 classes (1-6 students each); jail 10-15 people.
- Get materials/info to them early (April-ish). They'll create an email chain with jail and youth center, confirm June 3, and set a later call. Contact Stanford Spokes with correctional facility contact info.

**Mar 11 meeting**
- Adrian is principal of the juvenile detention center. Send materials list by early April (approve/deny). June 3 essentially confirmed.
- Past format: 40-minute classes; 8 housing units (ideally 1-2 people per unit); classes up to 13 (avg 11); lunch 11:10-11:55; facility available 8:00-2:30; 2 people at DC jail, rest at youth center; projectors available. 80 minutes too long, students lose interest around minute 40. Try not to isolate students this year. Each classroom has Promethean boards and four tables. "GET TO THE POINT fast."
- Get materials list and names (as on driver's license/valid ID) to Adrian Dhanaraj within two weeks.
